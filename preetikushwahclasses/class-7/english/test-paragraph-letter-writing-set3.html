Model Answers
Q1.
The three main parts of a paragraph are: (1) Topic sentence (introduces the main idea), (2) Supporting sentences (explain and develop the idea with details, examples, or reasons), and (3) Concluding sentence (sums up or gives a final thought).
Q2.
A formal letter is written to officials, authorities, or strangers using formal language and a specific format. An informal letter is written to friends, family, or close relatives using a friendly, casual tone. (Key difference: tone and recipient.)
Q3.
RSVP stands for the French phrase “Répondez s’il vous plaît”, meaning “Please respond” or “Please reply.” It is used in invitations and notices asking people to confirm attendance.
Q4.
The receiver’s address is written on the left-hand side, below the date and above the subject line in a formal letter.
Q5.
A supporting sentence provides details, examples, facts, or reasons that explain and develop the main idea introduced in the topic sentence.
Q6.
Two occasions: (1) To complain about a civic problem (e.g., broken roads), (2) To request the Principal for leave or permission. (Also accepted: applying for a job, writing to the editor, to a bank manager, etc.)
Q7.
Format of a Formal Letter:
1. Sender’s Address (top left)
2. Date
3. Receiver’s Address (Designation + Organisation + City)
4. Subject (one line, clearly stating purpose)
5. Salutation (Respected Sir/Madam,)
6. Body (Introduction → Main content → Conclusion/Request)
7. Closing (Yours faithfully/sincerely)
8. Name & Class/Designation
Q8.
(a) Topic sentence: “Planting trees is essential for our survival.”
(b) Concluding sentence: “Without trees, life on Earth would be impossible.”
Q9.
Four essential elements of a notice: (1) Name of the organisation/school, (2) Date, (3) Heading/Title (what the notice is about), (4) Body (what, when, where, who, how). (Also accepted: Name of the issuing authority.)
Q10.
Four errors: (1) “principle” → “Principal”, (2) “Delhi Public school” → “Delhi Public School” (capital S), (3) “new delhi” → “New Delhi”, (4) “complain” → “Complaint”.

Q17.
Model Answer:

Paragraph 1 — In Favour of Abolishing Homework:
Many people believe that homework should be abolished because it puts unnecessary pressure on students. After spending six to seven hours in school, children need time to play, pursue hobbies, and spend quality time with their families. Too much homework leads to stress, anxiety, and even sleep deprivation. Studies in some countries like Finland show that students perform excellently with little or no homework. Instead of bookish assignments, children could learn better through hands-on activities and creative projects done in the classroom itself.

Paragraph 2 — Against Abolishing Homework:
On the other hand, homework plays an important role in reinforcing what is taught in class. It helps students practise concepts, develop self-discipline, and learn time management. Without homework, many students would not revise their lessons at all, leading to poor retention of knowledge. Homework also helps parents understand what their children are learning in school. Teachers can identify weak areas through homework and provide extra support where needed. Complete abolition would leave a gap in the learning process.

My Opinion: I believe homework should not be abolished entirely, but it should be reduced and made more meaningful — quality matters more than quantity.
