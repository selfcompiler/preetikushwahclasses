Passage for Sections A & B
Rani Lakshmibai, the Queen of Jhansi, is one of the greatest heroines of Indian history. She was born on 19 November 1828 in Varanasi and was named Manikarnika. As a child, she was very brave and loved horse riding, sword fighting, and archery. She was also an excellent student who enjoyed reading books.

When she grew up, she married the Maharaja of Jhansi, Gangadhar Rao, and became known as Lakshmibai. After her husband’s death, the British tried to take over her kingdom using the Doctrine of Lapse. But Lakshmibai refused to give up Jhansi. She said, “I shall not surrender my Jhansi!”

In 1857, during the First War of Indian Independence, she fought bravely against the British army. She led her soldiers from the front, riding on horseback with her young son tied to her back. She died fighting on 18 June 1858, at the young age of 29. Even her enemies admired her courage. The British General Hugh Rose called her “the bravest and best of the Indian leaders.”

Model Answers
Q1.
Rani Lakshmibai’s childhood name was Manikarnika.
Q2.
She was born in Varanasi.
Q3.
As a child, she enjoyed horse riding and sword fighting. (Also accepted: archery, reading books.)
Q4.
The British used the Doctrine of Lapse to try to take over Jhansi.
Q5.
Rani Lakshmibai died at the age of 29.
Q6.
She said, “I shall not surrender my Jhansi!” This tells us that she was extremely brave, determined, and patriotic. She loved her kingdom and was ready to fight for it rather than give it up to the British.
Q7.
Two details showing her bravery: (1) She led her soldiers from the front during the battle, not from behind. (2) She rode on horseback with her young son tied to her back, showing incredible courage and dedication.
Q8.
(a) The skill of shooting arrows = “archery”
(b) Praised or respected = “admired”
Q9.
Title: “The Brave Queen of Jhansi” (or “The Fearless Rani Lakshmibai”). I chose this title because the passage is mainly about the bravery and courage of Rani Lakshmibai, who fought against the British to protect her kingdom.
Q10.
From the life of Rani Lakshmibai, we learn that we should never give up in the face of injustice. She teaches us the values of courage, patriotism, and determination. No matter how powerful the enemy, we should stand up for what is right.
Q11.
The three essential parts of a story are: (1) Beginning (introduces the characters, setting, and situation), (2) Middle (describes the main events, the problem, or the conflict), and (3) End (gives the conclusion, resolution, and moral of the story).
Q12.
Title: The Honest Woodcutter
Opening paragraph: Once upon a time, there lived a poor but honest woodcutter named Ramu near a forest. Every day, he would go to the forest to chop wood and sell it in the market to feed his family. One unlucky day, while he was cutting a tree near the river, his axe slipped from his hands and fell into the deep water. Ramu sat by the riverbank and began to cry, for he had no money to buy a new axe.
Q13.
(a) The Tortoise and the Hare: “Slow and steady wins the race.” (Overconfidence can lead to failure.)
(b) The Boy Who Cried Wolf: “Nobody believes a liar, even when he tells the truth.”
Q14.
Correct order: (b), (e), (d), (c), (a)
A farmer found a bag of gold coins on the road. → The farmer asked everyone in the village whose bag it was. → The king had lost the bag while travelling. → The farmer returned the bag to the king. → The king rewarded the honest farmer.
Q15.
Model Story: One day, a little girl named Meera found a magical lamp in her grandmother’s attic. When she rubbed the dusty lamp, a tiny genie appeared in a puff of golden smoke! “I will grant you one wish,” said the genie with a smile. Meera thought carefully. She could wish for toys, sweets, or a palace. But she remembered how her village had no library. “I wish for a library full of books for all the children in my village!” she said. The next morning, a beautiful library stood in the village square. Meera had made the best wish of all.
Moral: Using our wishes to help others brings the greatest happiness.
