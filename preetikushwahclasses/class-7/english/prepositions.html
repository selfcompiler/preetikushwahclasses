📚 Section 1: What is a Preposition?

Definition

A preposition is a word placed before a noun or pronoun to show its relationship with another word in the sentence.

The word preposition comes from Latin: pre (before) + position (to place).

Prepositions are like invisible bridges that connect words and show how they relate to each other in terms of time, place, direction, or manner.

🎨 See How Prepositions Change Meaning

The same sentence can have very different meanings just by changing the preposition:

🐱📦
ON the table
The cat is on the table.
📦🐱
UNDER the table
The cat is under the table.
🐱📦
BESIDE the table
The cat is beside the table.
🐱⬇️📦
BEHIND the table
The cat is behind the table.
🐱🔗📦
NEAR the table
The cat is near the table.
🐱➡️📦
BETWEEN the tables
The cat is between the tables.
💡 Quick Trick: A preposition is anything a squirrel can do with a tree — the squirrel can run up the tree, down the tree, around the tree, through the tree, into the tree, beside the tree, behind the tree!
📖 Structure: A preposition is always followed by a noun or pronoun (called the object of the preposition).
Example: She sat on the chair. (chair = object of the preposition)
📚 Section 2: Types of Prepositions

Prepositions are grouped by the kind of relationship they show. Let us explore the four main types.

⏰ A. Prepositions of Time

These tell us when something happens.

📍 AT — Specific Time

  • at 5 PM
  • at noon / midnight
  • at dawn / sunset
  • at the weekend (British)

📅 ON — Days & Dates

  • on Monday
  • on 15th August
  • on my birthday
  • on Christmas Day

📆 IN — Longer Periods

  • in June / in winter
  • in 2024 / in the 21st century
  • in the morning / evening
  • in two hours (future)
PrepositionUseExample
atSpecific clock timeSchool starts at 8 AM.
onDays and datesWe have a test on Friday.
inMonths, years, seasons, parts of dayMangoes are sweet in summer.
duringThroughout a periodIt rained during the match.
beforeEarlier thanFinish homework before dinner.
afterLater thanWe played after school.
sinceFrom a point in the pastI have lived here since 2018.
forDuration of timeShe danced for two hours.
byNot later than (deadline)Submit the project by Monday.
until / tillUp to a certain timeWait here until I return.
💡 Remember: AT = a point in time, ON = a surface (day/date), IN = enclosed in a period. Think of it as zooming in: IN 2024 → ON 15 August → AT 9 AM.
🏠 B. Prepositions of Place

These tell us where something or someone is.

📍 AT — Specific Point

  • at the bus stop
  • at the door
  • at home / school

📋 ON — Surface

  • on the table
  • on the wall
  • on the second floor

📦 IN — Enclosed Space

  • in the box
  • in the garden
  • in India / in Delhi
PrepositionMeaningExample
atA specific point/locationMeet me at the gate.
onOn a surfaceThe book is on the shelf.
inInside an enclosed spaceThe keys are in the drawer.
aboveHigher than (not touching)The fan is above the table.
belowLower thanThe valley lies below the hill.
betweenIn the middle of twoSit between Riya and Aman.
amongIn the middle of manyShe stood among the crowd.
behindAt the back ofThe garden is behind the house.
beside / next toAt the side ofShe sat beside her friend.
nearNot far fromThe park is near our school.
underDirectly below (may touch)The cat is sleeping under the bed.
overDirectly above / coveringShe draped a shawl over her shoulders.
📖 Between vs Among: Use between when talking about two things. Use among when talking about three or more things.
The secret is between you and me.  |  She distributed sweets among the children.
➡️ C. Prepositions of Direction / Movement

These tell us the direction in which something moves.

PrepositionDirectionExample
toTowards a destinationShe walked to school.
fromAway from a starting pointHe came from Jaipur.
intoMoving insideThe frog jumped into the pond.
out ofMoving outsideShe ran out of the room.
throughFrom one side to the otherThe train went through the tunnel.
acrossFrom one side to the other (surface)The children ran across the field.
alongFollowing a lineWe walked along the river.
towardsIn the direction ofThe dog ran towards its owner.
upTo a higher placeThe boy climbed up the tree.
downTo a lower placeWater flows down the hill.
💡 Into vs In: Use into when there is movement: "She walked into the room." Use in when there is no movement: "She is in the room."
🔧 D. Prepositions of Instrument / Agent

These show how something is done or who/what does it.

🔧 WITH — Instrument / Tool

  • Cut the paper with scissors.
  • She writes with a fountain pen.
  • He opened the door with a key.
  • Mix the flour with a spoon.

✍️ BY — Agent / Means

  • The poem was written by Tagore.
  • She travels by bus every day.
  • The window was broken by a ball.
  • He learned by practising daily.
📖 By vs With:
By = the person/agent/means that performs the action: "The book was written by J.K. Rowling."
With = the tool/instrument used: "She signed the letter with a blue pen."
🔗 Section 3: Preposition Pairs & Phrasal Prepositions

Some prepositions are made up of two or more words that work together as a single preposition. These are called phrasal prepositions (or compound prepositions).

Phrasal PrepositionMeaningExample
in front ofbefore / facingA car is parked in front of the house.
because ofdue to (reason)The match was cancelled because of rain.
in spite ofdespiteIn spite of the cold, he went swimming.
on behalf ofrepresentingI am speaking on behalf of my team.
instead ofin place ofUse honey instead of sugar.
according toas stated byAccording to the teacher, the test is easy.
in addition tobesides / as well asIn addition to English, she speaks Hindi.
on account ofbecause ofHe was absent on account of illness.
in favour ofsupportingMost students voted in favour of the trip.
along withtogether withRiya, along with her friends, came early.
with regard toconcerning / aboutI am writing with regard to your complaint.
in the middle ofat the centre ofThere is a fountain in the middle of the park.
💡 Tip: Phrasal prepositions behave exactly like single-word prepositions — they are always followed by a noun or pronoun.
⚖️ Section 4: Preposition vs Adverb

The same word can be a preposition or an adverb depending on how it is used. The key difference: a preposition is always followed by an object (noun/pronoun), while an adverb is not.

WordAs a Preposition (+ object)As an Adverb (no object)
on The book is on the table. Please carry on. (= continue)
in She is in the kitchen. Come in! (no object follows)
up He ran up the stairs. She looked up. (no object follows)
down Water flows down the hill. Please sit down. (no object follows)
behind The dog hid behind the sofa. Don’t fall behind. (= lag)
off The cat jumped off the roof. The alarm went off.
over The bird flew over the lake. The game is over.
through We walked through the forest. Read the book through. (= completely)
💡 Quick Test: Ask "before WHAT?" or "on WHAT?" — if there is an answer (an object), it is a preposition. If not, it is an adverb.
⚠️ Section 5: Common Mistakes with Prepositions

Many students add unnecessary prepositions or use the wrong one. Here are the top mistakes to avoid.

❌ Wrong

  • She discussed about the topic.
  • He entered into the room.
  • We reached at the station.
  • She ordered for a pizza.
  • He requested for a holiday.
  • She married with Rahul.
  • I asked to him a question.
  • He is comprised of many talents.
  • She returned back home.
  • He accompanied with me.
  • She emphasized on the point.
  • He resembles to his father.

✅ Correct

  • She discussed the topic.
  • He entered the room.
  • We reached the station.
  • She ordered a pizza.
  • He requested a holiday.
  • She married Rahul.
  • I asked him a question.
  • He comprises many talents.
  • She returned home.
  • He accompanied me.
  • She emphasized the point.
  • He resembles his father.

❌ Incorrect✅ CorrectRule

💡 Section 6: Quick Tips & Fun Facts

🌟 Tip 1: Preposition at End of Sentence

It is perfectly fine to end a sentence with a preposition in modern English!

"Who are you looking for?" is correct and natural.

🌟 Tip 2: No Preposition Needed!

Some verbs never take a preposition: discuss, enter, reach, order, request, resemble, accompany.

🌟 Tip 3: Since vs For

Since = a point in time (since Monday, since 2020).
For = a duration of time (for two hours, for three years).

🌟 Tip 4: On Time vs In Time

On time = punctual, at the scheduled time.
In time = not late, with time to spare.

🌈 Fun Fact 1: There are about 150 prepositions in English, but only about 70 are commonly used. The top 10 most common are: of, in, to, for, with, on, at, from, by, about.
🌈 Fun Fact 2: The word "preposition" literally means "placed before" (from Latin prae = before + ponere = to place). But in English, prepositions don’t always come before their objects — sometimes they appear at the end of a sentence!
🌈 Fun Fact 3: Winston Churchill reportedly once joked: "Ending a sentence with a preposition is something up with which I will not put!" — making fun of the old (and wrong) rule against ending sentences with prepositions.
💡 Memory Trick — AT / ON / IN (Time):
Think of time as a target: AT = the bullseye (exact time), ON = the middle ring (a day), IN = the outer ring (a month/year/season).
Smallest → Biggest: AT 6 PM → ON Friday → IN December.
💡 Memory Trick — AT / ON / IN (Place):
AT = a point (at the corner), ON = a surface (on the wall), IN = an enclosed area (in the box).
Think: AT a dot, ON a line/surface, IN a container.

📝 Exercise A: Fill in the Correct Preposition

Complete each sentence with the most suitable preposition.

  1. The train arrives ________ 6:30 PM.
  2. My birthday falls ________ 14th November.
  3. We go to the hill station ________ summer.
  4. The cat is hiding ________ the sofa.
  5. She has been waiting ________ two hours.
  6. The children ran ________ the road carefully.
  7. He cut the apple ________ a knife.
  8. The book was written ________ R.K. Narayan.
  9. There is a bridge ________ the river.
  10. The ball rolled ________ the table.
  11. We have lived here ________ 2019.
  12. She walked ________ the park to reach the other side.
Show Answers

Exercise A: Fill in the Correct Preposition

  1. The train arrives at 6:30 PM. (specific time → at)
  2. My birthday falls on 14th November. (date → on)
  3. We go to the hill station in summer. (season → in)
  4. The cat is hiding behind the sofa. (at the back of → behind)
  5. She has been waiting for two hours. (duration → for)
  6. The children ran across the road carefully. (from one side to the other → across)
  7. He cut the apple with a knife. (instrument → with)
  8. The book was written by R.K. Narayan. (agent → by)
  9. There is a bridge over the river. (above, spanning → over)
  10. The ball rolled under the table. (below → under)
  11. We have lived here since 2019. (point in time → since)
  12. She walked through the park to reach the other side. (from one end to the other → through)

Exercise B: Preposition of Time, Place, or Direction?

  1. She arrived at noon. → Time (noon = a point in time)

  7. She jumped into the pool. → Direction (movement into)

📝 Exercise C: Correct the Errors

Each sentence has a preposition error. Rewrite the sentence correctly.

  1. She discussed about the problem with her teacher.
  2. He entered into the classroom quietly.
  3. We reached at the airport on time.
  4. My sister is good in painting.
  5. The children are afraid from the dark.
  6. He congratulated her for her success.
  7. She ordered for a glass of juice.
  8. I have been living here since three years.
  9. He is angry on his brother.
  10. She resembles to her mother.
Show Answers

Exercise C: Correct the Errors

  1. She discussed the problem with her teacher. (no "about" after discuss)
  2. He entered the classroom quietly. (no "into" after enter)
  3. We reached the airport on time. (no "at" after reach)
  4. My sister is good at painting. (good at, not good in)
  5. The children are afraid of the dark. (afraid of, not afraid from)
  6. He congratulated her on her success. (congratulate on, not for)
  7. She ordered a glass of juice. (no "for" after order)
  8. I have been living here for three years. (duration → for, not since)
  9. He is angry with his brother. (angry with a person, not on)
  10. She resembles her mother. (no "to" after resemble)
