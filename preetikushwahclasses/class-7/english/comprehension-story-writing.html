📖 Section 1: Reading Comprehension — What Is It?

Reading comprehension means reading a passage carefully and answering questions based on it. It tests how well you understand, interpret, and think about what you read.

🔍 Types of Questions You Will Face

📄 Factual Questions

Answers found directly in the passage. Look for who, what, when, where.

Example: "What did the fox see on the tree?"

🧠 Inferential Questions

Answers are not directly stated. You must think and conclude from clues in the passage.

Example: "Why did the crow feel foolish?"

📚 Vocabulary Questions

You are asked the meaning of a word as used in the passage.

Example: "What does 'cunning' mean here?"

💬 Opinion-Based Questions

You give your own view, supported by the passage.

Example: "Do you think the fox was right? Why?"

🎯 6 Golden Tips for Answering Comprehension
1

Read Twice

Read the passage at least twice — once for overall meaning, once for details.

2

Underline Key Points

Mark important names, dates, actions, and feelings.

3

Read Questions Carefully

Understand exactly what each question is asking before answering.

4

Find Answers in Passage

Go back to the passage and locate the relevant sentence or paragraph.

5

Write Complete Sentences

Never answer in one word. Frame your answer as a full sentence.

6

Use Your Own Words

Don't copy from the passage. Rephrase in your own language.

💡 Pro Tip: If you don't understand a word, read the sentences around it — the context often gives you the meaning!
🦊 Section 2: Comprehension Practice — Passage 1
The Clever Fox and the Crow

A crow once found a large piece of cheese and flew up to a tall tree to enjoy it. She sat on a branch, holding the cheese proudly in her beak. A cunning fox, who was passing by, saw the cheese and wanted it for himself. He looked up at the crow and said in his sweetest voice, "Good morning, beautiful bird! Your feathers shine like silk in the sunlight. I have heard that your voice is the most melodious in the entire forest. Would you please sing just one song for me?"

The crow was flattered by the fox's praise. She had never received such kind words before. Eager to show off her voice, she opened her beak wide to sing — and the cheese fell straight down! The fox quickly grabbed the cheese, gobbled it up, and laughed. "Thank you, dear crow! Remember this — never trust someone who flatters you without reason." The crow sat in silence, feeling very foolish indeed.

📝 Questions
  1. What did the crow find, and where did she go with it?
  2. How did the fox try to trick the crow?
  3. What does the word "melodious" mean in the passage?
  4. Why did the cheese fall from the crow's beak?
  5. What lesson does this story teach us?
  1. The crow found a large piece of cheese and flew up to a tall tree to enjoy it.
  2. The fox praised the crow's beauty and voice, asking her to sing, so that she would open her beak and drop the cheese.
  3. "Melodious" means sweet-sounding and pleasant to hear.
  4. When the crow opened her beak to sing, the cheese fell out because she could no longer hold it.
  5. The story teaches us that we should never trust those who flatter us without reason. We should think carefully before acting on someone's sweet words.
🌎 Section 3: Comprehension Practice — Passage 2
Save Our Environment

The environment is everything around us — the air we breathe, the water we drink, and the land we live on. In recent years, pollution has become a serious problem. Factories release harmful smoke into the air, plastic waste fills our rivers and oceans, and forests are being cut down at an alarming rate.

Every living creature depends on the environment for survival. When we pollute the air, it causes breathing problems. When we throw plastic carelessly, animals eat it and fall sick. Deforestation leads to soil erosion and floods. We must take action now — plant more trees, reduce the use of plastic, save water, and use bicycles or public transport whenever possible. Small steps by each person can make a huge difference. The Earth is our only home, and it is our duty to protect it.

📝 Questions
  1. What are the three parts of the environment mentioned in the passage?
  2. Name two problems caused by pollution.
  3. What does the word "alarming" mean in this context?
  4. Why is deforestation harmful? Give two reasons from the passage.
  5. Suggest two actions you can take in your daily life to protect the environment.
  1. The three parts of the environment mentioned are the air, the water, and the land.
  2. Pollution causes breathing problems (from air pollution) and makes animals sick when they eat plastic waste (from water/land pollution).
  3. "Alarming" means causing worry or fear — it indicates that forests are being cut down at a dangerously fast rate.
  4. Deforestation is harmful because it leads to soil erosion and causes floods.
  5. Two actions we can take are: (a) reduce the use of plastic bags and bottles by carrying reusable ones, and (b) plant more trees in our neighbourhood and school grounds. (Any two reasonable answers from the passage are acceptable.)
🎪 Section 4: Comprehension Practice — Passage 3
A Day at the Fair

Last Sunday, my family and I went to the annual village fair. The moment we entered, we were greeted by the sweet smell of freshly made jalebis and the cheerful sound of drums. Colourful tents lined both sides of the road. There were stalls selling bangles, toys, clothes, and delicious food.

My younger brother ran straight to the giant wheel and insisted on a ride. I preferred the magic show, where a magician made a rabbit appear from an empty hat! Mother bought beautiful clay pots from a potter's stall, and Father treated us to steaming hot samosas and cold lassi. As the sun set, the fair was lit up with hundreds of tiny bulbs, making it look like a wonderland. We returned home tired but happy, carrying bags full of sweets and memories that would last forever.

📝 Questions
  1. When did the narrator visit the fair, and with whom?
  2. What attracted the narrator's younger brother at the fair?
  3. Describe the magic show in your own words.
  4. Find a word in the passage that means "yearly." Write the sentence in which it appears.
  5. How did the fair look in the evening? Why do you think the narrator called it a "wonderland"?
  1. The narrator visited the fair last Sunday along with his/her family.
  2. The narrator's younger brother was attracted to the giant wheel and wanted to ride on it.
  3. In the magic show, a magician performed a wonderful trick in which he made a rabbit appear out of an empty hat, amazing the audience.
  4. The word is "annual." The sentence: "Last Sunday, my family and I went to the annual village fair."
  5. In the evening, the fair was lit up with hundreds of tiny bulbs, making it look magical and beautiful. The narrator called it a "wonderland" because the glowing lights transformed the ordinary fairground into a magical, dream-like place.
✍️ Section 5: Story Writing — The Art of Creating Stories

A story is a piece of creative writing that describes a series of events involving characters. A good story captures the reader's attention and takes them on an interesting journey.

🎲 Five Elements of a Good Story

🧑 Characters

The people (or animals) in the story. Every story needs at least one main character.

🏠 Setting

Where and when the story takes place — a village, city, forest, the future, etc.

📜 Plot

The sequence of events — what happens in the story from beginning to end.

⚡ Conflict

The problem or challenge the character faces. This makes the story exciting.

🌟 Resolution

How the problem is solved. The ending should feel satisfying and complete.

📌 Story Structure Diagram

Every great story follows this flow:

🌱

Beginning

Introduce characters, setting

➔
🚶

Middle

Events unfold, problem arises

➔
🔥

Climax

Most exciting point of the story

➔
🌟

End

Problem solved, lesson learned

💭 Tips for Writing a Great Story
  • Use dialogues — make characters talk to each other. This makes the story feel alive.
  • Use descriptive words — instead of "She was sad," write "Tears rolled down her cheeks."
  • Show feelings — describe what characters feel: joy, fear, surprise, anger.
  • Keep it simple — don't make the plot too complicated. A clear, simple story is always better.
  • Give a title — a catchy title makes readers curious.
  • End with a moral — if possible, include a lesson at the end.

📝 Section 6: Story Writing from Outlines
📚 Story 1: The Magic Lamp

💡 Given Outline / Hints

A poor boy named Raju ... finds an old lamp in a forest ... rubs it ... a genie appears ... grants three wishes ... Raju wishes wisely ... becomes happy ... moral.

📖 The Magic Lamp

Once upon a time, there was a poor boy named Raju who lived with his old grandmother in a small hut near the forest. They often went to bed hungry because they had very little money.

One day, while collecting firewood in the forest, Raju stumbled upon a rusty old lamp half-buried in the ground. Out of curiosity, he rubbed the dust off its surface. To his amazement, a great puff of colourful smoke burst out, and a mighty genie appeared before him!

"I am the Genie of the Lamp," the magical being said with a booming voice. "I shall grant you three wishes. Choose wisely, young one."

Raju thought carefully. For his first wish, he asked for enough food so that he and his grandmother would never go hungry again. For his second wish, he requested a small, sturdy house with a warm roof. And for his third wish — instead of asking for gold or riches — he wished for a chance to go to school and get an education.

The genie smiled. "You are the wisest child I have ever met," he said, and granted all three wishes instantly.

From that day onwards, Raju and his grandmother lived happily. Raju studied hard, became a teacher, and helped many children in his village get an education too.

Moral: True wealth lies not in gold, but in knowledge and kindness.

📝 Story 2: Complete the Story

📌 Given Beginning

"It was the last day of the school year. As Meera was cleaning her desk, she found a mysterious folded note that she had never seen before. She opened it slowly and read..."

📖 The Mysterious Note

It was the last day of the school year. As Meera was cleaning her desk, she found a mysterious folded note that she had never seen before. She opened it slowly and read: "Meet me at the old banyan tree in the school garden at 4 o'clock. I have something important to tell you. — A Friend."

Meera was puzzled. Who could have written this note? She looked around, but everyone was busy packing their bags. Her heart beat fast with curiosity and a little bit of fear.

At four o'clock, she walked nervously to the old banyan tree. There, sitting on the bench, was Ananya — the quiet new girl who had joined their class just two months ago. Ananya looked up and smiled shyly.

"I'm sorry for the mystery," Ananya said softly. "I just wanted to say thank you. On my first day, when no one talked to me, you smiled and shared your lunch with me. That small act of kindness meant the world to me. I was too shy to say it in front of everyone, so I wrote this note."

Meera felt a warm glow in her heart. She hugged Ananya and said, "You don't need a note to tell me that — we're friends!" They walked home together that evening, laughing and making plans for the summer holidays.

Moral: A small act of kindness can make someone's whole world brighter.

🎨 Story 3: Picture-Based Story Writing

📸 Imagine This Scene

A rainy evening ... a little kitten sitting alone under a bench in a park ... a kind girl with an umbrella sees the kitten ... she takes it home ... the kitten becomes her best friend.

📖 The Rainy Evening Rescue

One rainy evening, as dark clouds covered the sky and raindrops pattered against the windows, a little girl named Priya was walking home from her tuition class. She held her bright yellow umbrella tightly as the wind tried to snatch it away.

As she passed through the park, she heard a soft, tiny sound — "Mew! Mew!" She stopped and looked around. Under the old wooden bench, shivering and soaking wet, was a tiny grey kitten. Its fur was drenched, and its big round eyes looked up at Priya as if begging for help.

"Oh, you poor little thing!" Priya whispered. She knelt down, gently picked up the kitten, and wrapped it inside her warm jacket. She shared her umbrella with the trembling creature and hurried home.

At home, her mother helped her dry the kitten with a soft towel and gave it a bowl of warm milk. The kitten purred happily and snuggled close to Priya. They named her "Barish" — the Hindi word for rain — because that was when they found her.

From that day on, Barish became Priya's best friend and the most loved member of the family.

Moral: Compassion towards all living beings brings joy and love into our lives.

🎨 Section 7: Descriptive Writing

Descriptive writing paints a picture with words. When you describe a person, place, or event, your reader should be able to see, hear, smell, and feel what you are describing.

🖌️ How to Write a Good Description

🎨 Use Adjectives

Add describing words: "a tall, old tree" is better than "a tree."

💎 Use Similes

Compare things: "Her smile was as bright as sunshine."

👂 Sensory Details

Describe what you see, hear, smell, taste, and touch.

💡 Be Specific

Instead of "a nice day," write "a warm, sunny morning with a gentle breeze."

📝 Describing a Person
What to include:
  • Appearance: height, build, hair, eyes, clothing
  • Personality: kind, brave, cheerful, hardworking
  • Habits: always smiling, loves reading, wakes up early
  • Why they are special to you
🏛️ Describing a Place
What to include:
  • Location: where it is, how to reach it
  • Sights: buildings, trees, colours, people
  • Sounds & Smells: birds chirping, food aroma, traffic noise
  • How it makes you feel
📖 Model Descriptive Paragraph

🌱 My Grandmother's Garden

My grandmother's garden is a little paradise tucked behind her old brick house. The moment you step through the wooden gate, you are greeted by the sweet fragrance of jasmine and marigold. Tall sunflowers stand along the fence like cheerful soldiers, their golden faces following the sun. A narrow stone path winds through rows of tomato plants, chili bushes, and fragrant mint. In one corner, a large mango tree spreads its leafy arms, offering cool shade on hot afternoons. Butterflies dance from flower to flower, and sparrows chirp happily in the branches above. My grandmother spends every morning here, watering her plants and humming old songs. For me, this garden is not just a place — it is where some of my happiest memories live.

💡 Writer's Secret: The best descriptions use all five senses — sight, sound, smell, taste, and touch. Try to include at least three senses in every descriptive paragraph you write!
💡 Section 8: Quick Tips, Dos and Don'ts

✅ Do's

  • Read the passage/question carefully before writing
  • Plan your story before you start writing
  • Write a catchy title and an interesting beginning
  • Use paragraphs to organize your writing
  • Include dialogues to make stories lively
  • Use adjectives and sensory details in descriptions
  • End with a clear conclusion or moral
  • Proofread your work for spelling and grammar mistakes
  • Write neatly and maintain proper margins
  • Stay within the word limit if one is given

❌ Don'ts

  • Don't copy sentences directly from the passage
  • Don't write one-word answers in comprehension
  • Don't make your story too long or complicated
  • Don't change tense in the middle of a story
  • Don't use slang or text language (like "u" for "you")
  • Don't start every sentence with "I" or "Then"
  • Don't leave your story without an ending
  • Don't ignore punctuation marks
  • Don't rush — take your time to think
  • Don't panic if you find a question difficult
📚 Remember: Good writing is not about using big, fancy words. It is about expressing your ideas clearly and creatively. Even simple words, when arranged well, can tell a powerful story.

📄 Exercise A: Comprehension Passage

The Value of Books

Books are our best friends. They never leave us, never complain, and are always ready to teach us something new. A good book can transport us to a different world — we can travel to ancient Egypt, fly to outer space, or dive deep into the ocean, all from the comfort of our chair.

Reading improves our vocabulary, sharpens our thinking, and makes us better writers. It also develops our imagination and empathy — when we read about characters from different backgrounds, we learn to understand and respect people who are different from us. Great leaders, scientists, and thinkers throughout history have been avid readers. As the famous saying goes, "A room without books is like a body without a soul." Make it a habit to read at least one book every month, and you will be amazed at how much you grow.

Answer these questions:

  1. Why does the author call books "our best friends"?
  2. Name three places books can take us to, according to the passage.
  3. How does reading develop empathy?
  4. What does the saying "A room without books is like a body without a soul" mean?
  5. Find a word in the passage that means "eager and enthusiastic." Write the sentence in which it appears.
  1. Why does the author call books "our best friends"?
    The author calls books our best friends because they never leave us, never complain, and are always ready to teach us something new. Unlike people, books are always available when we need them.
  2. Name three places books can take us to.
    According to the passage, books can take us to ancient Egypt, outer space, and deep into the ocean.
  3. How does reading develop empathy?
    Reading develops empathy because when we read about characters from different backgrounds, we learn to understand and respect people who are different from us.
  4. What does the saying mean?
    The saying "A room without books is like a body without a soul" means that just as a body is lifeless without a soul, a room without books is empty and dull. Books give life and meaning to our surroundings and our minds.
  5. Find a word meaning "eager and enthusiastic."
    The word is avid. The sentence: "Great leaders, scientists, and thinkers throughout history have been avid readers."

✍️ Exercise B: Complete the Story

📌 Given Beginning

"During the summer holidays, Arjun and his cousin went to explore the old abandoned house at the end of the lane. As they pushed open the creaky door, they heard a strange noise coming from inside..."

Continue this story. Write the middle and end. Include at least one dialogue. Give it a title and a moral.

📖 The Secret of the Old House

During the summer holidays, Arjun and his cousin Kabir went to explore the old abandoned house at the end of the lane. As they pushed open the creaky door, they heard a strange noise coming from inside — a soft, scratching sound followed by a tiny whimper.

"Did you hear that?" whispered Kabir, grabbing Arjun's arm. Arjun nodded, his heart pounding, but his curiosity was stronger than his fear. They tiptoed down the dusty corridor, following the sound.

In the corner of the last room, behind a pile of old newspapers, they found a mother dog with three tiny puppies! The mother dog looked thin and weak. She had taken shelter in the old house to protect her babies.

"She's hungry," said Arjun sadly. "Let's get her some food and water."

They rushed home, got milk and biscuits, and carefully fed the little family. Over the next few days, they visited every day, bringing food and cleaning the space. Soon, their parents found out and helped them take the dogs to an animal shelter where they were given proper care.

"I thought the house was haunted," laughed Kabir. "But instead, we found something much better — a chance to help."

Moral: Courage and kindness can turn a scary situation into a beautiful one.

📚 Exercise C: Write a Story from Hints

💡 Outline

A farmer and his lazy sons ... the farmer falls ill ... tells sons about hidden treasure in the field ... sons dig the entire field ... find no treasure ... but the field is now ready for planting ... they plant seeds ... get a rich harvest ... understand the real treasure.

Write a complete story based on this outline. Give it a suitable title and include a moral at the end.

📖 The Hidden Treasure

Once upon a time, an old farmer lived in a village with his three sons — Mohan, Sohan, and Rohan. The farmer was hardworking, but his sons were lazy. They spent their days sleeping under trees and chatting with friends, never helping their father in the fields.

One day, the farmer fell seriously ill. He called his three sons to his bedside and said in a weak voice, "My dear sons, I have buried a great treasure in our field. After I am gone, dig the field and you will find it."

A few weeks later, the old farmer passed away. The three sons, excited about the treasure, immediately grabbed shovels and began digging the entire field. They dug every inch of soil — from one corner to the other — but found no treasure at all.

Disappointed, they sat down under a tree. A wise neighbour, who had been watching them, said, "The field is now perfectly dug. Why don't you plant some seeds?"

Having no other choice, the brothers planted wheat in the freshly dug field. Months later, the field gave a rich golden harvest — the best in the entire village! The brothers sold the wheat and earned more money than they had ever seen.

Suddenly, they understood their father's words. The real treasure was not gold or jewels — it was the fertile land and the fruits of hard work.

Moral: Hard work is the greatest treasure of all.
