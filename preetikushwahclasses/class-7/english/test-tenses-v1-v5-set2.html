Answer Key & Detailed Solutions
Section A — MCQ
Q1. [1 Mark]
(c) Future Perfect
The sentence “The train will have left by the time we reach the station” uses the formula: Subject + will + have + V3 (past participle). Here “will have left” shows an action that will be completed before another future action. The signal word “by the time” is a strong indicator of Future Perfect Tense.
Q2. [1 Mark]
(b) cooks
The sentence talks about a daily routine/habit (“every evening”), which requires Simple Present Tense. The subject “My mother” is third person singular, so the verb takes the V5 form: cooks. Signal words like “every day”, “every evening”, “always”, “usually” indicate Simple Present.
Q3. [1 Mark]
(c) She had been writing letters for two hours before lunch.
Past Perfect Continuous Tense follows the formula: Subject + had + been + V4 (-ing form). Here “had been writing” shows an action that was continuing for a duration (“for two hours”) before another past action (“before lunch”). Option (a) is Past Perfect (had + V3), option (b) is Past Continuous (was + V4), and option (d) is Present Perfect Continuous (has been + V4).
Q4. [1 Mark]
(c) Present Continuous
The sentence uses the formula: Subject + is/am/are + V4 (-ing form). “Are playing” indicates an action happening at the moment of speaking. The signal word “right now” confirms that this is Present Continuous Tense.
Q5. [1 Mark]
(b) I completed my homework yesterday.
The word “yesterday” is a signal word for Simple Past Tense. Simple Past uses V2 (completed). Option (a) is wrong because Present Perfect (“have completed”) cannot be used with a definite past time like “yesterday”. Option (c) uses Past Perfect which requires a reference to two past events. Option (d) is Present Continuous which cannot be used with “yesterday”.
Section B — Fill in the Blanks
Q6. [1 Mark]
had started
Tense: Past Perfect (Subject + had + V3).
Explanation: There are two past actions here — (i) reaching the cinema hall and (ii) the movie starting. The movie started before they reached, so the earlier past action takes Past Perfect Tense. “By the time” + Simple Past in one clause signals Past Perfect in the other clause. “Already” further confirms completion before another action.
Q7. [1 Mark]
is dancing
Tense: Present Continuous (Subject + is/am/are + V4).
Explanation: The word “Look!” indicates an action happening right now, at this very moment. Present Continuous is used for actions in progress at the time of speaking. V4 (dancing) is the -ing form of “dance”.
Q8. [1 Mark]
has been practising
Tense: Present Perfect Continuous (Subject + has/have + been + V4).
Explanation: The phrase “for three years now” tells us that the action started in the past and is still continuing in the present. This is exactly what Present Perfect Continuous expresses. “For” + time duration with a present result is a classic signal for this tense.
Q9. [1 Mark]
will have been learning
Tense: Future Perfect Continuous (Subject + will + have + been + V4).
Explanation: “By next March” points to a future point in time, and “for five years” shows duration. The sentence describes an action that will have been continuing up to a point in the future. This combination requires Future Perfect Continuous Tense.
Q10. [1 Mark]
was telling
Tense: Past Continuous (Subject + was/were + V4).
Explanation: Two things happened in the past — one was ongoing (telling a story) and the other interrupted it suddenly (lights went off). The ongoing past action uses Past Continuous (was telling), while the sudden interruption uses Simple Past (went off). “While” is a signal word for Past Continuous.
Section C — Convert the Tense
Q11. [1 Mark]
Original: She writes a letter to her friend. (Simple Present)
Past Perfect: She had written a letter to her friend.
Explanation: Past Perfect formula is Subject + had + V3. The verb “write” changes as: V1 (write) → V3 (written). “Writes” becomes “had written”. The pronoun and rest of the sentence remain unchanged.
Q12. [1 Mark]
Original: The boys are playing football in the park. (Present Continuous)
Future Continuous: The boys will be playing football in the park.
Explanation: Future Continuous formula is Subject + will/shall + be + V4 (-ing form). The verb form V4 (playing) stays the same, but the helping verb changes from “are” to “will be”.
Q13. [1 Mark]
Original: Aman watched a movie yesterday. (Simple Past)
Present Perfect: Aman has watched a movie.
Explanation: Present Perfect formula is Subject + has/have + V3. The verb changes from V2 (watched) to has + V3 (has watched). Note: “Yesterday” is removed because Present Perfect cannot be used with definite past time expressions. “Aman” is third person singular, so we use “has”.
Q14. [1 Mark]
Original: I will finish my project by tomorrow. (Simple Future)
Future Perfect: I will have finished my project by tomorrow.
Explanation: Future Perfect formula is Subject + will/shall + have + V3. The verb changes from “will finish” (will + V1) to “will have finished” (will + have + V3). “By tomorrow” works well with Future Perfect as it indicates completion before a future point.
Q15. [1 Mark]
Original: They have been waiting at the bus stop for an hour. (Present Perfect Continuous)
Past Perfect Continuous: They had been waiting at the bus stop for an hour.
Explanation: Past Perfect Continuous formula is Subject + had + been + V4. The helping verb changes from “have been” to “had been”. The V4 form (waiting) remains the same. This shifts the ongoing action from the present timeframe to the past timeframe.
Section D — True or False with Correction
Q16. [1 Mark]
False.
Present Continuous Tense does not use V2. It uses V4 (the -ing form / present participle).
Correct statement: “Present Continuous Tense uses is/am/are + V4 (-ing form).”
Example: She is reading (V4) a book. V2 (read/wrote etc.) is used in Simple Past Tense, not Present Continuous.
Q17. [1 Mark]
True.
The signal words “since” (used for a point in time, e.g., since morning, since 2020) and “for” (used for a duration, e.g., for two hours, for five years) are indeed commonly associated with Perfect Continuous Tenses — Present Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect Continuous, and Future Perfect Continuous. They can also appear with Perfect Tenses (e.g., “I have known him for years”).
Q18. [1 Mark]
False.
Simple Past Tense does not use “has/have” as helping verbs. It uses the V2 form (past form) of the verb directly, without any helping verb in affirmative sentences.
Correct statement: “In Simple Past Tense, the main verb is used in its V2 (past) form without any helping verb.”
Example: She went (V2) to school. “Has/have” are helping verbs used in Present Perfect Tense (has/have + V3).
Q19. [1 Mark]
True.
Future Perfect Tense indeed follows the formula: Subject + will/shall + have + V3 (past participle). It shows an action that will be completed before a certain point in the future.
Example: She will have finished (V3) the exam by 3 PM.
Q20. [1 Mark]
False.
The sentence “She had been singing for two hours” is in Past Perfect Continuous Tense, not Present Perfect Continuous.
Correct statement: “The sentence uses Past Perfect Continuous Tense (Subject + had + been + V4).”
Explanation: The helping verb “had been” indicates Past Perfect Continuous. If it were Present Perfect Continuous, the helping verb would be “has been” or “have been” (e.g., “She has been singing for two hours”).
Section E — Passage-Based Answers
Q21. [1 Mark]
Simple Present Tense.
“Wakes up” is in Simple Present because it describes Ananya’s daily routine/habit (“every morning”). Simple Present is used for habitual or regularly repeated actions. The verb “wakes” is V5 (third person singular form of “wake”).
Q22. [1 Mark]
Past Continuous Tense.
“Was preparing” follows the formula: Subject + was/were + V4 (-ing form). Past Continuous is used here instead of Simple Past because the action of preparing was ongoing when another action (her friend calling) interrupted it. Past Continuous shows the background action that was in progress, while Simple Past shows the sudden action that happened during it.
Q23. [1 Mark]
Future Perfect Tense.
“Will have completed” follows the formula: Subject + will + have + V3 (past participle). This tense indicates that the action of completing all subjects will be finished before a certain point in the future (before her father comes home). “By the time” is a signal phrase for Future Perfect, showing completion before a future reference point.
Q24. [1 Mark]
Present Perfect Continuous Tense.
“Has been studying” follows the formula: Subject + has/have + been + V4. The signal word is “since” (since last Monday). “Since” indicates that the action started at a specific point in the past and is still continuing in the present, which is exactly what Present Perfect Continuous expresses.
Q25. [1 Mark]
Past Perfect Tense.
“Had scored” follows the formula: Subject + had + V3 (past participle). Past Perfect is used here instead of Simple Past because there are two past actions: (i) scoring the highest marks (earlier event) and (ii) the teacher telling her about it (later event). Past Perfect is used for the earlier of two past actions to show that it happened before the other past action. The teacher “told” (Simple Past) about something that “had scored” (Past Perfect — happened even earlier).
