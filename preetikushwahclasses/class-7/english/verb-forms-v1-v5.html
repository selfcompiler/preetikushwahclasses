What Are the Five Verb Forms?

V1, V2, V3, V4 & V5 — every verb wears five different costumes

📖

Definition

Every English verb has five forms. These forms are used to build different tenses. Knowing all five forms of a verb is the key to writing correct sentences!

V1
Base Form / Infinitive
The simplest dictionary form of the verb.
play go write eat
V2
Past Simple
Used for actions completed in the past.
played went wrote ate
V3
Past Participle
Used with has/have/had for perfect tenses.
played gone written eaten
V4
Present Participle (-ing)
Used with is/am/are/was/were for continuous tenses.
playing going writing eating
V5
Third Person Singular (-s/-es)
Used with he/she/it in Simple Present Tense.
plays goes writes eats

🔐

How to Form V5 (the -s/-es Form)

Rules for adding -s or -es to create the third person singular form

📜 When Do We Use V5?

V5 is used only in the Simple Present Tense when the subject is he, she, it, or any singular noun.

V1 (I/You/We/They)
They play football every evening.
V5 (He/She/It)
He plays football every evening.

Rule 1: Add -s

Most verbs — just add -s to the base form.

play → plays eat → eats run → runs read → reads write → writes sing → sings

Rule 2: Add -es

If the verb ends in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z, or -o, add -es.

go → goes do → does watch → watches wash → washes fix → fixes pass → passes buzz → buzzes

Rule 3: Consonant + y → -ies

If the verb ends in a consonant + y, change y to i and add -es.

carry → carries study → studies fly → flies try → tries cry → cries

Rule 4: Vowel + y → just -s

If the verb ends in a vowel + y, simply add -s.

play → plays stay → stays enjoy → enjoys buy → buys say → says

Rule 5: Irregular V5 Forms

A few verbs have special V5 forms.

have → has be → is

🤔 Did You Know?

🌟 The verb "read" is a tricky one! It looks the same in V1, V2, and V3, but the pronunciation changes: V1 = "reed," V2 & V3 = "red."

🌟 The verb "be" is the most irregular verb in English. It has 8 forms: be, am, is, are, was, were, been, being!

🌟 Only about 200 verbs in English are irregular. The other thousands of verbs are all regular — just add -ed!

Which Tense Uses Which Form?

A quick reference to know exactly which V-form goes where

📋 Tense → Verb Form Quick Reference

This table shows you which verb form (V1–V5) is used in each tense.

Tense Structure Form Used Example (verb: write)
Simple Present (I/you/we/they) Subject + V1 V1 They write letters.
Simple Present (he/she/it) Subject + V5 V5 She writes letters.
Simple Past Subject + V2 V2 She wrote a letter.
Simple Future Subject + will + V1 V1 She will write a letter.
Present Continuous Subject + is/am/are + V4 V4 She is writing a letter.
Past Continuous Subject + was/were + V4 V4 She was writing a letter.
Future Continuous Subject + will be + V4 V4 She will be writing a letter.
Present Perfect Subject + has/have + V3 V3 She has written a letter.
Past Perfect Subject + had + V3 V3 She had written a letter.
Future Perfect Subject + will have + V3 V3 She will have written a letter.
Present Perfect Continuous Subject + has/have been + V4 V4 She has been writing since morning.
Past Perfect Continuous Subject + had been + V4 V4 She had been writing for an hour.

💡 Memory trick: Continuous = V4 (-ing), Perfect = V3 (past participle), Simple Past = V2, Simple Present with he/she/it = V5!

💡 Tips & Tricks for Remembering Verb Forms

🎯 V5 is easy: It is just V1 + s/es. The only exceptions are have → has and be → is.
📚 V4 is always the same: Just add -ing to V1. It works for regular AND irregular verbs!
🎵 Sing the irregular V1-V2-V3: Make a rhythm — "go-went-gone, eat-ate-eaten, write-wrote-written, sing-sang-sung!"
📋 Regular verbs are easy: For regular verbs, V2 = V3 (both end in -ed). So you only really need to learn V2/V3 of irregular verbs!
🛠️ Use flashcards: Write V1 on the front, V2-V3-V4-V5 on the back. Test yourself 5 verbs a day, and in two weeks you will know them all!
💡 Group by pattern: Learn irregular verbs in families — all-different (go-went-gone), same-V2V3 (buy-bought-bought), all-same (cut-cut-cut). Patterns make memorisation 3x faster!

⚖️ V2 (Past Simple) vs V3 (Past Participle) — What's the Difference?

Students often confuse V2 and V3, especially because for regular verbs, they are the same! But for irregular verbs, they can be very different.

Verb (V1)V2V3Same?V2 in sentenceV3 in sentence
playplayedplayedYesShe played yesterday.She has played well.
gowentgoneNoHe went home.He has gone home.
writewrotewrittenNoI wrote a letter.I have written a letter.
eatateeatenNoWe ate lunch.We have eaten lunch.
buyboughtboughtYesShe bought a book.She has bought a book.
singsangsungNoThey sang a song.They have sung a song.
cutcutcutYesHe cut the rope.He has cut the rope.
swimswamswumNoShe swam fast.She has swum fast.
KEY RULE
V2 is used ALONE (Simple Past) → "I wrote"
V3 is used WITH has/have/had (Perfect Tenses) → "I have written"

👥 V1 (Base Form) vs V5 (Third Person -s/-es) — When to Use Which?

In the Simple Present Tense, the verb form depends on the subject:

SubjectForm UsedExampleWhy?
IV1I play cricket.First person → always V1
YouV1You play cricket.Second person → always V1
We / TheyV1They play cricket.Plural → always V1
He / She / ItV5He plays cricket.Third person singular → V5
Ram (name)V5Ram plays cricket.One person = he/she → V5
The dogV5The dog runs fast.One animal = it → V5
The boysV1The boys run fast.Plural = they → V1
MEMORY TRICK
If the subject is one person/thing (not I or you) → add -s/-es (use V5)
Everything else → use V1 (base form)

✍️ Exercise D: Correct the Verb Form Errors

Each sentence has one wrong verb form. Rewrite the sentence with the correct form.

  1. She have written a beautiful poem.
  2. He goed to the market last evening.
  3. They has eaten all the mangoes.
  4. The boy is ran very fast.
  5. My sister studys hard for her exams.
  6. We have already spoke to the principal.
  7. The birds was singing on the tree.
  8. She cutted the cake with a knife.
  9. He have been playing since morning.

🔒 Show Answer
1. She has written a beautiful poem.
"She" is third person singular → use "has" (not "have") with V3.
2. He went to the market last evening.
"Go" is irregular. V2 = "went" (not "goed"). There is no -ed form for "go."
3. They have eaten all the mangoes.
"They" is plural → use "have" (not "has") with V3.
4. The boy is running very fast.
Present Continuous needs is + V4 (-ing form). "Ran" is V2, not V4.
5. My sister studies hard for her exams.
Consonant + y → change y to i and add -es. study → studies (not "studys").
6. We have already spoken to the principal.
"Have" requires V3 (past participle). speak → spoke (V2) → spoken (V3). "Spoke" is V2, not V3.
7. The birds were singing on the tree.
"Birds" is plural → use "were" (not "was") in Past Continuous.
8. She cut the cake with a knife.
"Cut" is an all-same verb (V1=V2=V3 = cut). There is no "cutted."
9. He has been playing since morning.
"He" is singular → use "has" (not "have") in Present Perfect Continuous.
10. The baby cried all night long.
Consonant + y → change y to i and add -ed. cry → cried (not "cryed").

Part 2: Odd One Out

6. Odd one out: buy
cut, put, shut, hit are all "all-same" verbs (V1=V2=V3). "Buy" is different: buy-bought-bought.
7. Odd one out: teach
sing, drink, swim, begin all follow the "all-different" pattern (sang-sung, drank-drunk, etc.). "Teach" follows V2=V3 pattern (taught-taught).
8. Odd one out: went
played, walked, jumped, cooked are all regular V2 forms (add -ed). "Went" is the irregular V2 of "go."
9. Odd one out: give
come, run, become, overcome all follow V1=V3 pattern. "Give" follows the all-different pattern (give-gave-given).
10. Odd one out: go
buy, catch, think, find all follow V2=V3 pattern (bought-bought, caught-caught, etc.). "Go" is all-different (go-went-gone).

Part 3: True or False?

11. False
V5 is used with he/she/it (third person singular) in Simple Present, NOT with "I." With "I," we use V1.
12. True
V4 (Present Participle) is always formed by adding "-ing" to the base form.
13. False
"Gone" is V3 (past participle) of "go." V2 of "go" is "went."
14. True
For regular verbs, both V2 and V3 end in -ed and are identical (e.g., played-played, walked-walked).
15. True
"Has" is the V5 (third person singular) form of "have." He has, she has, it has.
