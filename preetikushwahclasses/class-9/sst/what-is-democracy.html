🗳 What is Democracy?
Why Democracy?

Definition · Features · Examples · Arguments For & Against

VOTE
📚 Definition of Democracy
📝 NCERT Definition: "Democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people."

A more detailed definition: Democracy is a form of government where:

  • The rulers are elected by the people through free and fair elections
  • Every citizen has an equal right to vote (one person, one vote, one value)
  • The government functions within the limits of the constitution
  • The rights of citizens are protected
  • The minority is protected even as the majority rules
What Democracy is NOT
  • Not military rule — army takes over without election (e.g., Pakistan under Musharraf)
  • Not monarchy — hereditary ruler, people have no say (e.g., Saudi Arabia)
  • Not one-party rule — only one party allowed, no real choice (e.g., China)
  • Not rigged elections — elections held but results are fixed (e.g., Zimbabwe under Mugabe)
💡 Exam Tip: The NCERT definition — "Democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people" — is asked directly in 1-mark questions. Learn it word for word.
✅ Features of Democracy

According to NCERT, the following are the key features that a democratic government must have:

#FeatureWhat It Means
1Elected RepresentativesRulers must be chosen by the people through elections; not self-appointed
2Free and Fair ElectionsElections must be held regularly; every citizen must have a real choice; no rigging or fraud
3One Person, One Vote, One ValueEvery adult citizen gets exactly one vote, and each vote has the same value (universal adult franchise)
4Rule of LawGovernment rules within the limits of the law; no one is above the law, including rulers
5Majority Rule with Minority RightsThe majority takes decisions, but the rights and interests of minorities must be protected
6Protection of Basic RightsCitizens must have fundamental rights like freedom of speech, expression, association; these cannot be taken away
🏭 Universal Adult Franchise: Every adult citizen (18 years and above in India) has the right to vote, regardless of caste, religion, gender, or economic status. This is a key pillar of democracy.
💡 Exam Tip: "One person, one vote, one value" is frequently asked. It means: (1) every adult citizen gets one vote, (2) every vote has equal value — no one's vote counts more. This eliminates discrimination based on wealth or social status.
🌎 Examples of Democracy & Non-Democracy (NCERT)

NCERT uses specific country examples to explain democracy. Learn these — they are frequently asked.

❌ Non-Democratic Examples

🇿🇼 Zimbabwe (Robert Mugabe)

Mugabe won every election since independence (1980). But opposition candidates were harassed, arrested, and killed. Media was controlled. Elections were not free or fair. Technically elections were held, but there was no real choice. → NOT a democracy

🇲🇽 Mexico (PRI Rule)

From 1930 to 2000, the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) won every election. The party used state machinery, bribed voters, and rigged results. Other parties could contest but could never win. → NOT a real democracy

🇨🇱 Chile (Pinochet)

General Augusto Pinochet overthrew the elected President Salvador Allende in a military coup in 1973. Thousands were killed or imprisoned. This is a military dictatorship. → NOT a democracy

🇵🇰 Pakistan (Musharraf)

General Pervez Musharraf led a military coup in 1999, dismissed the elected Prime Minister, and suspended the constitution. The army ruled Pakistan. → NOT a democracy

🇨🇳 China

Only the Communist Party of China is allowed to rule. Citizens cannot choose a different government. Elections are held but only for approved Communist Party candidates. → NOT a democracy

🇸🇦 Saudi Arabia

A monarchy — the King has all power. There are no elections for the head of state. Women had no right to vote for a very long time. → NOT a democracy

✅ Democratic Examples / Movements

🇵🇱 Poland (Solidarity Movement)

Under communist rule, workers formed Solidarity — a trade union. Led by Lech Walesa, they fought for free elections. In 1989, Poland held its first free elections, ending communist rule. A successful transition to democracy.

🇩🇪 East Timor (Myanmar)

Myanmar (Burma): Aung San Suu Kyi — democracy activist, kept under house arrest for years by the military junta. Her party (NLD) won elections but the military refused to hand over power. Symbol of fight for democracy.

💡 Exam Tip: The NCERT examples are very frequently asked in MCQs. Remember: Zimbabwe = Mugabe + rigged elections; Chile = Pinochet + military coup 1973 + overthrew Allende; Pakistan = Musharraf + 1999 coup; Mexico = PRI + 70 years + not free elections; Poland = Solidarity + Lech Walesa.
❓ Why Democracy? — Arguments
Arguments IN FAVOUR of Democracy (NCERT)
#ArgumentExplanation
1Accountable GovernmentA democratic government is responsible to the people. If rulers do not perform well, citizens can vote them out. Rulers know they must answer to the public.
2Better Decision-MakingDecisions are taken after consultation and debate. More people involved means better, more informed decisions that are accepted by all.
3Resolves Conflicts PeacefullyIn diverse societies, democracy provides a method to settle disputes peacefully through dialogue, negotiation, and elections rather than violence.
4Dignity of CitizensDemocracy treats all citizens as equal. Every person's vote has equal value. This enhances the dignity and self-respect of citizens.
5Correcting Own MistakesIn a democracy, if the government makes mistakes, people can vote them out and bring a better government. This self-correction is not possible in dictatorships.
Arguments AGAINST Democracy (and NCERT's Responses)
Argument AgainstResponse / Counter
Leaders keep changing — instabilityThis instability is actually accountability; people can remove bad leaders
Slow decision-making (too much consultation)Slow but broadly accepted decisions are better implemented than quick dictatorial ones
Politicians are corrupt and self-interestedPeople have the power to elect better representatives next time
Based on political competition, not good valuesDisagreement and debate are a sign of healthy democracy, not weakness
📚 NCERT's conclusion: "Democracy is a better form of government when compared with dictatorship or any other alternative." The key reason is accountability — in a democracy, the government must answer to the people.
💡 Exam Tip (5-mark): For "Why is democracy better?", always use these 5 NCERT points: (1) Accountable, (2) Better decisions, (3) Resolves conflicts, (4) Dignity of citizens, (5) Corrects own mistakes. These are straight from the textbook.
🧠 Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

Click an option to check your answer. All NCERT examples and key concepts are covered.

  • Q1. According to NCERT, democracy is a form of government in which:
    • a) The richest person rules
    • b) The army controls the government
    • c) The rulers are elected by the people
    • d) A king rules by divine right
    ✅ The NCERT definition: "Democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people." This is the most basic and direct definition given in the textbook.
  • Q2. General Pinochet came to power in Chile by:
    • a) Winning a free and fair election
    • b) Overthrowing the elected president through a military coup
    • c) Being appointed by the parliament
    • d) Inheriting power from his father
    ✅ In 1973, General Augusto Pinochet overthrew the democratically elected President Salvador Allende through a military coup. He then became a military dictator, executing and imprisoning thousands of opponents.
  • Q3. "One person, one vote, one value" means:
    • a) Rich people get more votes than poor people
    • b) Educated people get extra votes
    • c) Every adult citizen gets one vote and every vote has equal value
    • d) Only one political party is allowed to stand for election
    ✅ One person, one vote, one value means every adult citizen gets exactly one vote, and that vote has the same value as any other citizen's vote — regardless of wealth, education, gender, or caste. This is universal adult franchise.
  • Q4. In Zimbabwe, elections were held but it was not considered a democracy because:
    • a) Only one candidate was allowed to contest
    • b) Opposition candidates were harassed and elections were rigged by Mugabe
    • c) Citizens were not allowed to vote
    • d) Elections were held only once in 20 years
    ✅ In Zimbabwe under Robert Mugabe, elections were held regularly, but the government harassed opposition parties, arrested candidates, and rigged results. The media was controlled. Elections were held but they were not free or fair, so Zimbabwe was not a real democracy.
  • Q5. Mexico was not considered a full democracy before 2000 because:
    • a) Elections were never held
    • b) The PRI party won every election for 70 years using unfair means
    • c) The army controlled the government
    • d) Only men were allowed to vote
    ✅ Mexico's PRI (Institutional Revolutionary Party) won every election from 1930 to 2000 — for 70 years. They used government resources, bribed voters, and manipulated results. Opposition parties could contest but had no real chance of winning. It was not a genuine democracy.
  • Q6. Which movement in Poland fought against communist dictatorship for democracy?
    • a) Congress Party
    • b) National League for Democracy
    • c) Solidarity
    • d) ANC (African National Congress)
    ✅ Solidarity was the trade union movement in Poland led by Lech Walesa that fought against communist rule. In 1989, Poland held free elections and ended communist rule — a peaceful and successful transition to democracy.
  • Q7. Which of the following is the BEST argument for democracy according to NCERT?
    • a) It always leads to economic development
    • b) It is a more accountable form of government
    • c) It makes the fastest decisions
    • d) It gives the most power to elected leaders
    ✅ According to NCERT, the best argument for democracy is that it is a more accountable form of government. Democratic governments are responsible to the people. If they fail, the people can vote them out. This accountability is the most important feature that makes democracy better than other forms.
  • Q8. Aung San Suu Kyi is known as a symbol of democracy in which country?
    • a) Poland
    • b) Chile
    • c) Myanmar (Burma)
    • d) Zimbabwe
    ✅ Aung San Suu Kyi was kept under house arrest for years by the military junta in Myanmar (Burma) for fighting for democracy. Her party (National League for Democracy/NLD) won the 1990 elections, but the military refused to hand over power.
  • Q9. Which of the following is NOT a feature of democracy?
    • a) Free and fair elections
    • b) One person, one vote, one value
    • c) Hereditary rulers passing power to their children
    • d) Protection of basic rights of citizens
    ✅ Hereditary rule (power passing from parent to child) is a feature of monarchy, NOT democracy. In democracy, rulers must be elected by the people. Options a, b, and d are all core features of democracy as defined by NCERT.
  • Q10. General Pervez Musharraf took power in Pakistan in which year?
    • a) 1989
    • b) 1994
    • c) 1999
    • d) 2001
    ✅ General Pervez Musharraf led a military coup in 1999, dismissed the elected Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif, and suspended Pakistan's constitution. He then held a referendum in 2002 to extend his own rule — but the referendum itself was widely criticised as unfair.

📄 2-Mark Questions & Answers

Write in 30–40 words. Cover 2 clear points.

  • Q1. Why was Zimbabwe under Mugabe not considered a democracy? 2 Marks
    Zimbabwe under Robert Mugabe was not a democracy because, although elections were held, they were not free or fair. (1) Opposition candidates were harassed, arrested, and even killed. (2) The media was controlled by the government, preventing independent reporting. Citizens had no real choice in elections, which is a fundamental requirement of democracy.
  • Q2. Distinguish between a democratic and a non-democratic government. 2 Marks
    Democratic Government: Rulers are elected by the people through free and fair elections; every adult has one vote; citizens have basic rights; government is accountable to the people.
    Non-Democratic Government: Rulers are not elected by the people (e.g., military coup, monarchy); there are no free and fair elections; citizens' rights are often suppressed; no accountability to people.
  • Q3. What are the two conditions necessary for an election to be truly democratic? 2 Marks
    For an election to be truly democratic, it must be: (1) Free — every adult citizen must have the right to vote without any pressure or discrimination; candidates must be free to campaign. (2) Fair — the process must be transparent; votes must be counted accurately; no rigging, bribing of voters, or use of government machinery to help any party. Both conditions are essential — elections without freedom or fairness do not make a democracy.
  • Q4. How does democracy help in resolving social conflicts? 2 Marks
    India and many countries are home to people of different castes, religions, and languages who may disagree. Democracy provides a peaceful method to deal with these differences: (1) People can express disagreements through free speech, protests, and elections rather than violence. (2) Decisions are made through debate and negotiation, which all groups can participate in. This reduces the risk of conflicts becoming violent.
  • Q5. What does Pakistan's example tell us about military rule? 2 Marks
    Pakistan under Pervez Musharraf (1999) shows that military rule is not democratic because: (1) The army took power by dismissing the elected government without any mandate from the people. (2) The constitution was suspended; there was no accountability to citizens. Even when Musharraf later held elections and a referendum, they were widely seen as manipulated to give a democratic look to military rule.
  • Q6. What is the importance of majority rule in democracy? What is its limitation? 2 Marks
    Importance: In any democracy, decisions are taken by the majority (more than half) — this is a practical way to reach decisions when people disagree.
    Limitation: Majority rule can become dangerous if the majority oppresses the minority. That is why democracy also requires protection of minority rights. The majority cannot take away the basic rights of any minority group — that would make democracy a "tyranny of the majority."
📚 3-Mark Questions & Answers

Write in 60–80 words with 3 clear organized points.

💡 Tip: For 3-mark Civics answers, give 3 NCERT points with brief explanation each. Don't go off-textbook.
  • Q1. What are the key features of democracy? Explain any three. 3 Marks
    (a) Elected Rulers: In a democracy, the government must be elected by the people through regular elections. Rulers who are not elected by the people (like military dictators or kings) do not qualify as democratic leaders.
    (b) Free and Fair Elections: Elections must be held at regular intervals; every citizen must have the right to vote; candidates must be free to campaign; results must be counted fairly without rigging.
    (c) One Person, One Vote, One Value: Every adult citizen gets one vote, and each vote has equal value regardless of the voter's wealth, education, caste, religion, or gender. This is the foundation of political equality in democracy.
  • Q2. With examples, explain how non-democratic governments differ from democratic ones. 3 Marks
    (a) Military Coup (Chile): In 1973, General Pinochet overthrew the elected President Allende. The people had no say in this change of government — a clear non-democratic action.
    (b) Rigged Elections (Zimbabwe): Mugabe held elections but harassed opponents and rigged results. Elections without freedom and fairness do not make a government democratic.
    (c) One-Party Rule (China): In China, only the Communist Party can rule. Citizens cannot vote for a different government. Absence of meaningful choice makes it non-democratic. In contrast, a true democracy like India allows multiple parties to contest elections freely.
  • Q3. Give three arguments in favour of democracy. 3 Marks
    (a) Accountable Government: A democratic government must answer to its citizens. If it fails, the people can vote it out at the next election. This constant accountability keeps governments responsible.
    (b) Better Quality Decisions: In a democracy, decisions are made through consultation and debate involving many people. This process takes longer but produces decisions that are more balanced, widely accepted, and better implemented.
    (c) Enhances Dignity of Citizens: Democracy treats every citizen as equal — each person's vote has the same value regardless of their social or economic status. This promotes the self-respect and dignity of every individual in society.
  • Q4. Why is accountability the most important feature of democracy? 3 Marks
    (a) Rulers answer to people: In a democracy, elected rulers know that they must face the voters again. If they do not perform well, they will lose the next election. This makes them work responsibly.
    (b) Mechanism of removal: Citizens have the power to remove a bad government through elections without bloodshed or violence. In dictatorships, the only way to remove a bad ruler is through a coup or revolution — which is violent and uncertain.
    (c) Transparency: Democratic governments are expected to be transparent — their decisions and policies are open to public scrutiny through a free press, parliament, and courts. This ensures that power is not misused.
  • Q5. What is the broader meaning of democracy beyond just government? 3 Marks
    According to NCERT, democracy is not just a form of government — it is a broader set of principles that can apply to all aspects of life:
    (a) Family: A family where all members — including women and children — have a voice in decisions, and where there is no discrimination based on gender, follows democratic principles.
    (b) Workplace: Workers having a say in their working conditions through trade unions or negotiations is a form of economic democracy.
    (c) Society: A democratic society is one where people of all castes, religions, and classes are treated with equal dignity and have equal rights. Democracy thus means equality and respect in everyday life, not just in elections.
🌟 5-Mark Questions & Answers

Long answers (5 marks). Write 120–150 words: Introduction → 4–5 points → Conclusion.

💡 Tip: Use NCERT's exact arguments — don't add points not in the textbook. Examiners specifically check for textbook points.
Q1. "Democracy is a better form of government when compared with dictatorship or any other alternative." Discuss. 5 Marks

Introduction: Democracy, while not perfect, is widely considered the best available form of government. NCERT presents several reasons for this.

(1) Accountable Government: A democratic government must answer to its citizens. Rulers are elected for a fixed term and must face re-election. This makes them responsible and responsive to people's needs. In a dictatorship, there is no such accountability.

(2) Better Decision-Making: Democracy involves consultation, debate, and discussion before decisions are made. While this process is slower, decisions made this way are better thought-out and more widely accepted by the population, making implementation easier.

(3) Resolves Conflicts Peacefully: In a diverse society like India, with many religions, languages, and castes, conflicts are inevitable. Democracy provides a peaceful, institutionalised method to deal with differences — through elections, courts, and free speech — rather than through violence.

(4) Enhances Dignity: Democracy treats every citizen as politically equal. Every adult has one vote of equal value. This principle of political equality promotes the dignity and self-respect of all citizens, particularly the poor and disadvantaged.

(5) Correcting Mistakes: In a democracy, if the government makes wrong decisions, people can vote it out and bring a better government. This self-correction mechanism is the most powerful argument for democracy — it allows a society to improve itself from within.

Conclusion: Democracy is not just a system of government — it is a way of life that promotes accountability, peaceful conflict resolution, and human dignity. Despite its limitations (slow decisions, corrupt politicians), its advantages far outweigh those of any alternative form of government.

Q2. What are the key features of a democratic government? Explain with examples from NCERT. 5 Marks

Introduction: Not every government that holds elections is democratic. NCERT identifies specific features that a government must have to be truly democratic.

(1) Elected Representatives: The rulers must be chosen by the people through elections. A government like Pinochet's in Chile, which came through a military coup overthrowing the elected Allende, is non-democratic no matter what it claims.

(2) Free and Fair Elections: Elections must be genuinely free. In Zimbabwe, elections were held, but Mugabe's government harassed and arrested opposition candidates. Since elections were not free and fair, Zimbabwe was not a real democracy.

(3) One Person, One Vote, One Value: Every adult citizen must have equal voting rights. In some historical democracies, women or the poor were denied votes — this violated this principle. True democracy gives one vote of equal value to every adult citizen.

(4) Rule of Law: The government must operate within the framework of the constitution and law. No one — not even the elected government — is above the law. Courts must be independent to enforce this.

(5) Minority Rights Protected: The majority rules, but minorities must not be oppressed. Their fundamental rights — to religion, culture, expression — must be protected by the constitution even against the wishes of the majority.

Conclusion: All these features together define a genuine democracy. The absence of even one feature — as seen in Zimbabwe (unfair elections) or China (no choice) — means the government is not truly democratic.

Q3. What are the arguments against democracy? How does NCERT respond to these arguments? 5 Marks

Introduction: Democracy is often criticised. NCERT acknowledges these arguments but explains why democracy is still the better choice.

(1) Argument: Instability due to changing leaders
Critics say frequent elections cause instability — policies keep changing with each new government.
Response: This instability is actually accountability in action. A government that fails the people should be replaced. The ability to change leaders peacefully is a strength, not a weakness.

(2) Argument: Democracy leads to slow decisions
Too much consultation and debate slows down decision-making — a dictator can act faster.
Response: Slower decisions are often better decisions. They are broadly accepted and more effectively implemented, whereas quick dictatorial decisions may be wrong and face resistance.

(3) Argument: Politicians are corrupt and self-interested
Elected politicians often pursue personal interests rather than public good.
Response: In democracy, citizens have the power to vote out corrupt politicians. The remedy is within the system — more active citizens, free press, and stronger institutions.

(4) Argument: Decisions based on political competition, not good values
Politicians make decisions to win votes rather than for the country's genuine benefit.
Response: Political competition and disagreement are signs of a healthy democracy. Debate and different viewpoints lead to better policies over time. A society that silences debate (as in China or North Korea) is far worse off.

Conclusion: While democracy has real limitations, NCERT concludes that the alternatives — military rule, monarchy, one-party rule — are far worse. Democracy's flaws can be corrected from within by active, informed citizens.

  • Definition: "Democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people."
  • Features: Elected rulers, free & fair elections, one person-one vote-one value, rule of law, majority rule + minority rights, basic rights protected.
  • Non-democratic examples: Zimbabwe (Mugabe-rigged elections), Mexico (PRI-70 years), Chile (Pinochet-1973 coup), Pakistan (Musharraf-1999), China (one-party), Saudi Arabia (monarchy).
  • Democracy examples/struggles: Poland (Solidarity-Lech Walesa-1989), Myanmar (Aung San Suu Kyi-house arrest).
  • Why Democracy: (1) Accountable govt, (2) Better decisions, (3) Resolves conflicts, (4) Dignity of citizens, (5) Corrects own mistakes.
  • Arguments against & responses: Instability → accountability; Slow decisions → better implemented; Corrupt leaders → can be voted out.
