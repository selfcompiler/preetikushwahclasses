🇦🇫 South Africa and Apartheid
📚 What is Apartheid? The word "Apartheid" is an Afrikaans word meaning "separateness." It was a system of racial discrimination in South Africa where the white minority ruled over the black majority through extreme, legally enforced segregation.
The Apartheid System
  • South Africa was colonised by Dutch settlers (called Boers or Afrikaners) followed by the British
  • The white minority ruled South Africa; the black majority (along with mixed-race "Coloured" and Indian communities) had no political rights
  • Blacks could not vote, own land near whites, or use the same facilities (schools, hospitals, buses, beaches)
  • The National Party (white) formally introduced apartheid laws after winning the 1948 elections
  • ANC (African National Congress) — founded in 1912 — led the struggle against apartheid
  • Nelson Mandela was the key leader of ANC; he was imprisoned in 1964 for 27 years
  • International pressure, economic sanctions, and internal struggle forced the white government to negotiate
  • Mandela was released in 1990; F.W. de Klerk (last apartheid President) negotiated the transition
  • In 1994, South Africa held its first free elections — Mandela won and became the first black President
The New South African Constitution
  • A new constitution was written through a negotiated process between the ANC and the white National Party
  • All groups — black, white, coloured — participated in drafting the new constitution
  • It guaranteed equal rights to all citizens regardless of race
  • The constitution became a model for the world — showing how a deeply divided society could heal through a democratic constitution
💡 Exam Tip: The key chain for South Africa: Apartheid (separateness) → ANC + Mandela → imprisoned 1964 → released 1990 → de Klerk negotiates → 1994 first free elections → Mandela = first black President → new constitution. This chain is asked in MCQs and short answers.
📋 Why Do We Need a Constitution?

A constitution is a set of fundamental rules and principles that governs a country. NCERT explains why every democracy needs one:

#ReasonExplanation
1Sets Rules for GovernmentA constitution specifies how the government will be formed, how it will function, and what powers it will have. It prevents chaos.
2Prevents Abuse of PowerWithout a constitution, rulers could take unlimited power. A constitution sets limits on what the government can and cannot do.
3Protects Fundamental RightsA constitution guarantees basic rights to citizens (freedom, equality, right to life) that the government cannot violate, even through laws.
4Builds TrustIn diverse societies, a constitution assures different groups (minorities, states, communities) that their interests will be protected. This creates trust.
5Expresses National ValuesA constitution reflects the ideals, hopes, and aspirations of the people — what kind of society they want to build.
💡 Exam Tip: For "Why do we need a constitution?", use all 5 NCERT points. For a 2-mark answer, use any 2. For 5-mark, use all 5 with explanation.

📖 The Preamble of the Indian Constitution
📖 The Preamble

WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:


JUSTICE, social, economic and political;

LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;

EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;

and to promote among them all

FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;


IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.

Key Words of the Preamble — Meanings
WordMeaning
SovereignIndia is completely independent; not under the control of any foreign power; free to make its own decisions
SocialistWealth should be distributed equitably; government should reduce inequality; (added by 42nd Amendment, 1976)
SecularIndia has no official religion; the state treats all religions equally; freedom of religion for all citizens; (added by 42nd Amendment, 1976)
DemocraticGovernment is elected by the people; based on free and fair elections
RepublicThe Head of State (President) is elected, not hereditary; there is no monarchy
Four Goals of the Preamble
GoalTypes
JusticeSocial (no discrimination), Economic (equal wages, fair distribution), Political (equal right to vote and participate)
LibertyOf thought, expression, belief, faith, and worship
EqualityOf status and opportunity — no one is superior by birth
FraternityBrotherhood among all citizens; dignity of the individual; unity and integrity of the Nation
🏭 Important Note: The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were NOT in the original Preamble. They were added later by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment in 1976. The original Preamble had only "Sovereign Democratic Republic."
💡 Exam Tip: The Preamble is like the "soul of the Constitution". Know the 5 key words (SSSDR) and the 4 goals (JELF) — Justice, Equality, Liberty, Fraternity. Also remember: Socialist + Secular added in 1976 by 42nd Amendment. This is a very common exam question.

    • b) Rajendra Prasad

    • d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
    ✅ Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Indian Constitution. He is often called the "Father of the Indian Constitution." Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President of the Constituent Assembly.
  • Q2. The Indian Constitution came into effect on:
    • a) November 26, 1949
    • b) January 26, 1950
    • c) August 15, 1947
    • d) December 9, 1946
    ✅ The Indian Constitution came into force on January 26, 1950 — celebrated as Republic Day. It was adopted on November 26, 1949 (Constitution Day). Independence was on August 15, 1947, and the Constituent Assembly first met on December 9, 1946.
  • Q3. What does the word "Apartheid" mean?
    • a) Freedom
    • b) Democracy
    • c) Separateness
    • d) Equality
    ✅ Apartheid is an Afrikaans word meaning "separateness." It refers to the system of racial segregation practiced in South Africa, where the white minority enforced strict separation from the black majority through law.
  • Q4. Nelson Mandela was imprisoned in South Africa in the year:
    • a) 1948
    • b) 1960
    • c) 1964
    • d) 1990
    ✅ Nelson Mandela was imprisoned in 1964 and spent 27 years in jail (Robben Island). He was released in 1990. In 1994, after South Africa's first free elections, he became the country's first black President.
  • Q5. The Objectives Resolution was moved in the Constituent Assembly by:
    • a) Jawaharlal Nehru
    • b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
    • c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
    • d) Sardar Patel
    ✅ Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on December 13, 1946 in the Constituent Assembly. This resolution laid out the guiding principles for making the Constitution and later became the basis for the Preamble.
  • Q6. How long did it take to make the Indian Constitution?
    • a) 1 year, 6 months
    • b) 2 years, 6 months
    • c) 2 years, 11 months and 18 days
    • d) 3 years, 2 months
    ✅ The Constituent Assembly took 2 years, 11 months and 18 days to frame the Constitution. It held 11 sessions spanning 165 days of debate. The Assembly first met on December 9, 1946 and the Constitution was adopted on November 26, 1949.
  • Q7. The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were added to the Preamble by:
    • a) 1st Constitutional Amendment, 1950
    • b) 25th Constitutional Amendment, 1971
    • c) 42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976
    • d) 44th Constitutional Amendment, 1978
    ✅ The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were inserted into the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment in 1976 during the Emergency period under Prime Minister Indira Gandhi. The original 1950 Preamble read: "Sovereign Democratic Republic."
  • Q8. The first free elections in South Africa were held in the year:
    • a) 1990
    • b) 1992
    • c) 1994
    • d) 1996
    ✅ South Africa's first free and fair elections were held in 1994. All races — black, white, coloured — were allowed to vote for the first time. Nelson Mandela's ANC won, and he became South Africa's first black President.
  • Q9. "Republic" in the Preamble of India means:
    • a) India has a Prime Minister
    • b) The Head of State is elected, not hereditary
    • c) India has no states
    • d) All citizens are equal
    ✅ Republic means India's Head of State (the President) is elected by the people's representatives, not a hereditary position like a king or queen. India is NOT a monarchy. The President is elected every 5 years by elected members of Parliament and State Assemblies.
  • Q10. The Preamble begins with the words:
    • a) "In the name of God, We the People..."
    • b) "The Government of India hereby declares..."
    • c) "We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved..."
    • d) "All citizens of India shall be equal..."
    ✅ The Preamble begins with "We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved..." These opening words are very significant — they establish that the Constitution derives its authority from the people of India, not from any external power or ruler.

📄 2-Mark Questions & Answers

Write in 30–40 words. Cover 2 key points from NCERT.

  • Q1. What was Apartheid? Why did the black people of South Africa oppose it? 2 Marks
    Apartheid was a system of racial segregation in South Africa where the white minority government discriminated against the black majority. The black people opposed it because: (1) they were denied the right to vote and own property, (2) they were forced to use separate schools, hospitals, and public places of lower quality, and (3) they were treated as second-class citizens in their own land.
  • Q2. What is the Preamble? What is its significance? 2 Marks
    The Preamble is the introductory statement of the Indian Constitution that outlines its objectives and guiding principles. It begins with "We, the People of India." Significance: (1) It declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic, and (2) It sets out the four goals — Justice, Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity — that the Constitution aims to achieve for all citizens. It is considered the soul of the Constitution.
  • Q3. Mention any two important facts about the making of the Indian Constitution. 2 Marks
    Two important facts about the making of the Indian Constitution: (1) The Constituent Assembly first met on December 9, 1946. It had about 308 members, and Dr. Rajendra Prasad was its President. The Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar. (2) The Constitution was adopted on November 26, 1949 and came into force on January 26, 1950, after 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days of work.
  • Q4. Why do we need a constitution? Give any two reasons. 2 Marks
    (1) Prevents abuse of power: A constitution sets limits on what the government can do. Without it, rulers could take unlimited power and oppress citizens. The constitution ensures no one is above the law. (2) Protects fundamental rights: The constitution guarantees basic rights (freedom, equality, right to life) to every citizen. Even the government cannot take away these rights through ordinary laws.
  • Q5. Explain the terms "Justice" and "Fraternity" as used in the Preamble. 2 Marks
    Justice: The Preamble promises three types of justice — Social (no discrimination based on caste, religion, gender), Economic (equal wages, fair distribution of wealth), and Political (equal right to vote and participate in government).
    Fraternity: Fraternity means brotherhood and fellow-feeling among all citizens of India. It ensures the dignity of every individual and promotes the unity and integrity of the nation — so that people of different religions, castes, and regions feel part of one nation.
  • Q6. What role did Nelson Mandela play in South Africa's democratic transition? 2 Marks
    Nelson Mandela was the key leader of the ANC (African National Congress) in the struggle against apartheid. Despite being imprisoned for 27 years (1964–1990), he continued to symbolise the fight for equality. After his release, he negotiated with the white government (led by F.W. de Klerk) for a peaceful transition to democracy. In 1994, after South Africa's first free elections, Mandela became the country's first black President.
📚 3-Mark Questions & Answers

Write in 60–80 words with 3 clear organized points. NCERT content only.

  • Q1. Describe the making of the Indian Constitution. 3 Marks
    (a) Constituent Assembly: The Indian Constitution was framed by a specially elected Constituent Assembly. It first met on December 9, 1946. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was its President and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee. Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on December 13, 1946.
    (b) Process: The Assembly had about 308 members and held 11 sessions over 165 days of actual debate. All sections of society — including minorities and women — participated. Ideas were drawn from the independence movement, world democracies, and earlier constitutions.
    (c) Adoption: The Constitution was adopted on November 26, 1949 (Constitution Day) and came into force on January 26, 1950 (Republic Day) after taking 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days to complete.
  • Q2. Explain the five key words of the Preamble of the Indian Constitution. 3 Marks
    The Preamble declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic:
    (a) Sovereign: India is completely independent, not under any foreign power's control.
    (b) Socialist + Secular: Wealth is to be distributed equitably (Socialist); India has no official religion and treats all religions equally (Secular). Both words were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
    (c) Democratic: The government is elected by the people through free and fair elections.
    (d) Republic: The Head of State (President) is elected, not a hereditary monarch. These five words together capture the fundamental character of the Indian state.
  • Q3. Explain the Apartheid system in South Africa and how it ended. 3 Marks
    (a) The System: Apartheid (meaning "separateness") was the system of racial segregation in South Africa. The white minority ruled the black majority. Blacks could not vote, live near whites, or use equal facilities. The National Party formally enforced it from 1948 onwards.
    (b) Struggle: The ANC (African National Congress) led by Nelson Mandela fought against apartheid. Mandela was imprisoned for 27 years (1964–1990). There was also international economic pressure and sanctions against South Africa.
    (c) End of Apartheid: Mandela was released in 1990. The white government led by F.W. de Klerk negotiated with the ANC. In 1994, South Africa's first free elections were held. Mandela became the first black President. A new democratic constitution was then drafted.
  • Q4. Explain the four goals mentioned in the Preamble of the Indian Constitution. 3 Marks
    The Preamble sets four goals for all Indian citizens:
    (a) Justice: Three types — Social (no discrimination by caste/religion/gender), Economic (fair distribution of wealth and equal wages), and Political (every citizen has equal right to vote and participate in government).
    (b) Liberty: Freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith, and worship — citizens can think, speak, and practise religion freely.
    (c) Equality: Equality of status and opportunity — no one is superior by birth; everyone gets equal chances in life and government.
    (d) Fraternity: Brotherhood among all citizens; the dignity of every individual must be upheld; the unity and integrity of the nation must be maintained.
  • Q5. Why do we need a constitution? Give three reasons. 3 Marks
    (a) Sets rules for government: A constitution lays down the fundamental rules for how the government will be formed, how it will function, and what powers each branch (legislature, executive, judiciary) will have. Without these rules, there would be chaos and conflict over power.
    (b) Prevents abuse of power: A constitution sets clear limits on what the government can and cannot do. No ruler or party can cross these limits. This protects citizens from arbitrary rule and dictatorship.
    (c) Protects rights and builds trust: The constitution guarantees fundamental rights to every citizen that the government cannot violate. It also assures minorities, states, and different groups that their interests are protected — building trust in the nation among all sections of society.

Q1. Describe the making of the Indian Constitution. What were its guiding principles? 5 Marks

Introduction: The Indian Constitution was drafted by the Constituent Assembly over nearly three years. It reflects the aspirations of the independence movement and the diversity of India.

(1) The Constituent Assembly: The Constitution was framed by a Constituent Assembly — a specially elected body of about 308 members. It first met on December 9, 1946. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was its President, and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee.

(2) Objectives Resolution: On December 13, 1946, Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution, which outlined the vision for India — a sovereign democratic republic guaranteeing liberty, equality, and justice to all citizens. This resolution became the foundation of the Preamble.

(3) Process of Debate: The Assembly held 11 sessions over 165 days of actual debate. Members discussed every clause in detail. The process was inclusive — members came from diverse religions, regions, castes, and political backgrounds. Even minority and women members participated.

(4) Sources of Inspiration: The framers drew inspiration from multiple sources — the French Revolution (Liberty, Equality, Fraternity in the Preamble), the British parliamentary system, the American Bill of Rights, and most importantly, the Indian independence movement and Gandhian values.

(5) Adoption and Implementation: The Constitution was adopted on November 26, 1949 and came into force on January 26, 1950 — taking 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days in total.

Conclusion: The Indian Constitution was not just a legal document — it was the expression of the hopes and dreams of a newly independent nation. Its inclusiveness, careful deliberation, and guiding values make it one of the finest constitutions in the world.

Q2. Explain the Preamble of the Indian Constitution. What values does it enshrine? 5 Marks

Introduction: The Preamble is the introductory statement of the Constitution. It is called the soul of the Constitution because it captures India's fundamental values and goals. It begins with the powerful words: "We, the People of India..."

(1) Five Descriptors — Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic:

• Sovereign: India is fully independent; not under any foreign power.

• Socialist: Wealth distributed equitably; reduction of inequality (added 1976).

• Secular: No state religion; all faiths treated equally (added 1976).

• Democratic: Government elected by people through free elections.

• Republic: Head of State (President) is elected, not hereditary.

(2) Justice — Social, Economic, Political: Social justice means no discrimination by caste or religion. Economic justice means fair distribution of resources. Political justice means equal right to vote and hold office — available to all citizens regardless of wealth or status.

(3) Liberty: Every citizen has freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith, and worship. This ensures citizens can live, think, and speak without government interference in their personal and intellectual lives.

(4) Equality: Equality of status means no one is superior or inferior by birth. Equality of opportunity means every citizen has an equal chance in education, employment, and government — regardless of caste, gender, or religion.

(5) Fraternity: Fraternity (brotherhood) ensures the dignity of every individual and promotes the unity and integrity of the nation. It binds together the diverse communities of India into one nation.

Conclusion: The Preamble is a promise made by the people of India to themselves. It sets the moral compass for all laws and policies. Any law that violates the values of the Preamble can be challenged in court — making it the supreme guiding document of Indian democracy.

Q3. Describe the system of Apartheid in South Africa and explain how the new democratic constitution was made. 5 Marks

Introduction: South Africa's experience of apartheid and its transition to democracy is one of the most remarkable stories of the 20th century — showing how a deeply divided society can heal through a democratic constitution.

(1) The Apartheid System: Apartheid (meaning "separateness") was a system of extreme racial discrimination enforced by South Africa's white minority government. After 1948, the National Party formally institutionalised apartheid — blacks could not vote, were forced to live in separate areas, use separate schools and hospitals, and were paid lower wages.

(2) The Struggle Against Apartheid: The ANC (African National Congress), led most prominently by Nelson Mandela, fought against apartheid for decades. Mandela was arrested in 1964 and spent 27 years in prison. International community imposed economic sanctions on South Africa.

(3) The Transition: By the late 1980s, the apartheid system became economically and politically unsustainable. The white President F.W. de Klerk released Mandela in 1990 and agreed to negotiate. They were both awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for this peaceful transition.

(4) The First Free Elections (1994): In 1994, South Africa held its first free elections in which people of all races voted. The ANC won and Nelson Mandela became the country's first black President.

(5) The New Constitution: A new constitution was drafted through a broad, multi-party process. It guaranteed equal rights to all citizens regardless of race, religion, or gender. It became a model for the world — showing how constitutional design can transform a deeply unequal society into a democracy based on dignity and equality.

Conclusion: South Africa's example teaches us that a well-designed constitution, built through inclusive negotiation, can help a nation emerge from its darkest history and build a just and equal society.
