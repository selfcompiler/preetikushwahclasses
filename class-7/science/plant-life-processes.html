🌱 Life Processes in Plants

Leaf Morphology · Plant Cell · Plastids · Photosynthesis Physiology · MCQs

📋 What Are Life Processes?

All living organisms — including plants — perform certain essential activities to stay alive, grow, and reproduce. These are called life processes.

🌿 Nutrition

Making and using food for energy & growth

💨 Respiration

Breaking food to release energy (ATP)

🚛 Transportation

Moving water, minerals & food in plant body

🗑️ Excretion

Removing waste products

🌸 Reproduction

Producing new plants (seeds, spores)

📏 Growth

Cell division & enlargement

💡 Key Idea: Plants are autotrophs — they make their own food using sunlight! Animals are heterotrophs — they depend on plants/other animals.
🍃 Leaf Morphology (External Structure)

The leaf is the most important plant organ for photosynthesis — often called the food factory. Let's look at its external structure.

Fig. 1 — External structure of a typical leaf

🔹 Parts of a Leaf

1️⃣ Leaf Base

Where leaf attaches to the stem. May have stipules.

2️⃣ Petiole

The leaf stalk. Holds the leaf away from the stem so it gets maximum sunlight.

3️⃣ Lamina (Blade)

The flat green part — the main photosynthesis area. Broad to capture sunlight.

4️⃣ Apex

The pointed tip of the leaf.

5️⃣ Margin

The edge of the leaf — can be smooth (entire), wavy, or toothed (serrated).

6️⃣ Veins & Midrib

Transport tubes! Midrib = main central vein. Veins carry water in and food out.

🔹 Types of Venation

TypeDescriptionExamples
🕸️ Reticulate Veins form a net-like pattern. Seen in dicots. Mango, Rose, Hibiscus, Peepal
📏 Parallel Veins run parallel to each other. Seen in monocots. Grass, Banana, Maize, Wheat

🔹 Types of Leaves

🍃 Simple Leaf

Lamina is in one single piece (undivided or only partly cut).

Examples: Mango, Banyan, Peepal, Guava

🍀 Compound Leaf

Lamina is divided into many leaflets, each looking like a small leaf.

Examples: Neem, Rose, Tamarind, Coconut

🔹 Functions of the Leaf

☀️ Photosynthesis

Makes food using sunlight, CO₂ & water

💧 Transpiration

Releases water vapour to cool the plant

🫧 Gas Exchange

Takes in CO₂, releases O₂ via stomata

🗄️ Storage

Some leaves store food (onion) or water (cactus)

💡 Special Modifications: Cactus leaves become spines (reduce water loss). Pitcher plant leaf becomes a trap (catches insects). Onion leaves store food (bulb).
🔬 Plant Cell — The Living Unit

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Plant cells have unique features that animal cells do not have. Let's explore!

Fig. 2 — Detailed structure of a plant cell with organelles

🔹 Parts of a Plant Cell

🧱 Cell Wall

Outermost rigid layer made of cellulose. Gives shape & support. Only in plants!

🛡️ Cell Membrane

Thin, selectively permeable layer below cell wall. Controls what enters/exits.

🧬 Nucleus

"Brain" of the cell. Contains DNA (genetic info) & controls all activities.

💧 Vacuole

Large central sac filled with cell sap. Stores water, food, waste. Very large in plant cells!

🟢 Chloroplasts

Green plastids — site of photosynthesis. Contain chlorophyll. Only in plants!

⚡ Mitochondria

"Powerhouse" — site of respiration. Releases ATP (energy).

📦 Golgi Body

Packages & transports proteins and other materials.

🕸️ Endoplasmic Reticulum

Network of tubes — transports materials inside cell. Has ribosomes on it.

🔵 Ribosomes

Tiny dots that make proteins.

🌀 Cytoplasm

Jelly-like fluid where all organelles float. Most chemical reactions happen here.

🎨 Plastids — The Colourful Organelles

Plastids are double-membrane organelles found only in plant cells. They make and store food, give colour to flowers/fruits, and contain their own DNA!

📝 Definition: Plastids are membrane-bound organelles that synthesize and store food materials and pigments. The term was coined by Haeckel (1865).

🔹 Three Main Types of Plastids

🟢

1. Chloroplast

Colour: Green (contains chlorophyll)

Function: Photosynthesis — make food using sunlight, CO₂ & water.

Found in: Leaves, green stems, unripe fruits

Pigments: Chlorophyll a, Chlorophyll b, Carotenoids

🌸

2. Chromoplast

Colour: Yellow, orange, red (no chlorophyll)

Function: Give colour to flowers, fruits, autumn leaves. Attract insects for pollination & seed dispersal.

Found in: Petals of flowers, ripe fruits (tomato, mango), carrot roots

Pigments: Carotene (orange), Xanthophyll (yellow), Lycopene (red)

⚪

3. Leucoplast

Colour: Colourless / white

Function: Storage of starch, oils, and proteins. Found where light doesn't reach.

Found in: Underground roots (potato, carrot), seeds, tubers

Sub-types: Amyloplast (starch), Elaioplast (oil), Proteinoplast (protein)

🔹 Sub-types of Leucoplast (Storage Plastids)

🥔 Amyloplast

Stores starch (carbohydrate). Found in potatoes, rice, wheat grains.

🌻 Elaioplast (Oleoplast)

Stores oils & fats. Found in oily seeds like groundnut, mustard, sunflower.

🌰 Proteinoplast (Aleuroplast)

Stores proteins. Found in pulses, nuts, beans.

🔹 Comparison of Plastids

ColourGreenYellow/Orange/RedColourless

💡 Interesting Fact: Plastids can convert into each other! When a green tomato ripens, the chloroplasts change into chromoplasts — that's why it turns red! 🍅
⚙️ Inside the Chloroplast — The Food Factory

The chloroplast is where all photosynthesis happens. It has a fascinating internal structure!

🔬 Detailed Structure of Chloroplast

🔹 Parts of a Chloroplast

📦 Outer Membrane

Smooth, freely permeable. Controls movement of materials in/out.

🛡️ Inner Membrane

Selectively permeable. Controls what passes into the stroma.

🌊 Stroma

Jelly-like fluid filling the chloroplast. Contains enzymes, DNA, ribosomes. Site of the Dark Reaction (Calvin Cycle).

💚 Thylakoid

Flat, coin-shaped sacs containing chlorophyll. Site of the Light Reaction.

📚 Granum (plural: Grana)

Stacks of thylakoids (like a stack of coins). Each chloroplast has 40–80 grana.

🔗 Stroma Lamella

Tubes that connect different grana together.

🔹 Two Phases of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis happens in two stages:

☀️ Phase 1: Light Reaction (Photochemical Phase)

Where? In the thylakoids (grana) of the chloroplast. Needs sunlight!

1
Light Absorption

Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight energy (mainly red and blue light).

2
Photolysis of Water

Water is split into Hydrogen (H⁺) + Oxygen (O₂). The O₂ is released into air!

2 H₂O → 4 H⁺ + O₂ + 4 e⁻

3
Energy Storage

Light energy is converted into chemical energy stored as ATP and NADPH.

🌑 Phase 2: Dark Reaction (Calvin Cycle / Biosynthetic Phase)

Where? In the stroma of the chloroplast. Does NOT need sunlight directly!

1
CO₂ Fixation

CO₂ from air is combined with a 5-carbon molecule (RuBP) using enzyme RuBisCO.

2
Reduction

ATP and NADPH from Light Reaction are used to convert CO₂ into glucose (sugar).

3
Glucose Formation

The final product — glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) — is stored as starch in leaves.

🔹 Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

☀️ Light Intensity

More light = faster photosynthesis (up to a limit). Too much light damages chlorophyll.

💨 CO₂ Concentration

More CO₂ generally speeds up photosynthesis. Normal air has 0.03% CO₂.

🌡️ Temperature

Optimum range: 25–35°C. Below 0°C or above 45°C, enzymes stop working.

💧 Water

Lack of water closes stomata, reducing CO₂ uptake. Photosynthesis slows down.

🟢 Chlorophyll

Yellow or damaged leaves can't make food. Healthy green leaves work best.

🫧 Oxygen

High O₂ levels can slow down photosynthesis (this is called photorespiration).

🔹 Stomata — Tiny Doors of the Leaf

🌞 OPEN — During Day

Guard cells swell → pore opens
CO₂ in, O₂ out

🌙 CLOSED — At Night

Guard cells shrink → pore closes
Prevents water loss

💡 Important: The first product of photosynthesis is glucose, which is converted to starch for storage. We test for photosynthesis using the iodine test — starch turns iodine blue-black!
💨 Respiration in Plants

Plants need energy to grow, reproduce, and stay alive. They get this energy by breaking down glucose in a process called cellular respiration. It happens inside the mitochondria of every plant cell — 24 hours a day!

⚡ The Mitochondria — Powerhouse of the Cell

⚡
⚡
⚡

Mitochondria breaks down glucose → releases ATP (energy)

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + Energy (ATP)

🔹 Types of Respiration

🫧 Aerobic Respiration

Uses oxygen. Releases maximum energy (38 ATP). Most plants use this.

Glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + Energy

🚫 Anaerobic Respiration

Without oxygen. Releases less energy (2 ATP). Happens in waterlogged roots, yeast.

Glucose → Ethanol + CO₂ + Energy

🔹 Photosynthesis ↔ Respiration — The Beautiful Cycle

These two processes are opposite of each other and form a continuous cycle in nature. The output of one is the input of the other!

⚡ Plants produce O₂ & glucose — cells use them to make energy — releasing CO₂ & H₂O — which plants use again!

🌊 1. Osmosis — Water Entering Roots

Movement of water from high water concentration (soil) to low water concentration (root cell) through a semi-permeable membrane.

💧 Osmosis Experiment

Water moves from low-concentration solute side (pure water) to high-concentration solute side (sugar)

📍 In plants: Soil has dilute solution → root cell sap has concentrated solution. Water moves into roots by osmosis through root hair cell membranes.

💧 2. Transpiration — Water Vapour Out

Transpiration is the loss of water in the form of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants (mainly leaves) through stomata. It creates the transpiration pull that draws water upward!

☀️ Why Transpiration Happens?

Sun heats leaves → water evaporates from leaf cells → exits through stomata as vapour.

💪 Why is it Important?

(i) Creates suction pull to lift water from roots, (ii) Cools the plant, (iii) Brings minerals up with water.

📡 3. Translocation — Food Distribution

The movement of food (mainly sucrose) made in leaves to all parts of the plant via phloem. It can flow both upward and downward — wherever food is needed!

📚 Source-to-Sink: Food is made in source (leaves) and transported to sinks (fruits, flowers, roots, growing tips). Phloem is the "delivery system" of the plant!

🍽️ Types of Nutrition in Plants

Not all plants make their own food! Plants are classified by how they get their nutrition. Let's see all 6 types with animated illustrations!

💨
🫧

🌿 1. Autotrophic

Plants that make their own food by photosynthesis using sunlight, water, and CO₂. They are the producers in the food chain.

Examples: Mango, Rose, Grass, Wheat, Banyan, Hibiscus, Tulsi
🩸

🌾 2. Parasitic

Steal food from host plants using special root-like structures called haustoria. Cannot make their own food — no chlorophyll!

Examples: Cuscuta (Amarbel) — yellow vine, Rafflesia — world's largest flower, Mistletoe

🍄 3. Saprophytic

Feed on dead and decaying matter. They secrete digestive juices on dead material, then absorb the nutrients. Nature's important decomposers!

Examples: Mushroom, Bread mould (Rhizopus), Yeast, Bracket fungi
🪰

🪲 4. Insectivorous (Carnivorous)

Trap and digest insects to get nitrogen. Grow in nitrogen-poor marshy soil. They still photosynthesise — insects are just a nitrogen supplement!

Examples: Venus Flytrap, Pitcher Plant (Nepenthes), Sundew (Drosera), Bladderwort

🌳 5. Symbiotic

Two organisms live together, both helping each other. A lichen = alga (makes food via photosynthesis) + fungus (provides protection & water).

Examples: Lichen (alga + fungus), Mycorrhiza (fungus + roots), Coral algae

Rhizobium bacteria live in root nodules of legumes (peas, beans). Bacteria fix atmospheric nitrogen for the plant; plant gives them food & shelter. Win-win!

🌸 Reproduction in Plants

Reproduction is the process by which plants produce new plants of their own kind. It keeps the species alive! Plants reproduce in two main ways:

🌸 Modes of Reproduction in Plants

1. Sexual Reproduction (Flowers)

Through flowers — male and female parts unite to form seeds. Requires pollination and fertilization.

  • Pollen from anther → stigma (pollination)
  • Helped by wind, water, insects, birds
  • Forms seeds & fruits

2. Spore Formation

Tiny spores produced inside sporangia. When ripe, they fall and grow into new plants. No need for two parents!

  • Found in ferns, mosses, fungi
  • Wind disperses spores
  • Each spore = potential new plant

3. Vegetative Propagation

New plants grow from parts of the parent plant like roots, stems, or leaves. Asexual — no seeds needed!

  • 🥔 Potato — from eyes (buds on tuber)
  • 🌿 Rose — from stem cutting
  • 🌱 Bryophyllum — from leaf edges
  • 🧅 Onion, Ginger — from bulbs/rhizomes

🔹 Sexual Reproduction — The Flower

Fig. — Parts of a flower with pollination shown

🔹 Parts of a Flower

🟢 Sepals (Calyx)

Green outer leaves. Protect flower bud.

🌸 Petals (Corolla)

Colourful, fragrant. Attract pollinators.

♂️ Stamen (Male)

Anther (makes pollen) + Filament (stalk)

🔹 Pollination → Fertilization → Seed

1
Pollination

Pollen transferred from anther to stigma. Done by wind, water, insects (bees, butterflies), or birds.

2
Fertilization

Male gamete (pollen) + Female gamete (ovule) → forms zygote. Happens inside the ovary.

3
Seed & Fruit Formation

Zygote → develops into seed. Ovary → develops into fruit. Seed contains baby plant + stored food.

4
Seed Dispersal

Seeds spread by wind, water, animals, explosion. Germinate when conditions are right → new plant!

🔹 Vegetative Propagation — Examples

🥔 Tuber (Potato)

🌿 Stem Cutting (Rose, Sugarcane)

Cut a stem piece, plant in soil → grows new roots

🍃 Leaf (Bryophyllum)

Tiny buds grow on leaf edges → fall off → new plant

🧅 Bulb (Onion, Garlic)

Underground bulb produces new shoots & roots

🌱 Rhizome (Ginger)

Underground horizontal stem with buds — each bud = new plant

🥕 Root (Sweet Potato, Dahlia)

Roots have buds that grow into new plants

🧪 NCERT Activities & Experiments

🧪 Activity 1.1: Testing for Starch (Iodine Test)

📖 NCERT Textbook Activity

☀️
Sunlight Plant
Leaf turns blue-black ✅
🌙
Dark Plant
No colour change ❌

The green leaf cycles between green and blue-black to show the iodine reaction

🎯 Aim: To show that sunlight is necessary for photosynthesis (starch formation).

📦 Materials: Two potted plants, a dark room or black box, iodine solution, beaker, water, spirit lamp.

👣 Steps:

  1. Take two similar potted plants. Keep one in complete darkness for 72 hours (to destarch the leaves).
  2. Keep the other plant in bright sunlight during this time.
  3. Pluck a leaf from each plant. Boil in water, then in alcohol (to remove chlorophyll).
  4. Wash the leaves and add a few drops of iodine solution to each.
  5. Sunlight plant leaf turns blue-black — starch is present!
  6. Dark plant leaf shows no colour change (stays brown) — no starch formed.
  7. Now place the dark plant in sunlight for 3-4 days and repeat the test — it also turns blue-black!
👀 Observation & Conclusion: Starch is produced only when the plant receives sunlight. This proves that sunlight is essential for photosynthesis. Without sunlight, no food (starch) is made in the leaf.

🔬 Activity 1.2: Observing Mould on Bread

📖 NCERT Textbook Activity

Water drops fall on bread, then fuzzy mould patches grow and spread

🎯 Aim: To observe the growth of fungi (mould) on bread and understand saprotrophic nutrition.

📦 Materials: A piece of bread, water, a warm place (cupboard), magnifying glass.

👣 Steps:

  1. Take a slice of bread and moisten it with a few drops of water.
  2. Place it in a warm, dark place (like inside a cupboard) for 2-3 days.
  3. After a few days, observe the bread — you will see fluffy patches of green, white, and black colour.
  4. Use a magnifying glass to look closely — you can see cotton-like threads (hyphae).
  5. These patches are fungi (mould) — they feed on the bread by secreting enzymes and absorbing nutrients.
👀 Observation & Conclusion: Mould grows on moist bread kept in a warm place. The fungi are saprotrophs — they secrete digestive juices onto the bread, break down nutrients externally, and then absorb them. This is called saprotrophic nutrition. The thread-like structures are called hyphae.

🔬 Extended Project: Partially Covered Leaf Test

🌟 Extended Learning Project

☀️

Sunlight

After Iodine Test:

Blue-black = exposed

Brown = covered

The exposed half cycles green to blue-black; the covered half is revealed when the black paper fades

🎯 Aim: To show that photosynthesis only occurs in the parts of a leaf exposed to sunlight.

📦 Materials: A potted plant, black paper strips, paper clips, iodine solution, beaker, water, spirit lamp.

👣 Steps:

  1. Take a healthy potted plant and destarch it by keeping it in dark for 2-3 days.
  2. Cover part of a leaf with black paper on both sides and secure with paper clips.
  3. Place the plant in bright sunlight for 2-5 days.
  4. Pluck the leaf, remove the black paper.
  5. Boil in water, then in alcohol to remove chlorophyll. Wash with water.
  6. Add iodine solution to the leaf.
  7. Covered part: stays yellow/brown (no starch). Uncovered part: turns blue-black (starch present).
👀 Observation & Conclusion: Only the part of the leaf that was exposed to sunlight turned blue-black with iodine, showing starch was present. The covered part did not form starch. This proves that light is essential for photosynthesis and it occurs only in the parts receiving sunlight.
💡 Exam Tip: These activities are frequently asked in exams as "Describe an experiment to show that sunlight is necessary for photosynthesis." Always mention: (1) Destarching, (2) Iodine test, (3) Blue-black = starch present.

    ✅ Answer: (b) Lower surface — to reduce water loss from direct sun.
  • 4. Which type of plastid gives the red colour to a tomato?
    • (a) Chloroplast
    • (b) Leucoplast
    • (c) Chromoplast
    • (d) Amyloplast
    ✅ Answer: (c) Chromoplast — contains lycopene (red pigment).
  • 5. The light reaction of photosynthesis occurs in:
    • (a) Thylakoid (grana)
    • (b) Stroma
    • (c) Cytoplasm
    • (d) Outer membrane
    ✅ Answer: (a) Thylakoid — chlorophyll absorbs sunlight here.
  • 6. Xylem transports:
    • (a) Food only
    • (b) Water and minerals
    • (c) Hormones
    • (d) Oxygen
    ✅ Answer: (b) Water and minerals — from roots upward to leaves.
  • 7. Which leaf shows parallel venation?
    • (a) Mango
    • (b) Rose
    • (c) Banana
    • (d) Peepal
    ✅ Answer: (c) Banana — monocots have parallel venation.
  • 8. The "powerhouse" of the cell is:
    • (a) Nucleus
    • (b) Chloroplast
    • (c) Mitochondria
    • (d) Vacuole
    ✅ Answer: (c) Mitochondria — site of respiration & ATP production.
  • 9. Cuscuta is an example of:
    • (a) Autotroph
    • (b) Parasite
    • (c) Saprophyte
    • (d) Insectivorous
    ✅ Answer: (b) Parasite — climbs on other plants and sucks nutrients.
  • 10. The cell wall in plants is made up of:
    • (a) Chitin
    • (b) Protein
    • (c) Cellulose
    • (d) Lipid
    ✅ Answer: (c) Cellulose — gives strength & rigid shape.
  • 11. The process of water loss from leaves is called:
    • (a) Respiration
    • (b) Transpiration
    • (c) Photosynthesis
    • (d) Translocation
    ✅ Answer: (b) Transpiration — water vapour exits through stomata.
  • 12. Which plastid stores starch?
    • (a) Chromoplast
    • (b) Elaioplast
    • (c) Amyloplast
    • (d) Proteinoplast
    ✅ Answer: (c) Amyloplast — type of leucoplast storing starch.
  • 13. The dark reaction of photosynthesis is also called:
    • (a) Hill reaction
    • (b) Calvin cycle
    • (c) Krebs cycle
    • (d) Glycolysis
    ✅ Answer: (b) Calvin cycle — discovered by Melvin Calvin.
  • 14. Stack of thylakoids is called:
    • (a) Granum
    • (b) Stroma
    • (c) Cristae
    • (d) Matrix
    ✅ Answer: (a) Granum (plural: grana) — stacks of thylakoid discs.
  • 15. Which gas is released during photosynthesis?
    • (a) CO₂
    • (b) Nitrogen
    • (c) Oxygen
    • (d) Hydrogen
    ✅ Answer: (c) Oxygen — released from splitting of water.

✍️ Short Answer Questions
  • Q1. Define photosynthesis.
    Ans: Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food (glucose) using carbon dioxide, water, and sunlight in the presence of chlorophyll, releasing oxygen as a by-product.
  • Q2. Name the three types of plastids and their colours.
    Ans: (i) Chloroplast — green, (ii) Chromoplast — yellow/orange/red, (iii) Leucoplast — colourless (white).
  • Q3. Why are leaves called "food factories" of plants?
    Ans: Leaves are called food factories because they contain chlorophyll in their chloroplasts, which carries out photosynthesis to make food (glucose) for the entire plant.
  • Q4. What is the function of stomata?
    Ans: Stomata are tiny pores on the lower surface of leaves. They (i) allow exchange of gases (CO₂ in, O₂ out), and (ii) release water vapour through transpiration.
  • Q5. Differentiate between xylem and phloem.
    Ans: Xylem transports water and minerals from roots to leaves (upward only) and is made of dead cells. Phloem transports food from leaves to all plant parts (both up and down) and is made of living cells.
  • Q6. What is the difference between light reaction and dark reaction?
    Ans: Light reaction occurs in thylakoids, needs sunlight, splits water, and produces ATP, NADPH, and O₂. Dark reaction occurs in stroma, doesn't need sunlight, uses CO₂ + ATP + NADPH to make glucose.
  • Q7. Why is a Venus Flytrap called an insectivorous plant?
    Ans: Because it traps and digests insects to get nitrogen, which is lacking in the marshy soil where it grows. It still photosynthesises like other green plants — the insects are just a nitrogen supplement.
  • Q8. Name the parts of a chloroplast.
    Ans: Outer membrane, inner membrane, stroma (fluid), thylakoid (coin-shaped sacs), granum (stack of thylakoids), and stroma lamella (tubes connecting grana).
  • Q9. What is reticulate venation? Give example.
    Ans: When veins of a leaf form a net-like pattern, it is called reticulate venation. Found in dicot plants. Examples: Mango, Rose, Peepal.
  • Q10. Why is the central vacuole large in plant cells?
    Ans: The large vacuole stores water, cell sap, nutrients, and waste products. It also maintains turgor pressure, which keeps the plant firm and upright. Without it, plants would wilt.
  • Q11. Write the chemical equation of photosynthesis.
    Ans: 6 CO₂ + 12 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ + 6 H₂O (in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll).
  • Q12. What is the role of root hairs?
    Ans: Root hairs are tiny thread-like extensions of root cells. They greatly increase the surface area of the root and absorb water and minerals from soil by the process of osmosis.
  • Q13. List four factors affecting photosynthesis.
    Ans: (i) Light intensity, (ii) Concentration of CO₂, (iii) Temperature (optimum 25–35°C), and (iv) Availability of water.
  • Q14. Why does a green tomato become red when ripe?
    Ans: When the tomato ripens, the green chloroplasts inside its cells convert into red chromoplasts containing the pigment lycopene. This change of plastid type causes the colour change.
  • Q15. What is transpiration pull?
    Ans: The suction force created in xylem when water evaporates from leaves through stomata. This pull draws water upward from the roots through the entire plant — much like sipping juice through a straw.
