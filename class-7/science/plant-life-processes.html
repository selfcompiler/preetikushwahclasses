🌱 Life Processes in Plants

Leaf Morphology · Plant Cell · Plastids · Photosynthesis Physiology · MCQs

📋 What Are Life Processes?

All living organisms — including plants — perform certain essential activities to stay alive, grow, and reproduce. These are called life processes.

🌿 Nutrition

Making and using food for energy & growth

💨 Respiration

Breaking food to release energy (ATP)

🚛 Transportation

Moving water, minerals & food in plant body

🗑️ Excretion

Removing waste products

🌸 Reproduction

Producing new plants (seeds, spores)

📏 Growth

Cell division & enlargement

💡 Key Idea: Plants are autotrophs — they make their own food using sunlight! Animals are heterotrophs — they depend on plants/other animals.
🍃 Leaf Morphology (External Structure)

The leaf is the most important plant organ for photosynthesis — often called the food factory. Let's look at its external structure.

Fig. 1 — External structure of a typical leaf

🔹 Parts of a Leaf

1️⃣ Leaf Base

Where leaf attaches to the stem. May have stipules.

2️⃣ Petiole

The leaf stalk. Holds the leaf away from the stem so it gets maximum sunlight.

3️⃣ Lamina (Blade)

The flat green part — the main photosynthesis area. Broad to capture sunlight.

4️⃣ Apex

The pointed tip of the leaf.

5️⃣ Margin

The edge of the leaf — can be smooth (entire), wavy, or toothed (serrated).

6️⃣ Veins & Midrib

Transport tubes! Midrib = main central vein. Veins carry water in and food out.

🔹 Types of Venation

TypeDescriptionExamples
🕸️ Reticulate Veins form a net-like pattern. Seen in dicots. Mango, Rose, Hibiscus, Peepal
📏 Parallel Veins run parallel to each other. Seen in monocots. Grass, Banana, Maize, Wheat

🔹 Types of Leaves

🍃 Simple Leaf

Lamina is in one single piece (undivided or only partly cut).

Examples: Mango, Banyan, Peepal, Guava

🍀 Compound Leaf

Lamina is divided into many leaflets, each looking like a small leaf.

Examples: Neem, Rose, Tamarind, Coconut

🔹 Functions of the Leaf

☀️ Photosynthesis

Makes food using sunlight, CO₂ & water

💧 Transpiration

Releases water vapour to cool the plant

🫧 Gas Exchange

Takes in CO₂, releases O₂ via stomata

🗄️ Storage

Some leaves store food (onion) or water (cactus)

💡 Special Modifications: Cactus leaves become spines (reduce water loss). Pitcher plant leaf becomes a trap (catches insects). Onion leaves store food (bulb).
🔬 Plant Cell — The Living Unit

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Plant cells have unique features that animal cells do not have. Let's explore!

Fig. 2 — Detailed structure of a plant cell with organelles

🔹 Parts of a Plant Cell

🧱 Cell Wall

Outermost rigid layer made of cellulose. Gives shape & support. Only in plants!

🛡️ Cell Membrane

Thin, selectively permeable layer below cell wall. Controls what enters/exits.

🧬 Nucleus

"Brain" of the cell. Contains DNA (genetic info) & controls all activities.

💧 Vacuole

Large central sac filled with cell sap. Stores water, food, waste. Very large in plant cells!

🟢 Chloroplasts

Green plastids — site of photosynthesis. Contain chlorophyll. Only in plants!

⚡ Mitochondria

"Powerhouse" — site of respiration. Releases ATP (energy).

📦 Golgi Body

Packages & transports proteins and other materials.

🕸️ Endoplasmic Reticulum

Network of tubes — transports materials inside cell. Has ribosomes on it.

🔵 Ribosomes

Tiny dots that make proteins.

🌀 Cytoplasm

Jelly-like fluid where all organelles float. Most chemical reactions happen here.

🎨 Plastids — The Colourful Organelles

Plastids are double-membrane organelles found only in plant cells. They make and store food, give colour to flowers/fruits, and contain their own DNA!

📝 Definition: Plastids are membrane-bound organelles that synthesize and store food materials and pigments. The term was coined by Haeckel (1865).

🔹 Three Main Types of Plastids

🟢

1. Chloroplast

Colour: Green (contains chlorophyll)

Function: Photosynthesis — make food using sunlight, CO₂ & water.

Found in: Leaves, green stems, unripe fruits

Pigments: Chlorophyll a, Chlorophyll b, Carotenoids

🌸

2. Chromoplast

Colour: Yellow, orange, red (no chlorophyll)

Function: Give colour to flowers, fruits, autumn leaves. Attract insects for pollination & seed dispersal.

Found in: Petals of flowers, ripe fruits (tomato, mango), carrot roots

Pigments: Carotene (orange), Xanthophyll (yellow), Lycopene (red)

⚪

3. Leucoplast

Colour: Colourless / white

Function: Storage of starch, oils, and proteins. Found where light doesn't reach.

Found in: Underground roots (potato, carrot), seeds, tubers

Sub-types: Amyloplast (starch), Elaioplast (oil), Proteinoplast (protein)

🔹 Sub-types of Leucoplast (Storage Plastids)

🥔 Amyloplast

Stores starch (carbohydrate). Found in potatoes, rice, wheat grains.

🌻 Elaioplast (Oleoplast)

Stores oils & fats. Found in oily seeds like groundnut, mustard, sunflower.

🌰 Proteinoplast (Aleuroplast)

Stores proteins. Found in pulses, nuts, beans.

🔹 Comparison of Plastids

💡 Interesting Fact: Plastids can convert into each other! When a green tomato ripens, the chloroplasts change into chromoplasts — that's why it turns red! 🍅
⚙️ Inside the Chloroplast — The Food Factory

The chloroplast is where all photosynthesis happens. It has a fascinating internal structure!

🔬 Detailed Structure of Chloroplast

🔹 Parts of a Chloroplast

📦 Outer Membrane

Smooth, freely permeable. Controls movement of materials in/out.

🛡️ Inner Membrane

Selectively permeable. Controls what passes into the stroma.

🌊 Stroma

Jelly-like fluid filling the chloroplast. Contains enzymes, DNA, ribosomes. Site of the Dark Reaction (Calvin Cycle).

💚 Thylakoid

Flat, coin-shaped sacs containing chlorophyll. Site of the Light Reaction.

📚 Granum (plural: Grana)

Stacks of thylakoids (like a stack of coins). Each chloroplast has 40–80 grana.

🔗 Stroma Lamella

Tubes that connect different grana together.

🔹 Two Phases of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis happens in two stages:

☀️ Phase 1: Light Reaction (Photochemical Phase)

Where? In the thylakoids (grana) of the chloroplast. Needs sunlight!

1
Light Absorption

Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight energy (mainly red and blue light).

2
Photolysis of Water

Water is split into Hydrogen (H⁺) + Oxygen (O₂). The O₂ is released into air!

2 H₂O → 4 H⁺ + O₂ + 4 e⁻

3
Energy Storage

Light energy is converted into chemical energy stored as ATP and NADPH.

🌑 Phase 2: Dark Reaction (Calvin Cycle / Biosynthetic Phase)

Where? In the stroma of the chloroplast. Does NOT need sunlight directly!

1
CO₂ Fixation

CO₂ from air is combined with a 5-carbon molecule (RuBP) using enzyme RuBisCO.

2
Reduction

ATP and NADPH from Light Reaction are used to convert CO₂ into glucose (sugar).

3
Glucose Formation

The final product — glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) — is stored as starch in leaves.

🔹 Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

☀️ Light Intensity

More light = faster photosynthesis (up to a limit). Too much light damages chlorophyll.

💨 CO₂ Concentration

More CO₂ generally speeds up photosynthesis. Normal air has 0.03% CO₂.

🌡️ Temperature

Optimum range: 25–35°C. Below 0°C or above 45°C, enzymes stop working.

💧 Water

Lack of water closes stomata, reducing CO₂ uptake. Photosynthesis slows down.

🟢 Chlorophyll

Yellow or damaged leaves can't make food. Healthy green leaves work best.

🫧 Oxygen

High O₂ levels can slow down photosynthesis (this is called photorespiration).

🔹 Stomata — Tiny Doors of the Leaf

🌞 OPEN — During Day

Guard cells swell → pore opens
CO₂ in, O₂ out

🌙 CLOSED — At Night

Guard cells shrink → pore closes
Prevents water loss

💡 Important: The first product of photosynthesis is glucose, which is converted to starch for storage. We test for photosynthesis using the iodine test — starch turns iodine blue-black!
💨 Respiration in Plants

Plants need energy to grow, reproduce, and stay alive. They get this energy by breaking down glucose in a process called cellular respiration.

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + Energy (ATP)

🔹 Types of Respiration

🫧 Aerobic Respiration

Uses oxygen. Releases maximum energy (38 ATP). Most plants use this.

Glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + Energy

🚫 Anaerobic Respiration

Without oxygen. Releases less energy (2 ATP). Happens in waterlogged roots, yeast.

Glucose → Ethanol + CO₂ + Energy

💡 Should we sleep under a tree at night? Sleeping under a tree at night is generally not harmful — trees release very little CO₂ compared to a small room. But during the day, trees release lots of O₂ — so daytime under a tree is wonderfully refreshing! 🌳
🚛 Transportation in Plants

Plants have a special network of tubes called the vascular system to move water, minerals, and food throughout the plant body.

🔵 XYLEM

↑

Carries: Water + Minerals

Direction: Roots → Leaves
(UPWARD only)

Made of dead cells — like hollow pipes!

Powered by Transpiration Pull

🟡 PHLOEM

↕

Carries: Food (Sucrose)

Direction: Leaves → All parts
(BOTH UP and DOWN)

Made of living cells (sieve tubes)

Process called Translocation

🔹 Three Key Processes

🌊 Osmosis

Water enters root hairs from soil by moving from high concentration (soil) to low concentration (root cell sap) through a semi-permeable membrane.

💧 Transpiration

Loss of water vapour through stomata. Creates a suction force that pulls water up from roots through xylem — like sipping juice from a straw!

📡 Translocation

Movement of food (made in leaves) through phloem to all parts of the plant — roots, fruits, seeds, growing tips.

💡 Easy Memory Trick:
🔵 Xylem = eXclusively Water (UP only) 💧
🟡 Phloem = Phood (food, both ways) 🍬
🗑️ Excretion in Plants

Plants produce waste products too! Their excretion is much simpler than in animals.

💨 Gaseous Waste

CO₂ (from respiration), O₂ (from photosynthesis), and water vapour are released through stomata and lenticels.

🍂 Leaf Fall (Abscission)

In autumn, plants store solid waste in older leaves, then shed them — like throwing out the trash!

🌿 Stored Substances

Resins (in pine), gums (acacia), tannins (in bark), latex (rubber) — all stored waste materials.

🌱 Through Roots

Some plants excrete waste compounds directly into the surrounding soil through roots.

💡 Did you know? Many useful products come from plant waste! Rubber from latex, resin for varnish, opium from poppy, and many medicines like quinine!
🍽️ Types of Nutrition in Plants

Not all plants make their own food! Plants are classified by how they get their nutrition.

🌿 Autotrophic

Make own food by photosynthesis. All green plants.

Examples: Mango, Rose, Grass, Wheat, Banyan

🌾 Parasitic

Steal food from host plants using special structures called haustoria. No chlorophyll!

Examples: Cuscuta (Amarbel), Rafflesia, Mistletoe

🍄 Saprophytic

Feed on dead and decaying matter. Important decomposers in nature!

Examples: Mushroom, Bread mould (Rhizopus), Yeast

🪲 Insectivorous (Carnivorous)

Trap and digest insects for nitrogen. Grow in nitrogen-poor swampy soil.

Examples: Venus Flytrap, Pitcher Plant, Sundew, Bladderwort

🌳 Symbiotic

Two organisms live together, both benefit. Like lichen = alga + fungus living together!

Examples: Lichen, Mycorrhiza (fungi + roots)

🐝 Mutualistic

Rhizobium bacteria live in root nodules of legumes (like peas), fixing nitrogen for the plant in exchange for sugars.

✍️ Short Answer Questions
  • Q1. Define photosynthesis.
    Ans: Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food (glucose) using carbon dioxide, water, and sunlight in the presence of chlorophyll, releasing oxygen as a by-product.
  • Q2. Name the three types of plastids and their colours.
    Ans: (i) Chloroplast — green, (ii) Chromoplast — yellow/orange/red, (iii) Leucoplast — colourless (white).
  • Q3. Why are leaves called "food factories" of plants?
    Ans: Leaves are called food factories because they contain chlorophyll in their chloroplasts, which carries out photosynthesis to make food (glucose) for the entire plant.
  • Q4. What is the function of stomata?
    Ans: Stomata are tiny pores on the lower surface of leaves. They (i) allow exchange of gases (CO₂ in, O₂ out), and (ii) release water vapour through transpiration.
  • Q5. Differentiate between xylem and phloem.
    Ans: Xylem transports water and minerals from roots to leaves (upward only) and is made of dead cells. Phloem transports food from leaves to all plant parts (both up and down) and is made of living cells.
  • Q6. What is the difference between light reaction and dark reaction?
    Ans: Light reaction occurs in thylakoids, needs sunlight, splits water, and produces ATP, NADPH, and O₂. Dark reaction occurs in stroma, doesn't need sunlight, uses CO₂ + ATP + NADPH to make glucose.
  • Q7. Why is a Venus Flytrap called an insectivorous plant?
    Ans: Because it traps and digests insects to get nitrogen, which is lacking in the marshy soil where it grows. It still photosynthesises like other green plants — the insects are just a nitrogen supplement.
  • Q8. Name the parts of a chloroplast.
    Ans: Outer membrane, inner membrane, stroma (fluid), thylakoid (coin-shaped sacs), granum (stack of thylakoids), and stroma lamella (tubes connecting grana).
  • Q9. What is reticulate venation? Give example.
    Ans: When veins of a leaf form a net-like pattern, it is called reticulate venation. Found in dicot plants. Examples: Mango, Rose, Peepal.
  • Q10. Why is the central vacuole large in plant cells?
    Ans: The large vacuole stores water, cell sap, nutrients, and waste products. It also maintains turgor pressure, which keeps the plant firm and upright. Without it, plants would wilt.
  • Q11. Write the chemical equation of photosynthesis.
    Ans: 6 CO₂ + 12 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ + 6 H₂O (in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll).
  • Q12. What is the role of root hairs?
    Ans: Root hairs are tiny thread-like extensions of root cells. They greatly increase the surface area of the root and absorb water and minerals from soil by the process of osmosis.
  • Q13. List four factors affecting photosynthesis.
    Ans: (i) Light intensity, (ii) Concentration of CO₂, (iii) Temperature (optimum 25–35°C), and (iv) Availability of water.
  • Q14. Why does a green tomato become red when ripe?
    Ans: When the tomato ripens, the green chloroplasts inside its cells convert into red chromoplasts containing the pigment lycopene. This change of plastid type causes the colour change.
  • Q15. What is transpiration pull?
    Ans: The suction force created in xylem when water evaporates from leaves through stomata. This pull draws water upward from the roots through the entire plant — much like sipping juice through a straw.
