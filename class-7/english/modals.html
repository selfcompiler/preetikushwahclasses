Modals

Special helping verbs that express ability, permission, possibility & obligation

💪 ✅ 🎯 ⚡ 💡
📚 What are Modals?

Modals (also called modal auxiliaries or modal verbs) are special helping verbs that are used with the main verb to express meanings such as ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice, willingness, and necessity.

Unlike regular verbs, modals do not change their form. They are always followed by the base form (bare infinitive) of the main verb.

✍️ Golden Rule:
Modal + Base Form of Verb (no -s, no -ing, no -ed, no to)

✅ She can swim.    ❌ She can swims.    ❌ She can to swim.    ❌ She can swimming.
📑 Complete List of Modals

🌟 Primary Modals

  • Can — ability, permission
  • Could — past ability, polite request
  • May — permission, possibility
  • Might — weak possibility
  • Must — obligation, certainty

⭐ More Modals

  • Shall — future, suggestion
  • Should — advice, expectation
  • Will — future, promise
  • Would — polite request, habits

💎 Semi-Modals

  • Ought to — moral duty
  • Need — necessity
  • Dare — courage, challenge
💡 Key Features of Modals:
  • They do not have -s form in third person: He can sing (NOT He cans sing).
  • They form negatives by adding not directly: She cannot come.
  • They form questions by inverting with the subject: Can she come?
  • They do not need do/does/did for questions or negatives.
  • Two modals cannot be used together: He will can come.
🔎 Each Modal in Detail
🟢 CAN Ability & Permission
Used for: present ability, informal permission, general possibility
  • Ability: I can swim very fast.
  • Ability: She can speak three languages.
  • Permission: Can I go to the washroom?
  • Permission: You can use my pen.
  • Possibility: Accidents can happen anywhere.
🔵 COULD Past Ability & Politeness
Used for: past ability, polite requests, possibility (less certain than can)
  • Past ability: I could run very fast when I was young.
  • Past ability: She could read before she turned five.
  • Polite request: Could you please help me?
  • Possibility: It could rain this evening.
  • Suggestion: We could go to the park after lunch.
🟣 MAY Formal Permission & Possibility
Used for: formal permission, possibility (likely)
  • Permission: May I come in, ma'am?
  • Permission: You may leave after the bell rings.
  • Possibility: It may rain today.
  • Possibility: He may come to the party.
  • Blessing/wish: May God bless you!
🔼 MIGHT Weak Possibility
Used for: uncertain/weak possibility, polite suggestion
  • Weak possibility: He might come tomorrow. (not sure)
  • Weak possibility: It might snow tonight.
  • Polite suggestion: You might want to check your answers again.
  • Uncertainty: She might not agree to the plan.
🔴 MUST Strong Obligation & Certainty
Used for: strong obligation/necessity, strong certainty/logical deduction
  • Obligation: You must wear your school uniform.
  • Obligation: Students must complete their homework on time.
  • Certainty: He has been working all day. He must be tired.
  • Certainty: She got full marks! She must be very happy.
  • Prohibition: You must not cheat in the exam.
🍀 SHOULD Advice & Expectation
Used for: advice, recommendation, expectation, moral duty
  • Advice: You should eat more fruits and vegetables.
  • Advice: You should not waste water.
  • Expectation: The train should arrive by 5 o'clock.
  • Duty: We should help the needy.
  • Recommendation: You should read this book. It is excellent!
🔮 SHALL Future & Suggestion
Used for: future with I/We, suggestions, offers, formal promises
  • Future: I shall go to school tomorrow.
  • Suggestion: Shall we go for a walk?
  • Offer: Shall I carry your bag?
  • Formal promise: We shall overcome this difficulty.
🌟 WILL Future & Willingness
Used for: future, promise, willingness, request, determination
  • Future: She will come to the party tonight.
  • Promise: I will help you with your homework.
  • Willingness: I will do it for you.
  • Request: Will you please open the window?
  • Determination: I will never give up!
💧 WOULD Polite Request & Habits
Used for: polite requests, past habits, hypothetical situations, preferences
  • Polite request: Would you mind closing the door?
  • Polite request: Would you like some tea?
  • Past habit: When I was a child, I would play in the garden every evening.
  • Hypothetical: If I had wings, I would fly to the moon.
  • Preference: I would rather stay home than go out.
♥️ OUGHT TO Moral Duty
Used for: moral obligation, duty, strong recommendation (similar to "should" but stronger moral sense)
  • Moral duty: You ought to respect your elders.
  • Moral duty: We ought to help the poor.
  • Recommendation: You ought to exercise daily.
  • Expectation: The weather ought to improve by tomorrow.

Note: "Ought to" is the only modal followed by to + base verb.

🔑 NEED Necessity
Used for: necessity (usually in negative & interrogative sentences as a modal)
  • Question: Need I come tomorrow?
  • Negative: You need not worry about the test.
  • Negative: She need not bring her books today.
  • Absence of necessity: You needn't wait for me.

Note: "Need" works as a modal only in negatives and questions. In affirmative sentences, it works as a main verb: "I need to go."

🔥 DARE Courage & Challenge
Used for: expressing courage, challenge, boldness (mainly negative & interrogative)
  • Challenge: How dare you speak to me like that!
  • Negative: He dare not go near the dog.
  • Question: Dare he enter the dark room alone?
  • Statement: I dare say he is right.

Note: Like "need," "dare" can be both a modal and a main verb. As a main verb: "He dares to challenge the champion."

🎯 Modals for Different Functions

Different modals can express the same function but with different levels of strength, politeness, or certainty. Here is a comparison:

🔓 Permission (increasing politeness)

Informal → Formal / Polite

Can → May → Could

Can I go? (informal)  |  May I go? (formal)  |  Could I go? (most polite)

💪 Ability

Present Ability: CAN   |   Past Ability: COULD

✅ I can solve this puzzle. (now)

✅ I could climb trees when I was a child. (past)

⚠️ Obligation (strongest to mildest)

Strong → Mild

Must → Ought to → Should

Must = compulsory  |  Ought to = moral duty  |  Should = advice/recommendation

🌈 Possibility (most to least likely)

More Likely → Less Likely

May → Might → Could

It may rain. (quite possible)  |  It might rain. (less certain)  |  It could rain. (remote chance)

❓ Negative & Question Forms
🔴 Negative Forms

To make a modal negative, simply add not after the modal. Many have short (contracted) forms:

ModalFull NegativeShort FormExample
Cancannotcan'tI can't swim.
Couldcould notcouldn'tShe couldn't come.
Maymay notmayn't (rare)You may not enter.
Mightmight notmightn'tHe might not agree.
Mustmust notmustn'tYou mustn't lie.
Shallshall notshan'tI shan't forget.
Shouldshould notshouldn'tYou shouldn't waste food.
Willwill notwon'tShe won't come.
Wouldwould notwouldn'tHe wouldn't listen.
Needneed notneedn'tYou needn't worry.
Daredare notdaren'tHe daren't complain.
⚠️ Important: "Cannot" is always written as one word. "Must not" (mustn't) means prohibition (you are not allowed), while "need not" (needn't) means no necessity (you don't have to).
❔ Question (Interrogative) Forms

To form a question, move the modal before the subject:

Structure: Modal + Subject + Base Verb + ...?

  • Can you help me?
  • Should I bring my notebook?
  • Must we finish this today?
  • Will she come to the party?
  • May I borrow your eraser?
  • Shall we start the game?
  • Would you like some juice?

Negative Questions (for emphasis / surprise)

  • Can't you see the board? (surprise)
  • Won't you stay for dinner? (persuasion)
  • Shouldn't we tell the teacher? (expecting agreement)
⚠️ Common Mistakes & Quick Tips
❌ Common Mistakes to Avoid
Mistake 1: Adding -s to the verb after a modal.
He can plays cricket. → He can play cricket.
Mistake 2: Using to after a modal (except "ought to").
She must to study hard. → She must study hard.
Mistake 3: Using two modals together.
He will can come tomorrow. → He will be able to come tomorrow.
Mistake 4: Using did with modals for past.
Did she could swim? → Could she swim?
Mistake 5: Confusing must not and need not.
"You must not cheat" = It is forbidden.
"You need not come" = It is not necessary.
Mistake 6: Writing "can not" as two words.
I can not do this. → I cannot do this. (OR: I can't do this.)
💡 Quick Tips to Remember
Tip 1: Use "may" (not "can") to ask permission from teachers and elders — it is more polite and formal.
Tip 2: "Must" shows personal obligation (I must study), while "have to" shows external obligation (I have to wear a uniform — it's the school rule).
Tip 3: Use "shall" with I and We for offers and suggestions: "Shall I help you?" "Shall we dance?"
Tip 4: "Would" is the most polite way to make requests: "Would you mind opening the window?"
Tip 5: Remember the possibility chain: May (likely) > Might (less likely) > Could (remote). Use this to score full marks in exams!
Tip 6: "Ought to" is the odd one out — it is the only modal followed by "to."

Exercise E: Correct the Errors

Each sentence has an error related to modals. Find and correct it.

1. She can sings very well.
2. He must to finish his work before going out.
3. They will can come to the party tomorrow.
4. We should to respect our elders.
5. Can I may go to the washroom?
6. She cans ride a horse.
7. Did you could solve the problem?
8. You must not to talk during the assembly.
  1. She can sings very well.
    She can sing very well. — No "-s" after a modal; use base form.
  2. He must to finish his work before going out.
    He must finish his work before going out. — No "to" after modals (except "ought to").
  3. They will can come to the party tomorrow.
    They will be able to come to the party tomorrow. — Two modals cannot be used together.
  4. We should to respect our elders.
    We should respect our elders. — No "to" after "should."
  5. Can I may go to the washroom?
    May I go to the washroom? — Two modals cannot be used together. Use "May" for polite permission.
  6. She cans ride a horse.
    She can ride a horse. — Modals do not take "-s" in third person.
  7. Did you could solve the problem?
    Could you solve the problem? — Modals do not use "did" for questions.
  8. You must not to talk during the assembly.
    You must not talk during the assembly. — No "to" after "must not."
