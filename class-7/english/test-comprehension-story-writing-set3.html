Passage for Sections A & B
Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, known as the “Missile Man of India,” was one of the most loved and respected Presidents our country has ever had. Born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, he came from a humble family. His father, Jainulabdeen, was a boat owner, and his mother, Ashiamma, was a homemaker. Despite their limited means, his parents always encouraged education.

As a young boy, Kalam sold newspapers to support his family’s income. He was a curious and hardworking student who dreamed of flying. He studied physics at St. Joseph’s College and later aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. He joined ISRO and then DRDO, where he played a key role in developing India’s first satellite launch vehicle (SLV-III) and the Agni and Prithvi missiles.

In 2002, he became the 11th President of India. Unlike other politicians, he loved spending time with children and students. He believed that young people had the power to transform India. His famous book, “Wings of Fire,” inspired millions of readers. He often said, “Dream is not that which you see while sleeping; it is something that does not let you sleep.”

Dr. Kalam passed away on 27 July 2015 while giving a lecture at IIM Shillong — doing what he loved most: teaching. He remains an eternal inspiration for every Indian who dares to dream big.

Q12.
Title: The Greedy Dog

Once upon a time, a stray dog was wandering through the village searching for food. After a long time, he found a juicy bone near a butcher’s shop. He grabbed the bone happily in his mouth and ran away.

On his way home, the dog had to cross a narrow wooden bridge over a river. As he looked down into the clear water, he saw his own reflection. But the foolish dog thought it was another dog carrying an even bigger and juicier bone. Greed filled his heart. He wanted that bone too!

Without thinking, the greedy dog opened his mouth wide and barked loudly at the reflection — “Woof! Woof!” The moment he opened his mouth, his own bone fell into the river with a splash and sank to the bottom. The dog stared at the water in shock. The other dog and the bigger bone had both vanished. He was left with nothing.

Moral: Greed leads to loss. Be happy with what you have.

Q15.
Title: The Old Farmer and His Sons

Once, there was an old farmer who had three sons. The sons always fought with each other over small things. The farmer was very worried about their future.

One day, the farmer called his sons and gave each of them a single stick. “Break it,” he said. Each son easily snapped his stick in half. Then the farmer tied three sticks together in a bundle and asked them to break it. They tried their best, but none of them could break the bundle.

The farmer smiled and said, “When you are alone, you are weak like a single stick. But when you stay together, no one can break you.” The three brothers understood the lesson and promised to always support each other.

Moral: Unity is strength.

Q17.
Title: The Star of Rampur

In a small village called Rampur, there lived a girl named Kavita. Her father was a vegetable seller, and her mother stitched clothes to earn a little money. They could barely afford school fees, but Kavita’s parents always said, “Education will change your life.”

Kavita studied under a streetlight every night because there was no electricity at home. She walked three kilometres to school every day, carrying her books in a cloth bag. While other children played after school, Kavita helped her mother with chores and then sat down to study again.

Her teachers noticed her dedication. They gave her extra books and helped her prepare for the district scholarship exam. Kavita studied harder than ever. When the results came, the whole village celebrated — Kavita had topped the district! She received a full scholarship to one of the best schools in the city.

Years later, Kavita became a doctor and opened a free clinic in Rampur. She never forgot where she came from.

Moral: Hard work, dedication, and the support of loved ones can turn even the biggest dreams into reality.
