Answer Key & Solutions
Q1. [1 Mark]
(a) on, at — ‘On’ for days, ‘at’ for clock time.
Q2. [1 Mark]
‘to’ is unnecessary. Correct: He insisted on going there. ‘There’ is an adverb and does not need a preposition before it.
Q3. [1 Mark]
for — ‘For’ is used with durations (the last five years). ‘Since’ would need a specific point: since 2021.
Q4. [1 Mark]
(b) on time — ‘On time’ means at the scheduled time. ‘In time’ means before the deadline / not too late.
Q5. [1 Mark]
of — ‘Capable of’ is the correct collocation.
Q6. [1 Mark]
(b) through — ‘Through’ means passing from one side to the other of an opening.
Q7. [2 Marks]
on time = punctual, at the scheduled time: The train arrived on time at 3 p.m. [1 mark]
in time = before it’s too late: We reached the station in time to catch the train. [1 mark]
Q8. [2 Marks]
(a) India got independence from the British in 1947. [1 mark]
(b) The school is named after Mahatma Gandhi. [1 mark]
Q9. [2 Marks]
(a) keen on (not ‘keen of’): The students are keen on learning new things. [1 mark]
(b) This is correct. ‘Differ with’ means to disagree with a person. ‘Differ from’ means to be unlike something. Both are valid but have different meanings. [1 mark]
Q10. [2 Marks]
We use ‘on’ for large vehicles where you can stand/walk (bus, train, plane, ship). We use ‘in’ for small vehicles where you sit enclosed (car, taxi, auto-rickshaw). [1 mark]
Examples: She is on the train. / He is sitting in the auto-rickshaw. [1 mark]
Q11. [2 Marks]
above: The helicopter hovered above the city. (higher than, not directly over) [1 mark]
over: She held an umbrella over her head. (directly above and often covering) [1 mark]
Q12. [3 Marks]
Prepositional phrases: On a sunny morning (modifies verb ‘sailed’, time); across the lake (modifies ‘sailed’, direction); in his wooden boat (modifies ‘sailed’, means); With great patience (modifies ‘waited’, manner); for a catch (modifies ‘waited’, purpose); until noon (modifies ‘waited’, time). [1 mark per 2 correct phrases + modifiers]
Q13. [3 Marks]
(a) She is superior to her rivals. — ‘Superior to’ is correct; Latin-origin comparatives use ‘to’, not ‘than’. [1 mark]
(b) He prevented me from going outside. — ‘Prevent from’ is the correct pattern. [1 mark]
(c) The boy jumped into the river from the bridge. — ‘Into’ shows movement entering the river. [1 mark]
Q14. [3 Marks]
(a) In spite of / Despite the heavy traffic, we reached the airport on time. [1 mark]
(b) The meeting was held with regard to / in connection with the new project. [1 mark]
(c) He left the class without the teacher’s permission. [1 mark]
Q15. [3 Marks]
Sample pairs [1 mark each]:
1. She looked at the painting. (observing) vs She looked after the children. (caring for)
2. He ran into the room. (entered) vs He ran out of the room. (exited)
3. The cat sat on the box. (surface) vs The cat sat in the box. (inside)
Q16. [5 Marks]
Sample: Dear Arjun, Last week I went to the Red Fort in Delhi with my family. We walked through the main gate into the beautiful garden. The fort was built by Shah Jahan during the Mughal period. We sat under a tree and had lunch. It was a wonderful experience.
[1 mark per 2 correct prepositions, max 5 for 8+ different prepositions]
Q17. [5 Marks]
(a) The flight departed from Delhi at 6 a.m. on a foggy morning. [2 marks]
(b) The passengers were seated inside / in the plane, looking through / out of the window at the clouds below / beneath them. [2 marks]
(c) After two hours, they landed in Mumbai on / ahead of schedule. [1 mark]
Q18. Bonus [2 Marks]
Examples: “Which city are you from?” (Moving: “From which city are you?” sounds stiff.) [1 mark]
“This is the book I was looking for.” (Moving: “This is the book for which I was looking” sounds overly formal.) [1 mark]
Ending with a preposition is natural in everyday English, especially in questions and relative clauses.
