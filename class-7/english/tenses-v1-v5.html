💡 Quick Recap: The Five Verb Forms

Every English verb has five forms. We label them V1, V2, V3, V4 and V5. Each tense uses one or more of these forms.
LabelNameHow It LooksExample (write)
V1Base form / InfinitiveThe dictionary formwrite
V2Past SimpleV1 + -ed (regular) or irregularwrote
V3Past ParticipleV1 + -ed (regular) or irregularwritten
V4Present Participle / -ing formV1 + -ingwriting
V5Third-person Singular PresentV1 + -s / -es / -ieswrites

✅ Present Tenses

✅ Simple Present Tense

The Simple Present Tense describes habits, routines, facts, and general truths. It uses V1 or V5.

Structure (using V1 & V5)

I / You / We / They + V1  |  He / She / It + V5
⚠️
V5 = V1 + s/es/ies. We use V5 ONLY with third-person singular subjects (he, she, it, a name, a thing). All other subjects use V1.

V1 vs V5 — See the Difference

SubjectVerb FormExample
IV1 playI play cricket.
YouV1 playYou play cricket.
WeV1 playWe play cricket.
TheyV1 playThey play cricket.
HeV5 playsHe plays cricket.
SheV5 playsShe plays cricket.
ItV5 rainsIt rains heavily.
RamV5 readsRam reads daily.

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+ (I/you/we/they)S + V1They play cricket.
+ (he/she/it)S + V5He plays cricket.
− (I/you/we/they)S + do not + V1They do not play cricket.
− (he/she/it)S + does not + V1He does not play cricket.
? (I/you/we/they)Do + S + V1?Do they play?
? (he/she/it)Does + S + V1?Does he play?
⚠️
Important! In negatives and questions with does/do, always use V1, NEVER V5. "Does he plays?" is WRONG. Correct: "Does he play (V1)?"

When Do We Use It?

✔ Habits & routines

I brush (V1) my teeth every morning.

✔ General facts & universal truths

The sun rises (V5) in the east.

✔ Timetables & schedules

The train leaves (V5) at 6 PM.

✔ Permanent situations

She lives (V5) in Mumbai.

Signal Words

alwaysusuallyoftensometimesneverevery daygenerallyrarely

Examples (10 Sentences)

  • She drinks (V5) milk every morning.
  • We go (V1) to school by bus.
  • The Earth revolves (V5) around the Sun.
  • My father reads (V5) the newspaper daily.
  • They do not watch (do not + V1) TV on weekdays.
  • Does she speak (V1) French?
  • Birds fly (V1) south in winter.
  • Water boils (V5) at 100°C.
  • I love (V1) chocolate ice cream.
  • He always carries (V5) an umbrella.

⏰ Present Continuous Tense

The Present Continuous Tense describes actions happening right now or around the current time. It uses V4 (the -ing form).

Structure (using V4)

Subject + is / am / are + V4
🛠
I → am  |  He/She/It → is  |  We/You/They → are  |  Then add V4

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + is/am/are + V4She is playing (V4).
−S + is/am/are + not + V4She is not playing (V4).
?Is/Am/Are + S + V4?Is she playing (V4)?

When Do We Use It?

UsageExample
Actions happening right nowLook! The baby is crying (V4).
Temporary situationsMy uncle is staying (V4) with us this week.
Future plans (arranged)We are going (V4) to Delhi tomorrow.
Changing situationsThe weather is getting (V4) warmer.
Annoying habits (with "always")He is always losing (V4) his keys!

Signal Words

nowright nowat the momentcurrentlytodaythis weeklook!listen!

Examples

  • I am studying (V4) for the exam right now.
  • She is cooking (V4) dinner at the moment.
  • They are playing (V4) cricket in the park.
  • The sun is shining (V4) brightly today.
  • Look! The cat is climbing (V4) the tree.
  • Listen! Someone is singing (V4) beautifully.
  • I am not watching (V4) TV right now.
  • Are you coming (V4) to the party tonight?

🌟 Present Perfect Tense

The Present Perfect Tense connects the past to the present. It describes an action completed at some time before now. It uses V3 (past participle).

Structure (using V3)

Subject + has / have + V3
🔑
Has with He/She/It  |  Have with I/We/You/They  |  Then add V3

Since vs For

🔼 SINCE

= from a specific POINT in time

  • since Monday
  • since 2019
  • since January
  • since morning
  • since last summer

⏲️ FOR

= for a PERIOD / DURATION

  • for three days
  • for five years
  • for two hours
  • for a long time
  • for six months

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + has/have + V3She has written (V3) a letter.
−S + has/have + not + V3She hasn't written (V3) a letter.
?Has/Have + S + V3?Has she written (V3) a letter?

Signal Words

justalreadyyeteverneversinceforrecentlyso farlately

Examples

  • I have finished (V3) my homework.
  • She has already eaten (V3) lunch.
  • They have not seen (V3) this movie yet.
  • Have you ever visited (V3) Jaipur?
  • He has lived (V3) in Mumbai since 2018.
  • We have known (V3) each other for five years.
  • I have never ridden (V3) a horse.
  • She has just left (V3) the office.

📚 Past Tenses

📚 Simple Past Tense

The Simple Past Tense describes actions that started and finished at a specific time in the past. It uses V2 (past form).

Structure (using V2)

Subject + V2

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + V2She walked (V2) to school.
−S + did not + V1She did not walk (V1) to school.
?Did + S + V1?Did she walk (V1) to school?
⚠️
Important! In negatives and questions, use did + V1, NOT V2. "She did not walked (V2)" is WRONG. Correct: "She did not walk (V1)."

When Do We Use It?

✔ Completed actions in the past

She visited (V2) her grandmother last Sunday.

✔ A series of completed actions

He woke (V2) up, brushed (V2) his teeth, and ate (V2) breakfast.

✔ Past habits (now stopped)

When I was young, I played (V2) in the park every evening.

Signal Words

yesterdaylast weeklast monthlast yearagoin 2020the other dayonce upon a time

Examples

  • I watched (V2) a movie yesterday.
  • They went (V2) to Jaipur last summer.
  • She wrote (V2) a beautiful poem.
  • We did not play (did not + V1) outside because it rained.
  • Did you finish (V1) your homework?
  • The dog chased (V2) the cat around the garden.
  • My grandmother told (V2) us a wonderful story.
  • He bought (V2) a new bicycle two days ago.

⏪ Past Continuous Tense

The Past Continuous Tense describes actions that were ongoing at a specific time in the past. It uses V4 (the -ing form).

Structure (using V4)

Subject + was / were + V4
🛠
I/He/She/It → was  |  We/You/They → were  |  Then add V4

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + was/were + V4She was playing (V4).
−S + was/were + not + V4She was not playing (V4).
?Was/Were + S + V4?Was she playing (V4)?

When Do We Use It?

UsageExample
Ongoing action at a specific past timeAt 8 PM, I was watching (V4) a movie.
Action interrupted by anotherI was sleeping (V4) when the phone rang (V2).
Two simultaneous past actionsShe was cooking (V4) while he was reading (V4).
Setting background in a storyThe birds were singing (V4) and the sun was shining (V4).

Signal Words

whilewhenat that timeall day yesterdayat 5 o'clockas

Examples

  • I was reading (V4) a novel at 9 PM last night.
  • The children were playing (V4) in the garden all afternoon.
  • She was writing (V4) a letter when her friend arrived (V2).
  • We were having (V4) dinner when the lights went (V2) off.
  • It was raining (V4) heavily all day yesterday.
  • They were not listening (V4) to the teacher.
  • Were you sleeping (V4) when I called?
  • Mom was cooking (V4) while Dad was washing (V4) the car.

✅ Past Perfect Tense

The Past Perfect Tense describes an action completed BEFORE another past action. It uses V3 (past participle). Think of it as "the past of the past."

Structure (using V3)

Subject + had + V3

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + had + V3He had finished (V3) dinner.
−S + had not + V3He hadn't finished (V3) dinner.
?Had + S + V3?Had he finished (V3) dinner?
💡
When two things happened in the past, the EARLIER action = Past Perfect (had + V3), the LATER action = Simple Past (V2).

Signal Words

beforeafteralreadyby the timewhenuntilas soon asnever...before

Examples

  • He had left (V3) before I arrived (V2).
  • By the time the teacher came (V2), students had already taken (V3) their seats.
  • She had never seen (V3) snow before she visited (V2) Kashmir.
  • After he had finished (V3) homework, he went (V2) out to play.
  • The train had departed (V3) when we reached (V2) the station.
  • I had not eaten (V3) anything since morning.
  • They had completed (V3) the project before the deadline.
  • She told (V2) me that she had read (V3) the book twice.

🌟 Future Tenses

🌟 Simple Future Tense

The Simple Future Tense describes actions that will happen later. It uses V1 (base form) after will/shall.

Structure (using V1)

Subject + will / shall + V1

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + will + V1She will visit (V1) us tomorrow.
−S + will not + V1She won't visit (V1) us tomorrow.
?Will + S + V1?Will she visit (V1) us tomorrow?

When Do We Use It?

✔ Predictions

It will rain (V1) tomorrow.

✔ Promises

I will help (V1) you with your project.

✔ Instant decisions

"The phone is ringing." — "I will answer (V1) it."

✔ Future facts

The exam will start (V1) at 10 AM next Monday.

Signal Words

tomorrownext weeknext monthnext yearsoonin the futurelater

Examples

  • I will study (V1) hard for the exam.
  • We shall go (V1) to the park this evening.
  • She will cook (V1) dinner tonight.
  • They will not come (V1) to the party.
  • Will you help (V1) me with this problem?
  • The school will reopen (V1) next Monday.
  • It will be (V1) sunny tomorrow.
  • Shall we begin (V1) the meeting?

🚀 Future Continuous Tense

The Future Continuous Tense describes actions that will be ongoing at a specific future time. It uses V4 (the -ing form).

Structure (using V4)

Subject + will be + V4

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + will be + V4She will be playing (V4).
−S + will not be + V4She will not be playing (V4).
?Will + S + be + V4?Will she be playing (V4)?

When Do We Use It?

UsageExample
Ongoing at a specific future timeAt 10 AM tomorrow, I will be taking (V4) an exam.
Polite enquiriesWill you be using (V4) the car tonight?
Parallel future actionsWhile I will be studying (V4), she will be painting (V4).

Signal Words

at this time tomorrowby thenall day tomorrowthis time next weekat 8 PM tonight
💡
"will be" stays the same for ALL subjects! No need to worry about was/were or is/am/are. Just add V4.

Examples

  • I will be studying (V4) at this time tomorrow.
  • She will be travelling (V4) to Mumbai next Monday.
  • At 9 PM tonight, we will be watching (V4) the match.
  • This time next year, he will be attending (V4) college.
  • I will not be working (V4) on Sunday.
  • Will you be joining (V4) us for lunch tomorrow?
  • Don't call at 3 PM — I will be sleeping (V4).
  • By this time next week, they will be flying (V4) to London.

✅ Future Perfect Tense

The Future Perfect Tense describes an action that will be completed BEFORE a specific future time. It uses V3 (past participle).

Structure (using V3)

Subject + will have + V3

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + will have + V3She will have completed (V3) the work.
−S + will not have + V3She won't have completed (V3) the work.
?Will + S + have + V3?Will she have completed (V3) the work?

Signal Words

by tomorrowby next weekby next yearby the timebeforeby then

Examples

  • By next Monday, I will have submitted (V3) my project.
  • She will have reached (V3) Delhi by evening.
  • By the time you wake up, I will have cooked (V3) breakfast.
  • They will have built (V3) the bridge by December.
  • We will not have finished (V3) the exam before 3 PM.
  • Will you have learnt (V3) all chapters by Friday?
  • By next year, he will have worked (V3) here for ten years.
  • By the time guests arrive, Mom will have prepared (V3) food.
