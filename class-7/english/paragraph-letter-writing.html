✍️ Section 1: Paragraph Writing

📖 What is a Paragraph?

A paragraph is a group of related sentences that develop one single idea or topic. It is the basic building block of all writing — essays, letters, stories, and reports.

Every well-written paragraph has three parts:

🎯 Topic Sentence Introduces the main idea of the paragraph. It tells the reader what the paragraph is about.
📚 Supporting Sentences (3-5) Give details, examples, reasons, or facts that explain or support the topic sentence.
🏁 Concluding Sentence Wraps up the paragraph. It restates the main idea or gives a final thought.

💡 Tips for a Good Paragraph

🎯 Unity

All sentences should talk about one topic only. Do not mix different ideas.

🔗 Coherence

Sentences should flow smoothly. Use linking words to connect ideas logically.

📏 Order

Arrange ideas in a logical order — chronological, importance, or cause and effect.

📝 Completeness

Include enough details to fully explain the topic. Don't leave the reader confused!

🔗 Useful Linking Words

These words connect your sentences and make your paragraph smooth:

Firstly Secondly Moreover However In addition Furthermore On the other hand For example As a result Therefore Finally In conclusion Thus Meanwhile Consequently

📰 Model Paragraph 1: My Favourite Festival

🎈 My Favourite Festival

My favourite festival is Diwali, the festival of lights.Topic Sentence It is celebrated in October or November every year. Firstly, our whole family cleans and decorates the house with colourful rangoli and diyas. Moreover, we wear new clothes and visit relatives to exchange sweets and gifts.Supporting The best part is bursting crackers with my friends in the evening, while the sky lights up with fireworks. However, nowadays many people celebrate an eco-friendly Diwali by using fewer crackers to protect the environment. In conclusion, Diwali brings joy, togetherness, and a spirit of sharing, which is why it is my favourite festival.Concluding

📰 Model Paragraph 2: Importance of Trees

🌳 Importance of Trees

Trees are essential for the survival of life on Earth.Topic Sentence Firstly, they give us oxygen, which every living being needs to breathe. In addition, they absorb harmful carbon dioxide and help reduce air pollution.Supporting Trees also provide us with fruits, wood, medicines, and shade. Furthermore, their roots hold the soil together and prevent floods and soil erosion. For example, mangrove trees along coastlines protect villages from storms and cyclones. Therefore, we should plant more trees and protect our forests so that future generations can also enjoy a healthy environment.Concluding

💌 Section 2: Informal Letter

💬 When Do We Write Informal Letters?

We write informal (personal/friendly) letters to people we know well — friends, family members, cousins, and relatives. The tone is warm, friendly, and conversational.

📋 Format of an Informal Letter

Sender's Address
42, Green Park Colony
Indore, M.P. — 452001
Date 10 June 2026
Salutation Dear Rahul,
Body — Para 1 (Ask about well-being / reason for writing)
Body — Para 2 (Main content / details / news)
Body — Para 3 (Closing thoughts / request / invitation)
Closing Yours lovingly, / Your friend, / With love,
Name Priya
💡 Remember: In informal letters, the receiver's address is NOT written. There is no subject line. The language is warm and personal!

📧 Model Letter 1: To a Friend About Summer Vacation

🌴 Letter to a Friend About Summer Vacation
15, Nehru Nagar
Bhopal, M.P. — 462001
5 May 2026

Dear Ankit,

I hope this letter finds you in good health and high spirits. I am writing to tell you about the wonderful time I had during my summer vacation.

This year, my family and I visited Shimla for ten days. The weather was cool and pleasant. We stayed in a cosy hotel near the Mall Road. We visited the Ridge, Jakhoo Temple, and Kufri. I especially enjoyed horse riding in Kufri and the toy train ride from Kalka to Shimla. We also tasted delicious local food, especially the hot momos and apple pie.

I wish you had been there with me! Do write back and tell me about your vacation plans. Give my regards to your parents.

Your friend,
Priya

💼 Section 3: Formal Letter

🏢 When Do We Write Formal Letters?

We write formal (official/business) letters to people we do not know personally or to people in authority — the principal, headmaster, editor of a newspaper, government officials, company managers, etc. The tone is polite, respectful, and to the point.

📋 Format of a Formal Letter

Sender's Address
42, Green Park Colony
Indore, M.P. — 452001
Date 10 June 2026
Receiver's Address
The Principal
Delhi Public School
Indore, M.P. — 452001
Subject Application for three days' leave
Salutation Respected Sir/Madam,
Body — Para 1 (Introduction — state the purpose)
Body — Para 2 (Details — explain reasons/facts)
Body — Para 3 (Conclusion — request/hope/thanking)
Closing Yours faithfully, / Yours obediently, / Yours sincerely,
Name & Details Rohan Sharma
Class VII-B, Roll No. 25
💡 Key Differences from Informal:
  • Receiver's address is written (below the date, on the left).
  • A Subject line is mandatory.
  • Use "Yours faithfully" if you begin with "Sir/Madam", or "Yours sincerely" if you use the person's name.
  • Language must be polite, formal, and concise.

📧 Model Letter 2: To the Editor About Traffic Problems

🚗 Letter to the Editor About Traffic Problems
22, Scheme No. 78
Indore, M.P. — 452010

25 August 2026

The Editor
The Hindustan Times
New Delhi — 110001

Subject: Growing traffic congestion in Indore city

Respected Sir/Madam,

Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the concerned authorities to the growing traffic problems in Indore city, especially in the Vijay Nagar and Palasia areas.

Every morning during school hours and every evening during office closing time, the roads become extremely congested. Vehicles are parked haphazardly on both sides of the road, leaving very little space for moving traffic. Auto-rickshaws and buses stop anywhere they wish to pick up and drop passengers. There are very few traffic signals, and those that exist are often not working. As a result, accidents have become common, and even school children are at risk while crossing the roads.

I request the traffic police and the municipal corporation to take strict action against illegal parking, install more traffic signals, and deploy traffic wardens near schools and busy intersections. I hope this letter brings the issue to the notice of the authorities.

Yours faithfully,
Sneha Verma

⚖️ Section 4: Informal vs Formal — Comparison

Understanding the difference between informal and formal letters is very important. Here is a clear side-by-side comparison:

Feature 💌 Informal Letter 💼 Formal Letter
Written to Friends, family, relatives Principal, editor, officials, strangers
Tone Warm, friendly, personal Polite, respectful, professional
Sender's Address Top right side Top left side
Receiver's Address Not written Written below the date (left side)
Subject Line Not needed Mandatory (written before salutation)
Salutation Dear Rahul, / Dear Papa, / Hi Meera, Respected Sir/Madam, / Dear Mr. Sharma,
Language Casual, conversational, contractions okay Formal, no slang, no contractions
Closing Yours lovingly, / Your friend, / With love, Yours faithfully, / Yours sincerely, / Yours obediently,
Examples Thanking a friend, sharing holiday news, invitation to birthday party Leave application, complaint to editor, requesting information
📖 Quick Rule: Use "Yours faithfully" when salutation is "Sir/Madam" (you don't know the name). Use "Yours sincerely" when you use the person's name (Dear Mr. Sharma). Use "Yours obediently" when writing to the principal or headmaster as a student.
📣 Section 5: Notice Writing (Bonus)

📄 What is a Notice?

A notice is a short, formal piece of writing that gives information or announcements to a group of people. It is put up on a notice board in schools, offices, or public places.

📋 Format of a Notice

1. Name of Organisation

School name or club name at the top, centred.

2. Heading: "NOTICE"

Written in bold, capital letters, centred.

3. Date

Written below the heading.

4. Title / Subject

A short title telling what the notice is about.

5. Body

Short and clear — What? When? Where? Who? Keep it within 50 words.

6. Authority Name & Designation

Name and post of the person issuing the notice.

📰 Model Notice

DELHI PUBLIC SCHOOL, INDORE

NOTICE

Date: 15 September 2026

INTER-HOUSE ESSAY WRITING COMPETITION

All students of Classes VII to IX are hereby informed that an Inter-House Essay Writing Competition will be held on 22 September 2026 (Thursday) in the School Auditorium from 10:00 a.m. to 12:00 noon.

The topic of the essay will be announced on the spot. Students must bring their own stationery. Interested students should give their names to their respective class teachers by 18 September 2026.

Aisha Khan
Head Girl

💡 Tips for Notice Writing: Keep it short (under 50 words for the body). Always answer the 5 Ws: What, When, Where, Who, Why. Use a formal tone. Put it in a box or border for the exam!
💡 Section 6: Quick Tips, Dos & Don'ts

✅ DO

  • Follow the correct format strictly (marks depend on it!)
  • Write in neat paragraphs (not one big block)
  • Use linking words for smooth flow
  • Keep the word limit in mind (100-150 words for paragraphs, 120-180 for letters)
  • Use polite language in formal letters
  • Always write the date in full (10 June 2026, not 10/6/26)
  • Indent the first line of each paragraph
  • Proofread your writing for spelling and grammar mistakes
  • End with a clear closing

❌ DON'T

  • Don't mix up informal and formal formats
  • Don't use slang or text language (lol, brb, btw) in letters
  • Don't write very long paragraphs (aim for 5-8 sentences each)
  • Don't forget the subject line in formal letters
  • Don't write the receiver's address in informal letters
  • Don't use "Dear Sir" with "Yours sincerely" (use "Yours faithfully" instead)
  • Don't write addresses in the body of the letter
  • Don't leave the letter without a proper ending
  • Don't use pencil or red ink in exams
🎯 Exam Tip — Marks Distribution: In most CBSE exams, writing tasks carry 5-8 marks. Typically: Format = 1-2 marks, Content = 2-3 marks, Expression/Language = 1-2 marks. So never skip the format!
📝 Section 7: Exercises

✍️ Exercise A: Write a Paragraph (100-120 words each)

Choose any one topic and write a well-structured paragraph with a topic sentence, supporting sentences, and a concluding sentence. Use linking words!

  1. My Best Friend
    Hints: who, when you met, qualities, what you do together, why special
  2. The Importance of Reading Books
    Hints: knowledge, imagination, vocabulary, relaxation, habit since childhood
  3. A Visit to a Historical Place
    Hints: which place, when, with whom, what you saw, feelings, what you learnt
✍️ Exercise A — Model Paragraphs

1. My Best Friend

My best friend Arjun is the kindest person I know. We have been friends since Class 3, when he joined our school as a new student and I was asked to show him around. Since then, we have been inseparable. Arjun is extremely helpful and always shares his notes with classmates who miss school. Moreover, he has a wonderful sense of humour and can make anyone laugh, even on a bad day. We love playing cricket together during recess and often visit each other's homes on weekends to play video games or study for exams. What makes him truly special is that he always stands up for his friends and never speaks ill of anyone. In short, Arjun is not just my best friend but more like a brother to me.

2. The Importance of Reading Books

Reading books is one of the best habits a person can develop. Firstly, books are a treasure house of knowledge and information. Whether it is science, history, or geography, books help us learn about the world around us. In addition, reading improves our vocabulary and language skills, making us better writers and communicators. Furthermore, story books and novels spark our imagination and take us to magical worlds that we could never visit in real life. Reading is also a wonderful way to relax after a tiring day. For example, many doctors recommend reading before bedtime as it helps calm the mind and improves sleep quality. Therefore, we should develop the habit of reading from an early age, as it enriches our minds and opens the doors to endless possibilities.

3. A Visit to a Historical Place

Last winter, I had the wonderful opportunity to visit the Taj Mahal in Agra. I went there with my family during the Christmas holidays. The moment I saw the magnificent white marble monument, I was completely mesmerised by its beauty. Firstly, the intricate carvings and inlay work on the walls were breathtaking. Moreover, our guide told us fascinating stories about Emperor Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal, and how it took 22 years and 20,000 workers to build this wonder. We also visited the beautiful gardens surrounding the Taj and the nearby Agra Fort. The most magical moment was watching the Taj Mahal glow in the golden light of the setting sun. In conclusion, this visit taught me about India's rich history and artistic heritage, and I feel proud that one of the Seven Wonders of the World is in our country.

🔎 Exercise E — Format Error Corrections

Letter 1 (Formal) — Errors Found:

  1. Sender's address is missing. In a formal letter, the sender's address must appear at the top left.
  2. Date is missing. The date must be written below the sender's address.
  3. Receiver's address is placed on the right. It should be on the left side, below the date.
  4. Subject line is missing. Every formal letter must have a subject line.
  5. Salutation is wrong. "Hi Sir" is too casual. It should be "Respected Sir/Madam,"
  6. Language is informal. Words like "coz", "I'll", "Thanks a lot!" are too casual for a formal letter. Should use "because", "I shall", "I shall be grateful".
  7. Closing is wrong. "Bye" is informal. Should be "Yours obediently," or "Yours faithfully,"
  8. Body is too short and lacks structure. Should have 3 proper paragraphs.

Letter 2 (Informal) — Errors Found:

  1. Receiver's address is written. Informal letters do NOT have the receiver's address. Remove it.
  2. Sender's address is missing. The sender's address should be written at the top right.
  3. Date is missing. The date must appear below the sender's address.
  4. Subject line is written. Informal letters do NOT have a subject line. Remove it.
  5. Salutation is too formal. "Respected Ankit" sounds formal. Should be "Dear Ankit," or "Hi Ankit,"
  6. Language is too formal. "I am writing to inform you" sounds stiff. Should be more friendly and natural.
  7. Closing is wrong. "Yours faithfully" is used in formal letters. Should be "Your friend," or "Yours lovingly,"
  8. Body is too short. Should have at least 2-3 paragraphs with warm, personal details.
