Answer Key & Detailed Solutions
Q1. [1 Mark]
(b) has/have + V3 — Present Perfect Tense uses the past participle (V3). Example: She has written (V3) a poem. V3 of “write” is “written”.
Q2. [1 Mark]
(b) Past Continuous — The formula “Subject + was/were + V4” is Past Continuous Tense. V4 is the -ing form. Example: She was reading (V4) a book.
Q3. [1 Mark]
(d) V5 — In Simple Present, third-person singular subjects (he/she/it) take V5 (verb + s/es). Example: He goes (V5) to school. Other subjects use V1 (base form).
Q4. [1 Mark]
(b) Future Perfect — The formula “Subject + will/shall + have + V3” is Future Perfect Tense. Example: She will have completed (V3) the work by evening.
Q5. [1 Mark]
(c) writing — V4 is the present participle or -ing form of the verb. Write → V1: write, V2: wrote, V3: written, V4: writing, V5: writes.
Q6. [2 Marks]
Ravi has written a letter to his grandmother.
V-form change: “wrote” (V2) changes to “has written” (has + V3). [1 mark for correct sentence, 1 mark for identifying V2 → V3 change]

Q10. [2 Marks]
(a) She taught English every day. — V5 (teaches) changes to V2 (taught). [1 mark]
(b) They have visited Agra. — V1 (visit, from “will visit”) changes to have + V3 (visited). The time expression “next month” is removed as it conflicts with Present Perfect. [1 mark]
Q11. [2 Marks]
V2 (Past Simple form) is used in Simple Past Tense — it shows a completed action in the past. It stands alone without a helping verb.
Example: go → went (V2): She went to school yesterday.

V3 (Past Participle form) is used with helping verbs (has/have/had) in Perfect Tenses and in Passive Voice.
Example: go → gone (V3): She has gone to school.

Another example: eat → ate (V2) vs eat → eaten (V3). “I ate lunch” (Simple Past) vs “I have eaten lunch” (Present Perfect).
[1 mark for correct explanation, 1 mark for correct examples]
Q12. [2 Marks]
Verb: speak (V1: speak, V2: spoke, V3: spoken, V4: speaking, V5: speaks)

(a) Simple Present (he/she): He speaks (V5)
(b) Simple Past: He spoke (V2)
(c) Present Continuous: He is speaking (is + V4)
(d) Present Perfect: He has spoken (has + V3)
(e) Past Continuous: He was speaking (was + V4)
(f) Past Perfect: He had spoken (had + V3)
(g) Simple Future: He will speak (will + V1)
(h) Future Continuous: He will be speaking (will be + V4)
(i) Future Perfect: He will have spoken (will have + V3)
[¼ mark each; full 2 marks for 8+ correct]
Q13. [2 Marks]
(a) Error: “has went” — “went” is V2 (Simple Past form). With “has” we need V3 (Past Participle).
Correct: She has gone (V3) to the market. [1 mark]

(b) Error: “was play” — Two errors: (i) “They” needs “were” not “was”, (ii) “play” is V1 but Past Continuous needs V4 (-ing form).
Correct: They were playing (V4) football yesterday evening. [1 mark]
Q14. [2 Marks]
Simple Present uses V1 (for I/we/you/they) or V5 (for he/she/it). It describes habitual actions, general truths, or fixed schedules.
Example: She reads (V5) the newspaper every morning.

Present Continuous uses is/am/are + V4 (-ing form). It describes actions happening right now or around the current time.
Example: She is reading (V4) a storybook right now.

Key difference: V1/V5 shows routine/permanent actions; V4 (with helping verb) shows temporary/ongoing actions. [1 mark for explanation, 1 mark for examples]
Q15. [2 Marks]
(i) has finished — Present Perfect Tense; V-form: has + V3 (finished). [½ mark]
(ii) is watching — Present Continuous Tense; V-form: is + V4 (watching). [½ mark]
(iii) played — Simple Past Tense; V-form: V2 (played). [½ mark]
(iv) will go — Simple Future Tense; V-form: will + V1 (go). [½ mark]
