Answer Key & Solutions
Q1. [1 Mark]
(b) She became a famous singer. — ‘Became’ is a linking verb; it connects the subject ‘She’ to the complement ‘a famous singer’ without showing action.
Q2. [1 Mark]
Seeing — It is a present participle used as a non-finite verb (participle). ‘Running’ is also a non-finite verb (gerund after ‘started’). Accept either.
Q3. [1 Mark]
True. Cut → cut → cut. It is an irregular verb whose form does not change across the three tenses.
Q4. [1 Mark]
(b) rains — In a conditional sentence (Type 1: if + present simple, will + base verb), the ‘if’ clause uses simple present, not future tense.
Q5. [1 Mark]
Modal verb (also called modal auxiliary) — ‘Must’ expresses obligation or necessity.
Q6. [1 Mark]
bitten — bite → bit → bitten.
Q7. [2 Marks]
Finite verb: Changes form according to the subject and tense. Example: She writes a letter. (‘writes’ changes with subject: I write, she writes) [1 mark]
Non-finite verb: Does not change with subject or tense. Example: She likes to write letters. (‘to write’ stays the same regardless of subject) [1 mark]
Q8. [2 Marks]
(a) flows — Intransitive (I); the river flows but there is no object. [1 mark]
(b) knitted — Transitive (T); it takes the object ‘sweater’ (knitted what? — a sweater). [1 mark]
Q9. [2 Marks]
(a) The concert had begun by the time we arrived. (past perfect — the concert started before we arrived) [1 mark]
(b) She has been practising her dance for two hours now. (present perfect continuous — action started in the past and is still going on) [1 mark]
Q10. [2 Marks]
(a) Main verb — ‘Have’ means ‘to possess/own’; it shows the action of owning a garden. [1 mark]
(b) Helping verb — ‘Have’ assists ‘planted’ to form the present perfect tense. [1 mark]
Q11. [2 Marks]
(a) fly → flew → flown [1 mark]
(b) choose → chose → chosen [1 mark]
Q12. [3 Marks]
(a) The results were announced by the Principal on the loudspeaker. [1 mark]
(b) The house is being decorated by my sister for Diwali. [1 mark]
(c) A new bridge will be built over the Narmada by the government. [1 mark]
Q13. [3 Marks]
(a) She has gone to the library after school. — With ‘has’, use the past participle ‘gone’, not past tense ‘went’. [1 mark]
(b) The mangoes were ripe and sweet. — ‘Mangoes’ is plural, so use ‘were’ instead of ‘was’. [1 mark]
(c) I know the answer to this question. — ‘Know’ is a stative verb (expresses a state, not an action). Stative verbs are not used in the continuous tense. [1 mark]
Q14. [3 Marks]
(a) You must wear the school uniform on all working days. — Expresses obligation. [1 mark]
(b) Could / Can you help me carry these books? — Expresses polite request. [1 mark]
(c) It may / might rain this evening; the sky looks cloudy. — Expresses possibility. [1 mark]
Q15. [3 Marks]
(a) Swimming — gerund (used as the subject of the sentence) [½ mark]
staying — gerund (object of the preposition ‘for’) [½ mark]
(b) sitting — participle (present participle modifying ‘boy’) [½ mark]
to read — infinitive (object of ‘wants’) [½ mark]
[1 mark for correct identification of all four non-finite verbs]
Q16. [5 Marks]
(a) Verbs: visited, had prepared, eating, was playing, were laughing, running, promised, visit [2 marks]
(b) Main verbs: visited, prepared, eating, playing, laughing, running, promised, visit. Helping verbs: had, was, were. [2 marks]
(c) Transitive: visited (visited whom? — grandparents) or prepared (prepared what? — paratha). Intransitive: laughing or running (no object needed). [1 mark]
Q17. [5 Marks]
“Kavita woke up early every morning. She brushed her teeth and took a bath. Then she ate her breakfast. Her father dropped her at school. She studied hard and played with her friends during the lunch break. In the evening, she read her favourite storybook.”

Irregular verbs (underline any two): woke (wake → woke), took (take → took), ate (eat → ate), read (read → read, pronounced ‘red’).
[3 marks for correct changes, 2 marks for identifying irregular verbs]
Q18. Bonus [2 Marks]
In “I am having a car,” the verb ‘have’ is used as a stative verb meaning ‘to possess/own’. [1 mark]
Stative verbs express a state of being, not an ongoing action. They are not used in the continuous (-ing) tense. The correct form is: “I have a car.” However, ‘have’ CAN be used in continuous tense when it means ‘to eat/experience’ (e.g., “I am having lunch” is correct). [1 mark]
