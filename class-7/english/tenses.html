Complete Tenses

Master all 9 English Tenses — Present, Past & Future

✅ Present 📚 Past 🌟 Future

💡 What Are Tenses?

Tense is the form of a verb that shows the time of an action. It tells us when something happens — in the present, past, or future.

There are three time periods and three types, making 9 tenses in total:

Type✅ Present📚 Past🌟 Future
SimpleI playI playedI will play
ContinuousI am playingI was playingI will be playing
PerfectI have playedI had playedI will have played
💡
Simple = fact/completed action. Continuous = action in progress. Perfect = action completed before a certain time.

✅ Present Tenses

✅ Simple Present Tense

The Simple Present Tense describes actions that happen regularly, always, or are general facts. It uses the base form (V1).

Structure

Subject + V1 / V1 + s/es

Rules for -s / -es / -ies

RuleWhenExamples
Add -sMost verbs (with he/she/it)play → plays, read → reads
Add -esVerbs ending in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -owatch → watches, go → goes
-y to -iesConsonant + ycarry → carries, study → studies
Keep -ysVowel + yplay → plays, enjoy → enjoys

When Do We Use It?

✔ Habits & routines

I brush my teeth every morning.

✔ General facts & universal truths

The sun rises in the east.

✔ Timetables & schedules

The train leaves at 6 PM.

✔ Permanent situations

She lives in Mumbai.

Signal Words

alwaysusuallyoftensometimesneverevery daygenerallyrarely

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+ (I/you/we/they)S + V1They play cricket.
+ (he/she/it)S + V1+s/esHe plays cricket.
− (I/you/we/they)S + do not + V1They do not play cricket.
− (he/she/it)S + does not + V1He does not play cricket.
? (I/you/we/they)Do + S + V1?Do they play?
? (he/she/it)Does + S + V1?Does he play?

Examples

  • She drinks milk every morning.
  • We go to school by bus.
  • The Earth revolves around the Sun.
  • My father reads the newspaper daily.
  • They do not watch TV on weekdays.
  • Does she speak French?
  • Birds fly south in winter.
  • Water boils at 100°C.

⏰ Present Continuous Tense

The Present Continuous Tense describes actions happening right now or around the current time. Uses the -ing form.

Structure

Subject + is / am / are + V-ing
🛠
I → am  |  He/She/It → is  |  We/You/They → are

When Do We Use It?

UsageExample
Actions happening right nowLook! The baby is crying.
Temporary situationsMy uncle is staying with us this week.
Future plans (arranged)We are going to Delhi tomorrow.
Changing situationsThe weather is getting warmer.
Annoying habits (with "always")He is always losing his keys!

Signal Words

nowright nowat the momentcurrentlytodaythis weeklook!listen!

Positive, Negative & Question

FormIHe/She/ItWe/You/They
+am playingis playingare playing
−am not playingis not playingare not playing
?Am I playing?Is he playing?Are they playing?

Examples

  • I am studying for the exam right now.
  • She is cooking dinner at the moment.
  • They are playing cricket in the park.
  • The sun is shining brightly today.
  • Look! The cat is climbing the tree.
  • Listen! Someone is singing beautifully.
  • I am not watching TV right now.
  • Are you coming to the party tonight?

🌟 Present Perfect Tense

The Present Perfect Tense connects the past to the present. It describes an action completed at some time before now — the result matters, not the exact time.

Structure

Subject + has / have + V3 (past participle)
🔑
Has with He/She/It  |  Have with I/We/You/They

When Do We Use It?

✔ Past action with present result

I have lost my keys. (I still don't have them!)

✔ Life experience

She has visited Paris twice.

✔ Actions that just happened

He has just arrived.

✔ Changes over time

My English has improved.

Signal Words

justalreadyyeteverneversinceforrecentlyso farlately

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + has/have + V3She has written a letter.
−S + has/have + not + V3She hasn't written a letter.
?Has/Have + S + V3?Has she written a letter?

Examples

  • I have finished my homework.
  • She has already eaten lunch.
  • They have not seen this movie yet.
  • Have you ever visited Jaipur?
  • He has lived in Mumbai since 2018.
  • We have known each other for five years.
  • I have never ridden a horse.
  • She has just left the office.

📚 Past Tenses

📚 Simple Past Tense

The Simple Past Tense describes actions that started and finished at a specific time in the past. Uses the past form (V2).

Structure

Subject + V2 (past form)

Regular Verbs: Adding -ed

RuleWhenExamples
Add -edMost verbsplay → played, wash → washed
Add -dEnding in -elike → liked, dance → danced
Double + edCVC patternstop → stopped, plan → planned
-y to -iedConsonant + ycarry → carried, study → studied

Irregular Verbs (25 Common)

speak→spoke
break→broke

lose→lost

When Do We Use It?

✔ Completed actions in the past

She visited her grandmother last Sunday.

✔ A series of completed actions

He woke up, brushed his teeth, and ate breakfast.

✔ Past habits (now stopped)

When I was young, I played in the park every evening.

Signal Words

yesterdaylast weeklast monthlast yearagoin 2020the other dayonce upon a time

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + V2She walked to school.
−S + did not + V1She did not walk to school.
?Did + S + V1?Did she walk to school?
⚠️
Important! In negatives and questions, use did + V1 (base form), NOT V2. "She did not walked" is WRONG. Correct: "She did not walk."

Examples

  • I watched a movie yesterday.
  • They went to Jaipur last summer.
  • She wrote a beautiful poem.
  • We did not play outside because it rained.
  • Did you finish your homework?
  • The dog chased the cat around the garden.
  • My grandmother told us a wonderful story.
  • He bought a new bicycle two days ago.

⏪ Past Continuous Tense

The Past Continuous Tense describes actions that were ongoing at a specific time in the past.

Structure

Subject + was / were + V-ing
🛠
I/He/She/It → was  |  We/You/They → were

When Do We Use It?

UsageExample
Ongoing action at a specific past timeAt 8 PM, I was watching a movie.
Action interrupted by anotherI was sleeping when the phone rang.
Two simultaneous past actionsShe was cooking while he was reading.
Setting background in a storyThe birds were singing and the sun was shining.

Signal Words

whilewhenat that timeall day yesterdayat 5 o'clockas

Past Continuous + Simple Past

PatternExample
When + Simple Past, Past ContinuousWhen the bell rang, students were writing the test.
Past Continuous + when + Simple PastI was crossing the road when I saw an accident.
While + Past Continuous, Simple PastWhile she was shopping, she met her friend.
While + Past Cont., Past Cont.While Mom was cooking, Dad was cleaning.

Examples

  • I was reading a novel at 9 PM last night.
  • The children were playing in the garden all afternoon.
  • She was writing a letter when her friend arrived.
  • We were having dinner when the lights went off.
  • It was raining heavily all day yesterday.
  • They were not listening to the teacher.
  • Were you sleeping when I called?
  • Mom was cooking while Dad was washing the car.

✅ Past Perfect Tense

The Past Perfect Tense describes an action completed BEFORE another past action. Think of it as "the past of the past."

Structure

Subject + had + V3 (past participle)

When Do We Use It?

✔ Earlier action before another past action

She had left before I arrived.

✔ Cause before a past result

He failed because he had not studied.

✔ With "by the time"

By the time we reached, the movie had started.

Signal Words

beforeafteralreadyby the timewhenuntilas soon asnever...before

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + had + V3He had finished dinner.
−S + had not + V3He hadn't finished dinner.
?Had + S + V3?Had he finished dinner?
💡
When two things happened in the past, the EARLIER action = Past Perfect (had + V3), the LATER action = Simple Past (V2).

Examples

  • He had left before I arrived.
  • By the time the teacher came, students had already taken their seats.
  • She had never seen snow before she visited Kashmir.
  • After he had finished homework, he went out to play.
  • The train had departed when we reached the station.
  • I had not eaten anything since morning, so I was very hungry.
  • They had completed the project before the deadline.
  • She told me that she had read the book twice.

🌟 Future Tenses

🌟 Simple Future Tense

The Simple Future Tense describes actions that will happen later. Uses will or shall + V1.

Structure

Subject + will / shall + V1

Will vs Shall

WordUsed WithExample
ShallI, We (formal)I shall go. / We shall meet.
WillAll subjects (modern)He will come. / I will go.

When Do We Use It?

✔ Predictions

It will rain tomorrow.

✔ Promises

I will help you with your project.

✔ Instant decisions

"The phone is ringing." — "I will answer it."

✔ Future facts

The exam will start at 10 AM next Monday.

Signal Words

tomorrownext weeknext monthnext yearsoonin the futurelater

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + will + V1She will visit us tomorrow.
−S + will not + V1She won't visit us tomorrow.
?Will + S + V1?Will she visit us tomorrow?

Examples

  • I will study hard for the exam.
  • We shall go to the park this evening.
  • She will cook dinner tonight.
  • They will not come to the party.
  • Will you help me with this problem?
  • The school will reopen next Monday.
  • It will be sunny tomorrow.
  • Shall we begin the meeting?

🚀 Future Continuous Tense

The Future Continuous Tense describes actions that will be ongoing at a specific future time.

Structure

Subject + will be + V-ing

When Do We Use It?

UsageExample
Ongoing at a specific future timeAt 10 AM tomorrow, I will be taking an exam.
Polite enquiriesWill you be using the car tonight?
Parallel future actionsWhile I will be studying, she will be painting.

Signal Words

at this time tomorrowby thenall day tomorrowthis time next weekat 8 PM tonight
💡
"will be" stays the same for ALL subjects! No need to worry about was/were or is/am/are.

Examples

  • I will be studying at this time tomorrow.
  • She will be travelling to Mumbai next Monday.
  • At 9 PM tonight, we will be watching the match.
  • This time next year, he will be attending college.
  • I will not be working on Sunday.
  • Will you be joining us for lunch tomorrow?
  • Don't call at 3 PM — I will be sleeping.
  • By this time next week, they will be flying to London.

✅ Future Perfect Tense

The Future Perfect Tense describes an action that will be completed BEFORE a specific future time.

Structure

Subject + will have + V3

When Do We Use It?

✔ Completed before a future time

I will have finished by 5 PM.

✔ Completed before another future event

She will have left before you arrive.

Signal Words

by tomorrowby next weekby next yearby the timebeforeby then

Positive, Negative & Question

FormStructureExample
+S + will have + V3She will have completed the work.
−S + will not have + V3She won't have completed the work.
?Will + S + have + V3?Will she have completed the work?

Examples

  • By next Monday, I will have submitted my project.
  • She will have reached Delhi by evening.
  • By the time you wake up, I will have cooked breakfast.
  • They will have built the bridge by December.
  • We will not have finished the exam before 3 PM.
  • Will you have learnt all chapters by Friday?
  • By next year, he will have worked here for ten years.
  • By the time guests arrive, Mom will have prepared food.

📝 -ing Spelling Rules

Used for all Continuous Tenses. Get these right and never lose marks!

RuleBase Verb-ing Form
Just add -ingplay, read, eat, talkplaying, reading, eating, talking
Drop silent -ewrite, make, dance, comewriting, making, dancing, coming
Double consonant (CVC)run, sit, swim, stop, getrunning, sitting, swimming, stopping, getting
-ie → -yingdie, lie, tiedying, lying, tying
-ee: just add -ingsee, agree, freeseeing, agreeing, freeing
Double -l (British)travel, canceltravelling, cancelling
⚠️
Do NOT double -w, -x, or -y: snow → snowing, fix → fixing, play → playing

⚖️ Since vs For

Both used with Present Perfect for duration, but NOT interchangeable.

🔼 SINCE

= from a specific POINT in time

  • since Monday
  • since 2019
  • since January
  • since morning
  • since last summer
  • since I was a child

⏲️ FOR

= for a PERIOD / DURATION

  • for three days
  • for five years
  • for two hours
  • for a long time
  • for six months
  • for ages
🌟
SINCE = Specific Starting point. FOR = Flat length of time.

I have lived here since five years.

I have lived here for five years. ("Five years" is duration)

Write which of the 9 tenses each sentence uses.

  1. She drinks a glass of milk every morning.
  2. We visited the museum last Saturday.

✅ Exercise A — Solutions
  1. Simple Present — "drinks" (V1+s), signal: "every morning"
  2. Simple Past — "visited" (V2), signal: "last Saturday"

  4. Present Continuous — "is crying" (is + V-ing), signal: "Look!"
  5. Past Continuous — "was reading" (was + V-ing), signal: "at 9 PM last night"
  6. Present Perfect — "has eaten" (has + V3), signal: "already"
  7. Past Perfect — "had left" (had + V3), signal: "before"
  8. Future Continuous — "will be taking" (will be + V-ing), signal: "at 10 AM tomorrow"
  9. Future Perfect — "will have submitted" (will have + V3), signal: "by next Monday"
  10. Simple Present — "sets" (V1+s), universal truth
  11. Past Continuous — "were playing" (were + V-ing), signal: "when"
  12. Present Perfect — "have visited" (have + V3), signal: "ever"
