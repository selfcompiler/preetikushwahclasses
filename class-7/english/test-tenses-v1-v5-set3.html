Answer Key & Detailed Solutions
Q1. [1 Mark]
(b) Subject + will + have + V3
Future Perfect Tense formula: Subject + will + have + V3 (past participle).
Example: She will have written (V3) the essay by tomorrow.
Option (a) is Simple Future (will + V1). Option (c) is Future Continuous (will + be + V4). Option (d) is Future Perfect Continuous (will + have + been + V4).
Q2. [1 Mark]
(c) V3
“Written” is the past participle form (V3) of “write.” V-forms of write: write (V1) → wrote (V2) → written (V3) → writing (V4) → writes (V5). In passive voice, the main verb always appears as V3 regardless of the tense.
Q3. [1 Mark]
(a) Because “does” is already in present tense, so the main verb stays as V1.
When a helping verb (does/did) is used, it carries the tense information. The main verb must remain in its base form (V1). Formula: Subject + does not + V1. “Does” already shows third-person present, so “sing” (V1) is correct, not “sings” (V5).
Q4. [1 Mark]
(b) Present Perfect & Past Perfect
Present Perfect: Subject + has/have + V3 (e.g., She has gone).
Past Perfect: Subject + had + V3 (e.g., She had gone).
All three Perfect tenses (Present, Past, Future) use V3. Simple Past uses V2. Continuous tenses use V4. Simple Present uses V1/V5.
Q5. [1 Mark]
(b) V1 — because “did” already carries the past tense
In past tense interrogatives, “did” is the past-tense helper. The main verb reverts to V1 (base form). Formula: Did + Subject + V1? “Finish” is V1 (base form), not V2 (“finished”). The same rule applies to negatives: “They did not finish (V1).”
Q6. [2 Marks]
Corrected passage:
“Yesterday, Rina went (V2) to the market. She bought (V2) fresh vegetables and cooked (V2) dinner. Her family ate (V2) the meal happily.”

Errors explained:
• “has went” → “went” (V2): “Yesterday” signals Simple Past, not Present Perfect. Also, “went” is V2, not used with “has.” (½ mark)
• “buyed” → “bought” (V2): “Buy” is irregular: buy → bought → bought. There is no “buyed.” (½ mark)
• “has cooked” → “cooked” (V2): Past context requires Simple Past, not Present Perfect. (½ mark)
• “eated” → “ate” (V2): “Eat” is irregular: eat → ate → eaten. There is no “eated.” (½ mark)
Q7. [2 Marks]
(a) Active: The children are playing (V4) cricket in the park.
Passive: Cricket is being played (V3) in the park by the children. (1 mark)
V-form change: V4 (playing) → V3 (played). In passive voice, the main verb always converts to V3. The tense is shown by the helping verbs (is being = Present Continuous Passive).

(b) Active: She has written (V3) three letters today.
Passive: Three letters have been written (V3) by her today. (1 mark)
V-form change: V3 remains V3 (written → written). In Present Perfect Passive, the formula is: Object + have/has + been + V3. “Has” changes to “have” because the new subject “Three letters” is plural.
Q8. [2 Marks]
(i) She writes. (V5) (¼ mark)
(ii) Subject + is/am/are + V4 (¼ mark)
(iii) She has written. (V3) (¼ mark)
(iv) Subject + V2 (¼ mark)
(v) She was writing. (V4) (¼ mark)
(vi) Subject + had + V3 (¼ mark)
(vii) She will write. (V1) (¼ mark)
(viii) Subject + will + be + V4 (¼ mark)
(ix) She will have written. (V3)

Key pattern: Simple tenses use V1/V2/V5. Continuous tenses always use V4. Perfect tenses always use V3.
Q9. [2 Marks]
Past Perfect version:
“The teacher had explained (V3) the lesson. The students had understood (V3) the concept. They had solved (V3) all the problems correctly.” (1 mark for correct conversion)

V-form identification: Past Perfect uses had + V3 (past participle). (1 mark)
• explains (V5) → had explained (V3)
• understand (V1) → had understood (V3) [irregular: understand → understood → understood]
• solve (V1) → had solved (V3)
Q10. [2 Marks]
(a) Present Perfect Continuous: She has been dancing (V4) since morning.
Negative: She has not been dancing (V4) since morning. — V4 remains unchanged; “not” is placed after “has.” (½ mark)
Interrogative: Has she been dancing (V4) since morning? — “Has” moves before the subject; V4 stays. (½ mark)

(b) Future Perfect: They will have completed (V3) the task by evening.
Negative: They will not have completed (V3) the task by evening. — “Not” comes after “will”; V3 is unchanged. (½ mark)
Interrogative: Will they have completed (V3) the task by evening? — “Will” moves before subject; V3 stays. (½ mark)
Q11. [2 Marks]
Verb analysis: (⅓ mark each, approx.)
• wakes — Simple Present, V5 (wakes = wake + s, third person singular habitual action) (⅔ mark)
• was preparing — Past Continuous, V4 (preparing = -ing form; formula: was + V4, ongoing action interrupted in the past) (⅔ mark)
• went — Simple Past, V2 (went = past form of “go”; sudden completed action that interrupted the ongoing action) (⅔ mark)
• had already studied — Past Perfect, V3 (studied = past participle; formula: had + V3; action completed before another past action) (⅔ mark)
• will appear — Simple Future, V1 (appear = base form; formula: will + V1; planned future action) (⅔ mark)
Q12. [2 Marks]
Simple Past uses V2 because the verb itself directly shows that the action is over — V2 is a standalone past marker. No helping verb is needed. Example: “I went (V2) to school yesterday.” The action is finished and has no connection to the present. (1 mark)

Present Perfect uses V3 because it needs the helping verb “has/have” to connect a past action to the present. V3 (past participle) cannot stand alone — it must pair with a helper. Example: “I have gone (V3) to that school before.” The experience still matters now. (1 mark)

Fundamental difference: V2 = self-sufficient past marker (action done, disconnected from now). V3 = dependent form requiring a helper (has/had/will have), showing the result or relevance of a completed action.

Q14. [2 Marks]
Text A (Narrative/Story): Dominated by Simple Past (V2) and Past Perfect (V3). (1 mark)
• “saw” (V2), “jumped” (V2), “could not reach” (V1 with modal), “had tried” (V3), “gave up” (V2).
• Reason: Stories narrate events that already happened, so past tenses dominate. V2 moves the plot forward; V3 (Past Perfect) shows earlier background events.

Text B (Descriptive/Scientific): Dominated by Simple Present (V1/V5) and Present Perfect (V3). (1 mark)
• “boils” (V5), “evaporates” (V5), “forms” (V5), “has continued” (V3).
• Reason: Scientific facts and universal truths use Simple Present (V5 for third person). Present Perfect (V3) shows a process that started in the past and continues now.
Q15. [2 Marks]
Flowchart-style Decision Guide: (1 mark for correct flow; 1 mark for V-form mapping)

Step 1: When does the action happen?
→ PAST → Go to Step 2A
→ PRESENT → Go to Step 2B
→ FUTURE → Go to Step 2C

Step 2A (Past):
• Completed action? → Simple Past → Use V2 (e.g., went)
• Was ongoing at a point? → Past Continuous → Use was/were + V4 (e.g., was going)
• Completed before another past action? → Past Perfect → Use had + V3 (e.g., had gone)

Step 2B (Present):
• Habit / universal truth? → Simple Present → Use V1 (I/we/they) or V5 (he/she/it) (e.g., goes)
• Happening right now? → Present Continuous → Use is/am/are + V4 (e.g., is going)
• Just completed with present result? → Present Perfect → Use has/have + V3 (e.g., has gone)

Step 2C (Future):
• Will happen? → Simple Future → Use will + V1 (e.g., will go)
• Will be ongoing at a future point? → Future Continuous → Use will + be + V4 (e.g., will be going)
• Will be completed before a future point? → Future Perfect → Use will + have + V3 (e.g., will have gone)
