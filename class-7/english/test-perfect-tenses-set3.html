Answer Key & Detailed Solutions
Q1. [1 Mark]
(a) steal → stolen (½ mark) (b) speak → spoken (½ mark)
Q2. [1 Mark]
will have launched — Future perfect tense. “By 2030” indicates completion before a future time.
Q3. [1 Mark]
(a) has/cleaned — Present perfect: has + past participle. “Just” indicates a recently completed action.
Q4. [1 Mark]
False — “Yesterday” is a specific past time. Present perfect cannot be used with definite past time markers. Correct: “I saw him yesterday.”
Q5. [1 Mark]
since — “Monday” is a point in time, so we use “since”. (“For” is used with durations.)
Q6. [1 Mark]
Past Perfect Tense — When two past events occur, the earlier one uses past perfect (had + V3).
Q7. [2 Marks]
Mother had decorated the house before the guests arrived.
The decorating happened first (past perfect), the arrival happened later (simple past). (1 mark for correct order, 1 mark for correct tenses)
Q8. [2 Marks]
(a) He has gone to the market. — V3 of “go” is “gone”, not “went”. (1 mark)
(b) By tomorrow, they will have completed their work. — Future perfect needs “will have + V3”. (1 mark)
Q9. [2 Marks]
(a) We have not received any reply yet. — Present perfect (“yet” is the signal). (1 mark)
(b) After she had finished cooking, she sat down to rest. — Past perfect (earlier of two past actions). (1 mark)
Q10. [2 Marks]
(a) “I lost my keys” — Simple past. States a fact about the past. The keys may or may not still be lost. (1 mark)
(b) “I have lost my keys” — Present perfect. The keys are still lost now. The past action has a present result/relevance. (1 mark)
Q11. [2 Marks]
Interrogative: Had Rani memorised all the poems before the competition? (1 mark)
Negative: Rani had not memorised all the poems before the competition. (1 mark)
Q12. [3 Marks]
has loved — Present Perfect Tense (1 mark)
had read — Past Perfect Tense (1 mark)
will have read — Future Perfect Tense (1 mark)
Q13. [3 Marks]
Sample dialogue:
Anita: Hi Meera! What have you done this summer? (Present Perfect)
Meera: I have learnt swimming at the new sports club. (Present Perfect)
Anita: That’s great! Before that, had you tried any other sport? (Past Perfect)
Meera: Yes, I had joined a painting class last year. What about you? (Past Perfect)
Anita: I have visited my grandparents in Indore. We had planned this trip for months! (Present Perfect + Past Perfect)
(1 mark per tense correctly used, max 3)
Q14. [3 Marks]
“Since” is used with a point in time (specific moment when the action started). (1 mark)
Examples: I have lived here since 2020. / She has known him since childhood.

“For” is used with a duration/period of time. (1 mark)
Examples: We have waited for three hours. / He has worked here for five years.
(1 mark for all four correct examples)
Q15. [3 Marks]
(a) The farmer:
Present Perfect: The farmer has ploughed the field.
Past Perfect: The farmer had ploughed the field.
Future Perfect: The farmer will have ploughed the field. (1.5 marks)

(b) Neha:
Present Perfect: Neha has sung a song.
Past Perfect: Neha had sung a song.
Future Perfect: Neha will have sung a song. (1.5 marks)
Q16. [5 Marks]
(a) had studied — Past Perfect; has scored — Present Perfect; have promised — Present Perfect; had discussed — Past Perfect; will have visited — Future Perfect. (2 marks)
(b) “Had studied” is in past perfect because studying happened before scoring — it is the earlier of two past events. (1 mark)
(c) By next month, the family will not have visited both places. (1 mark)
(d) Has Priya scored 95% this time? OR Have her parents promised her a trip to Kerala? (1 mark)
Q17. [5 Marks]
Table: (2 marks — ½ each row)
begin → began → begun
catch → caught → caught
drive → drove → driven
freeze → froze → frozen
shake → shook → shaken

Sentences: (1 mark each)
Present Perfect: The match has begun on time.
Past Perfect: The police had caught the thief before dawn.
Future Perfect: By evening, the lake will have frozen completely.
Q18. Bonus [2 Marks]
“I have seen him yesterday” is wrong because present perfect tense cannot be used with specific/definite past time expressions like “yesterday”, “last week”, “in 2020”, etc. (1 mark)

Present perfect connects the past to the present without specifying exactly when. When we mention a specific past time, we must use simple past: “I saw him yesterday.” (1 mark)

However, “I have seen him recently” is correct because “recently” is an indefinite time word.
